The Pages Every Small Business Website Needs (and the Ones You Can Skip)

What pages should a business website have? A minimum set of four, what each must contain, the extras worth adding and the pages to skip, for India.

What pages should a business website have? Four: a homepage, a services page, an about page and a contact page, plus proof that you are real (reviews, photos or credentials) placed on those pages instead of on a page of their own. Everything else is optional until a customer's question demands it. The honest qualification is that all four sources I read are practitioner and vendor guides with no tested results, so the minimum set below is my reading of where they agree.

  • Minimum. Home, services, about and contact, with a privacy notice and a custom 404 page as the quiet fifth and sixth.
  • Count. Sources give no single number: one says fewer than 10 pages suits a local business, another says many choose eight or more.
  • Add later. FAQ, reviews, pricing and a blog, each when a real customer question or search calls for it.
  • Skip. Press kit, HTML sitemap, feature pages and an accessibility statement, unless your trade or buyer asks.

What Pages Should a Business Website Have? The Minimum Set

A business website needs four pages at launch because each answers one question a buyer asks before getting in touch. The table below is my synthesis of the homepage, about, services and contact pages that all four sources name (read 17 November 2026).

PageThe question it answersMust contain
HomepageWhat do you do, and is it for me?One-line promise, the main action, links to the other pages
ServicesWhat exactly can I buy, and for whom?Each service in plain words, a price or "starting at" note
AboutWho is behind this, and can I trust them?Names, faces, credentials, where you are
ContactHow do I reach you right now?Form, phone, WhatsApp, address, hours, map

A website is the whole set of pages under one domain, and a web page is one document at one address within it. That difference matters because a single page can be a complete site: a landing page is a one-page site built for one action. If that is your situation, the landing page or full website comparison helps you choose.

How Many Pages Should a Small Business Website Have?

The number of pages on a website has no single right answer, and the sources agree only that a small local business needs fewer than a large one. MailerLite says a small local business with one or two services is often fine with fewer than 10 pages, while an online store may need hundreds. 1SEO says there is no right or wrong count but that many small businesses end up with eight or more standard pages.

My reading: five to eight pages at launch suits a one-location service business, and that is a judgement, not a measured result.

The Homepage: What Should Be on It

A homepage should state what you do and who it is for in its first screen, give one clear action, and link to your other pages. MailerLite lists a headline and subheading, an explanation of the offer, links to key pages, a call to action and social proof. Uni Square adds simple navigation, and 1SEO wants your name, address and phone number (NAP) and the action visible without scrolling.

For an Indian local business, the action is usually a WhatsApp tap or a call. The studio's own web development page says most visitors arrive on a phone, often on patchy 4G, and that most small-business sites fail by loading slowly, burying the one action and being impossible for the owner to update. A homepage that loads slowly fails before anything on it is read.

Use this order, which is my arrangement of the sources' lists:

  1. Promise and action (WhatsApp, call, book).
  2. Your two or three main services, each linked to its page.
  3. One piece of proof: a review, a credential or a photo of your premises.
  4. Address, hours and map.

Services and About: What Each Page Must Say

Give each service its own page when customers search for it by name, and keep one overview page when you have only a few. MailerLite suggests a main services page that links to specific ones if you offer many. The studio's SEO page says one page per service lets each rank for a specific buying search, and its clinic page recommends one page per treatment for the same reason.

On every services page, say what is included, who it suits and what happens next. Uni Square advises benefit-led wording and either a price, a "starting at" figure or a request-a-quote route, because openness builds trust. My reading: a page with no price pushes that question onto your phone.

The about page earns its place by being specific. Uni Square lists origin story, values, the team and milestones; MailerLite says it can help your pages rank by showing expertise. Uni Square adds that real team photos work best. For a small team, 1SEO says a separate team page and a team section on the about page both work, so choose the shorter route.

The Contact Page: Form, WhatsApp, Phone and Map

A contact page should offer at least three ways in: a short form, a tap-to-call number and a WhatsApp link, with your address, hours and a map. The sources split on one point. MailerLite and Somerfleck advise a form over a published email address because published addresses get scraped for spam, while Uni Square lists a visible email beside the form.

My reading: use a form, a call button and WhatsApp, and publish an email only if it is on your own domain. Somerfleck makes the same point about free-mail addresses looking less professional. Ask only for what you need: MailerLite says to drop the phone-number field if you will not use it.

The studio's pages describe WhatsApp click-to-chat with a pre-filled message, a call button and a Google Maps embed wired up and tracked, so you can see which route brings the most enquiries. The guide to WhatsApp booking options compares the ways to add it. Your Google Business Profile should show the same address and phone number: the studio notes that Maps rankings depend mainly on that profile, and the website supports it with consistent details.

Basic Pages of a Website Beyond the Core Four: Add or Skip

Add an extra page only when a customer question or a search needs it, and skip the rest until then. The table gives my verdict on each page the sources mention, with the source's reasoning.

PageVerdictReason
FAQAdd when the same questions repeatMailerLite: it cuts enquiries you must answer one by one
ReviewsAdd on the homepage firstOwn page only once you hold many real reviews
PricingAdd if customers ask "how much"Uni Square: openness builds trust; ranges are enough
BlogAdd later, with a planMailerLite calls it not mandatory; Uni Square and Somerfleck recommend it
TeamFold into About1SEO: either works for a small team
Custom 404Add; it is a small jobMailerLite: guides lost visitors to your key pages
Press kit, HTML sitemap, feature pagesSkipMeant for media, big sites or software firms
Accessibility statementSkip as a pageMailerLite says EU and US public bodies must have one; I found nothing that requires it of an Indian small business
Shipping and returnsSkip unless you sell goodsA shop needs them; a clinic does not

The sources disagree on the blog: MailerLite says it is optional, while Uni Square says it helps search rankings. Neither gives test data. A blog earns its place only if someone will keep it going with answers to real customer questions.

Legal and Trade Details: What to Publish in India

Publish a privacy notice if your forms collect personal details, and display any registration your trade is expected to show. MailerLite lists a privacy policy covering cookies, emails and advertising, and says terms and conditions suit businesses that sell. It also advises against drafting legal documents with AI tools unless you have legal expertise.

The research supplied no primary Indian law, so I cannot state a legal requirement here. The studio's industry pages say food businesses are expected to show an FSSAI licence number and real estate projects a RERA number.

They also say councils restrict how clinics advertise, ICAI and Bar Council rules limit CA firms and lawyers, and a CA firm's contact form needs a privacy notice aligned to India's DPDP Act. Confirm your own obligations with a qualified professional and your regulator, as rules change.

A privacy line goes beside the contact form, and a custom 404 page is added. In my plan, a reviews page and blog posts wait until there are enough real reviews and someone to write them.

Most page plans fail from copying a big site's list or from hiding the action, not from missing a page. The causes below come from the sources and from the studio's own pages.

What to Do Next

List the questions customers ask you on calls and on WhatsApp, then turn each into a page or a section. Use this checklist, and then compare the result with the pricing page.

  • [ ] I named the one action I want visitors to take.
  • [ ] I have a homepage, services, about and contact page planned.
  • [ ] Each service customers search for has its own page.
  • [ ] My contact page has a form, a call button, WhatsApp and a map.
  • [ ] I decided what waits until version two, in writing.
  • [ ] I know which registration numbers my trade expects me to show.
  • [ ] A privacy line sits beside every form.

Frequently asked questions

How many pages should a website have?

A small local business can usually launch with five to eight pages. MailerLite says fewer than 10 suits a business with one or two services, and 1SEO says many choose eight or more. The right count is the pages your customers' questions need.

What should a business website include?

A business website should include a homepage, services, about and contact pages, with a clear action, proof of trust and your address. Add a privacy notice if forms collect details. Optional extras are an FAQ, reviews, pricing and a blog.

What should be on a website homepage?

A homepage should show what you do, who it is for, one clear action and links to your main pages. MailerLite also lists social proof. Put your phone number and address within easy reach.

What legal pages should a website have?

A website that collects personal details should have a privacy notice, and a shop should add terms and returns. MailerLite lists these pages. I had no primary Indian source, so confirm the wording and any trade registration with a professional.

Is a Facebook page enough instead of a website?

A social page can show your details, but you do not control its page set, layout or tracking. This is my reading, not a sourced finding. A website gives you your own pages for each service and your own contact routes.

What is the difference between a website and a web page?

A web page is one document at one address, and a website is the connected set of pages under one domain. A landing page is one page that can act as a small website for a single action.

Why does a business need a website?

A website is where customers check you before they call. 1SEO claims up to 80% of consumers research a business online before visiting or buying, but it gives no source for that figure, so treat it as its claim.

Do I need a blog on my business website?

No, a blog is optional at launch. MailerLite calls it not mandatory, while Uni Square says it helps rankings, and neither shows test data. Add one when someone can publish answers to real customer questions on a steady schedule.
