Writing Website Copy That Sells When You're Not a Writer

Website copywriting tips for owners who are not writers: headline formulas, a seven-block service page, proof you can show and CTA wording, with Indian examples.

The website copywriting tips that matter most for a non-writer are to give each page one goal and one reader, say what you do in plain words in the headline, show proof a visitor can check, and end every section with one clear next step. None of that needs flair. The honest qualification is that this page rests on six practitioner and vendor guides, not on a controlled test, so the formulas below are templates to try on your own enquiries, not proven results.

  • Goal and reader. Decide the one action and the one person before you write a line.
  • Headlines. Four fill-in formulas, with fictional Indian examples you can adapt.
  • Service page. Seven blocks in a fixed order, each answering one visitor question.
  • Proof and buttons. What to show, what to leave out, and button wording that names the next step.

What Is Website Copywriting, and How Is It Not Blog Writing?

Website copywriting is writing the words on your pages so a visitor takes one specific action, which is a different job from writing articles that inform. Copyhackers puts it as words that drive action, and says content writing aims to inform. Mailchimp adds that copywriting pushes for an immediate action while content writing builds authority over time.

For a small business the copy is the home, service, about and contact pages, plus button labels and form text. Shopify's guide also counts photo captions, footers and menu tabs, which owners tend to skip.

Website Copywriting Tips for Beginners: One Goal, One Reader

Start with one goal and one reader, because every later choice, from headline to button, depends on them. Copyhackers tells writers to fix a single goal for the page before the first word and to write for one specific reader. Jacob McMillen, a copywriter, asks for one primary objective for the whole site, such as a contact form for a service business.

Answer three questions in one sentence each:

  1. What should the visitor do? Example: request a free demo class on WhatsApp.
  2. Who is reading? Example: a working professional in Mysuru who needs an IELTS score.
  3. What do they fear or doubt? Example: whether evening batches fit a 9-to-6 job.

The example is Lakshya IELTS Academy, a made-up coaching centre, the same one used in the website brief template. McMillen has a rule for homepages: if one service brings in 70% or more of your revenue, make the homepage a landing page for it. Check your own invoices before you decide. If you would rather talk the goal through, the web development service starts with a discovery call.

Gather Your Customers' Own Words Before You Draft

Collect the words customers already use before you write, because research is where a non-writer gains the most. Copyhackers calls this voice-of-customer data and says it is the largest part of a copywriter's job.

My reading for an Indian small business is that your richest record is your WhatsApp and call log. Try this, which is my suggestion and not a sourced method:

  1. Copy ten recent enquiries into one document and delete the names and numbers.
  2. Underline each question the person asked before booking.
  3. Tally the three most common. Those become your headings, your FAQ and the doubts the page must answer.

McMillen says to raise the objections your leads already have, and to leave out the ones they do not.

Four Headline Formulas to Adapt

A headline works when a stranger can say what you do, for whom and where within a few seconds. McMillen cites research that you have three to fifteen seconds, depending on the study, to hold attention. Shopify says a headline should answer "so what?" for the reader.

Kayla Hollatz, a website copywriter, advises a headline that names what you offer and who it is for, adds what makes you different, and stays short. The table gives four patterns. Every example is fictional and is a template of mine, not a tested formula.

FormulaPatternFictional exampleUse when
Plain statementWhat you do, for whom, whereIELTS coaching for working professionals in MysuruPeople search by service and town
Remove the dreadThe result, without what they dreadIELTS preparation that fits around a full-time jobThe visitor's doubt is time or effort
Answer the questionThe question they ask youWhich IELTS batch suits a 9-to-6 job?The page compares options
Documented numberA real figure, then what it meansTwelve students per batch, so more speaking practiceYou hold a record of the figure

The second pattern follows Copyhackers' example of a gifting brand that promised no post office queues and no kitsch, two things its buyers dread. Hollatz writes 10 on the low end and 20 to 30 on the high end when she suggests headlines to clients, because early drafts are rarely the best. Write ten, then pick by reading only the headings aloud, which WordStream recommends.

Service-Page Structure: Seven Blocks in Order

A service page works as seven blocks in a fixed order, each answering the question a visitor has at that point. I assembled this order from Hollatz, McMillen and Copyhackers, so treat it as a template, not a tested layout.

BlockVisitor's questionWhat to write
1. Headline and actionIs this for me?A formula headline and one button
2. BenefitsWhat do I get?The result first, the feature second
3. What is includedWhat exactly is in it?A bulleted list of deliverables
4. How it worksWhat happens next?Three to five steps, with timing
5. ProofCan I trust you?Reviews, cases, documented numbers
6. Doubts and FAQWhat if...?The tallied questions, answered plainly
7. Final actionHow do I start?The same button, plus a phone number

Hollatz says to lead with benefits and to list every deliverable in a scannable bulleted list. She also advises a short re-introduction between the intro and the packages, because some visitors land on the service page first.

For Lakshya, block 4 would read: demo class, level test, batch allocation, weekly mock test. Block 7 would repeat the WhatsApp button, so nobody has to scroll back up. The landing page article goes deeper on the hero section when the page is a single ad destination.

Proof That Holds Up: What To Show and What To Leave Out

Proof is anything a visitor can check, and copy built from proof beats copy that only claims. McMillen calls proof the real magic of website copywriting and says to write your lines directly from it. Mailchimp lists testimonials and case studies, and warns that exaggerated claims cost credibility. WordStream suggests numbers such as locations, customers, reviews or projects.

The table sorts proof by what makes it believable and the caution that applies in India. The cautions come from the studio's own industry pages, so confirm current rules with your regulator or a professional.

ProofBelievable whenCaution
Google reviewsCount and rating match the live profileNever write reviews yourself
Named testimonialName, role and a specific detailGet written permission
Case studyIt states time, scope and what was builtClaim only what you can show
Registration numbersPrinted in fullFSSAI for food, RERA for projects
Results or rankYou hold the documentsCoaching ad guidelines target misleading claims
Before and afterThe person consentedClinic advertising rules apply

For a worked example of "specific", the Ashirwad Dental Clinic case study on this site records 8 weeks from kickoff to launch and 1 tap to start a WhatsApp booking. The clinic's co-founder is quoted saying the work shipped in eight weeks what two earlier agencies had not delivered in a year. That sentence persuades because it contains a time, not an adjective.

CTA Copy: What the Button Says and Where It Goes

A good call to action names the exact next step in a few words and appears wherever a visitor might be ready. WordStream says common CTA phrases have become wallpaper and recommends descriptive, specific wording. Shopify says a page without a clear end point loses visitors who would otherwise have acted.

The table lists weak wording and a rewrite. The rewrites are my suggestions for the fictional academy.

WeakBetterWhy it works
SubmitRequest a free demo classNames the thing you get
Learn moreSee batch timings and feesPromises the answer they want
Contact usMessage us on WhatsAppNames the channel they already use
Click hereCheck your IELTS levelNames the outcome

The studio's web development page describes WhatsApp click-to-chat that opens a pre-filled message. Write that message too, for example: "Hi, I would like a free demo class for the evening batch." McMillen offers an arbitrary rule of thumb that nobody should scroll past two screens of text without a chance to act. Hollatz says the homepage is one of the few pages where several calls to action make sense, so keep other pages to one main action.

How Much To Write: Length, Scanning and Phone Screens

Write less than feels natural, because visitors read only a fraction of any page. WordStream cites an unnamed academic study in which users read about 20% of text on pages of 400 to 1,200 words, and up to 50% on pages of 200 words or fewer. WordStream also reports an average time on page of 53 seconds, so treat both figures as secondary.

The arithmetic shows what that means. At 20%, a 400-word page gets about 80 words read and a 1,200-word page about 240. At 50%, a 200-word page gets up to 100.

My reading: a long page is not wrong, but about four-fifths of it goes unread, so the headline, the first screen and the button carry the page. Spend your best words there. Mailchimp advises short sentences and paragraphs for phone readers, and WordStream flags a three-line heading on a phone as worth an edit. WordStream reports that 67% of web traffic was on mobile in March 2024, and the studio's web development page says most visitors arrive on a phone, often on patchy 4G.

Edit and Check Your Copy Without Any Tools

You can edit website copy well with three passes: read it aloud, read only the headings, and give it to someone outside your business. McMillen says to read copy aloud and to wait a day, because beginners tend to sound stiff and full of jargon. WordStream cites a Tidio study in which 97% of respondents said grammar errors hurt their view of a brand.

Copy this checklist before you publish:

  • [ ] The headline says what I do, for whom and where.
  • [ ] The headings alone tell the story of the page.
  • [ ] Every claim has a document, review or number behind it.
  • [ ] One main button, with a verb and a result.
  • [ ] A phone number or WhatsApp button in the last block.
  • [ ] A stranger read the first screen and told me what I offer.

WordStream cites a Chartbeat study in which sites that regularly tested headlines drew over five times the engagement. That figure is second-hand and from a vendor, and a small site rarely has enough traffic for formal tests. My reading is to change one element, then compare enquiries over the same number of weeks. The landing page article's section on testing covers this in more detail.

Write It Yourself, Use AI or Hire a Writer?

Write the first draft yourself and bring in help for the edit, unless the page decides a large sale or your drafts keep stalling. Shopify says a skilled copywriter can be expensive but may lift visitors and conversions, and suggests a small trial project before handing over the whole site. WordStream says AI is useful for headline ideas but its output needs an edit and should not be the final copy.

My research holds no rate card for copywriters in India, so I cannot quote one. Shopify says the work can take from a few hours to a few weeks depending on scope, and is quicker for a rewrite than for a new site.

Choose thisWhen
Write it yourselfOne page, a clear goal, and customers you speak to daily
Write, then pay for an editYou have a draft but doubt the tone or the headings
Use AI for ideas onlyYou are stuck on headline variations; remove customer names first
Hire a writerSeveral pages, a launch date, or a high-value service

What Usually Goes Wrong With Self-Written Copy

Most self-written copy fails because it talks about the owner, not the visitor. McMillen names that as the main mistake of non-copywriters. The table lists the common failures with a cause and a fix.

What goes wrongCauseFix
The page is about "us"Writing from the owner's viewRewrite each line as a visitor benefit
Clever headline, unclear offerCopyhackers: creativity can hurt clarityUse the plain statement formula
Claims nobody can checkNo proof gathered firstRemove them or find the document
Five buttons, five goalsNo single page goalOne main action per page
No contact on the service pageAssumed visitors start at homeRepeat the button and number
Copy written after the designWords squeezed into a layoutWrite headings first, then design

On the last row, Copyhackers describes writing and wireframing the copy together, so the layout fits the words and not the reverse.

What To Do Next

Pick the one page that matters most and work through five steps this week. The order is my suggestion, and the time each takes depends on you.

  1. Choose the page. Your main service page or homepage.
  2. Collect ten enquiries and tally the three top questions.
  3. Write ten headlines from the four formulas, then pick one by reading headings aloud.
  4. Fill the seven blocks, using only proof you can document.
  5. Run the checklist, publish, and count enquiries by source for a few weeks.

Frequently asked questions

What is website copywriting?

Website copywriting is writing the words on a site so visitors take a specific action, such as booking, calling or buying. Copyhackers separates it from content writing, which aims to inform. It covers pages, buttons, forms and captions.

How is writing for websites different from other copywriting?

Website copy has to be scanned quickly and work with buttons, menus and phone screens. WordStream says visitors have limited attention but a site must still hold a lot of information. Mailchimp adds that web copy can be updated at any time.

Should I hire a copywriter for my website?

Hire one when the site has many pages, a fixed launch date or a high-value service. Shopify says a writer can be expensive but can improve conversions, and suggests a small trial project first. For one page, a self-written draft with a paid edit is often enough.

How many hours does it take to write a website's copy?

It takes anywhere from a few hours to a few weeks, depending on how many pages you write. Shopify says a rewrite is generally quicker than writing for a new site. McMillen adds that website copy takes longer word for word than a blog post.

How long should website copy be?

Website copy should be as short as the decision allows. WordStream cites a study where users read about 20% of text on pages of 400 to 1,200 words and up to 50% on pages of 200 or fewer. Put the main point and the button on the first screen.

What are easy quick copywriting tips?

Give the page one goal, write for one reader, put benefits before features, and make the button name the next step. Add one piece of proof you can document and read the page aloud before publishing. These are drawn from the Copyhackers, McMillen and Hollatz guides above.

Can I use AI to write my website copy?

You can use AI for ideas, but not as the final copy. WordStream recommends it for headline variations and says its output needs an edit. My suggestion is to remove customer names and numbers before pasting anything into a tool.

Do I need Hindi or Kannada copy as well?

Write one language well first, then add others if your visitors need them. The studio's cost calculator lists multiple languages as a separate option, so a second language has its own cost. Translate the finished page; do not run two drafts at once.
