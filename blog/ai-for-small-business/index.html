25 Ways Indian Small Businesses Can Use AI Today, With Free Tools

AI for small business in India: 25 uses grouped by function, the free tools five public sources name, their limits and a 30-day plan to test one.

AI for small business works best as a set of small helpers, not one big system: a chatbot for repeat questions, a drafting tool for emails and captions, a note-taker for calls and an automation that moves form entries into a sheet. This page sets out 25 such uses across five business functions, with the tools and free tiers that five public pages name for each. The honest qualification is that I did not test any tool, most of the figures come from companies that sell AI, and the sources say little about Hindi or Kannada, so trial one use on your own messages before you rely on it.

  • Start narrow. Pick one repeat task, use a free tier and review every output for a month.
  • Free has limits. Most writing, design, notes and automation tools have a free tier, and paid plans start when a team or volume grows.
  • Customers need most care. Chatbots and call assistants speak for your business, so they need approved answers and a handover to a person.
  • Distrust the big numbers. Most savings figures are vendor case results, and the limits section shows who published each.

What AI for Small Business Means, and How This List Was Built

AI for small business means ordinary software that drafts, sorts, summarises, answers or predicts so a small team spends less time on repeat work. The British Business Bank says smaller firms most often meet chatbots, data analysis tools and content services. Salesforce adds that AI now arrives inside platforms owners already use, so many uses need no code.

I built the list from five pages read on 20 November 2026: Salesforce India, Google Workspace India, Dialpad, MindStudio and the British Business Bank. A use is on the list only if a page describes it, and "not stated" in the tables means the page gives no free option, not that none exists.

Three limits apply. Four of the five pages come from companies selling AI products, and Salesforce's own page discloses a financial interest. I ran no tool myself, and none of the pages says how well a tool copes with Hindi, Kannada or Indian phone lines.

A Worked Example: Is One Task Worth an Hour a Week?

A task is worth automating when the hours it saves in a month clearly exceed the time spent checking its output. The inputs below are assumed to show the method. They are not measured and not a client result, so replace them with your own log.

  • By hand today: 30 routine emails a week at 8 minutes each is 240 minutes.
  • With a reviewed draft: 30 emails at 4 minutes each is 120 minutes.
  • Weekly saving: 240 minus 120 is 120 minutes, or 2 hours.
  • Setup: assume 3 hours once, so 3 divided by 2 is 1.5 weeks to break even.

If your own log shows less than half an hour saved a week, drop the task and try another. To judge a paid plan, multiply the monthly hours saved by what an hour of your time is worth, then compare that with the fee in the same currency.

What to Do Next: A 30-Day Plan

Spend the first month on one use, count the results and only then keep, drop or upgrade it. The steps below are my own plan, not a sourced method.

  1. Days 1 to 7: count. Note the three repeat tasks that take most hours and how long each takes.
  2. Days 8 to 10: pick. Choose one use from the 25 that has a free start and low risk.
  3. Days 11 to 24: trial. Run it on real work with one person, and review every output before it reaches a customer.
  4. Days 25 to 30: decide. Compare minutes before and after, count the errors you fixed, then keep, drop or upgrade.

Before you start, copy this checklist:

  • [ ] I can state the task and what a right answer looks like in one sentence.
  • [ ] I know what customer data the tool will see and where it is stored.
  • [ ] A named person reviews output before it reaches a customer.
  • [ ] I tested it in the language my customers actually write in.
  • [ ] I have a before-and-after number in minutes.

Frequently asked questions

How can small businesses use AI?

Small businesses use AI for repeat work such as answering customer questions, drafting emails and posts, summarising meetings, moving data between apps and sorting leads. The 25 uses above group these by function. Start with one task, a free tier and a person who reviews the output.

Is AI good for small business?

AI is good for small business when it takes over repeat tasks and a person still checks the result. The British Business Bank lists productivity, lower costs and better customer satisfaction as possible benefits, and poor data, cost, bias and legal concerns as risks. Measure your own savings, since most published figures come from vendors.

What is AI for small businesses?

AI for small businesses is everyday software that drafts, sorts, summarises, answers or predicts, usually built into tools you already use. The British Business Bank names chatbots, data analysis and content creation as the most common kinds.

Which AI is best for small business?

No single AI is best for every small business, because the right one depends on the task, your data and who reviews the output. The sources name different tools for writing, design, calls, meetings and sales, and this article does not rank them. Pick the use first, then try a free tier.

What are the best free AI tools for small businesses?

The free tools the sources name include ChatGPT, Canva, Perplexity, Notion, Zapier (100 tasks a month), Scribbl (10 meetings a month), Fathom and the HubSpot free CRM. These are as listed by Salesforce and MindStudio on 20 November 2026, and plans change, so check each vendor's site.

Do I need technical skills to use AI tools?

You do not need technical skills for most tools on this list. Google says that if you can send an email you can use Gemini, and Salesforce says most AI tools take plain-English instructions. Connecting AI to WhatsApp or a CRM is where a developer helps.

How much do AI tools cost for a small business?

Many tools are free to start, and the first paid plans in the MindStudio list run from $10 to $20 a month per user in US dollars, as of 20 November 2026. Chatbots and call assistants are priced separately, and AI model usage is billed on top by the provider. Elyx engagements start from ₹9,999, and everything else is quoted after a call.

Can AI replace employees?

The sources treat AI as help for staff, not a replacement. Salesforce says AI should support employees and that complex or sensitive issues should go to a human. Plan for assisted work first and keep a person on anything involving empathy, judgement or customer data.

Are AI tools secure enough for business use?

AI tools are secure enough for business use only if you check what data they see and where it goes. Salesforce advises caution with free public AI models when handling sensitive customer information or trade secrets. Google states that Gemini in Workspace does not use your data to train its models, which is the vendor's own claim and worth checking against your plan's terms.
