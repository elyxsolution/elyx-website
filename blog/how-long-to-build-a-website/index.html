How Long Does It Take to Build a Website? Real Timelines by Project Type

How long to build a website: timelines for landing pages, business sites, stores and apps, why they differ, and what delays a launch.

How long to build a website depends mostly on scope and on how quickly you supply content. At Elyx Solutions, a landing page usually takes 1–2 weeks, a business website 2–4 weeks and an e-commerce store or custom web app 4–8 weeks. Published guides from Elementor and Forbes Advisor give similar or longer ranges, and all of them assume you are not the bottleneck: late copy, photos and approvals stop the clock.

  • Short answer. One page is about 1–2 weeks, a store or web app 4–8 weeks, and an app or SaaS MVP longer, all indicative.
  • The gap. The jump from 1 to 2 weeks to 4 to 8 weeks comes from more pages, payments or logins, more content and more people approving, not from slower work.
  • The usual delay. Elementor, Wix and Forbes Advisor all point to content, and Forbes puts content creation at 4 to 6 weeks, the longest step in its process.
  • Your lever. One decision maker, final copy and photos in hand, and a written page list before design starts.

Every figure in the Elyx column is a usual timeline, not a promise, and a fixed quote and schedule are confirmed in writing after a call.

Two cautions on the Elementor column. Its own summary list gives e-commerce as 8 to 16 weeks while its table says 8 to 12, so treat that row as soft. Its "custom web application" row covers social networks and SaaS tools, which is wider than a portal that turns one workflow into software, so the gap to the web app figure is mostly about definition.

Why a Landing Page Takes 1 to 2 Weeks and a Store 4 to 8

A landing page takes 1–2 weeks and a store or web app 4–8 weeks because five things grow together: pages, functions, content, approvals and testing. The Elyx column describes a landing page as one page with an enquiry form and a WhatsApp button, and e-commerce as a catalogue, cart, UPI and card payments, order emails and an admin panel.

What growsLanding pageStore or web app
PagesOneCatalogue, cart, checkout or dashboards
FunctionsEnquiry form, WhatsApp buttonPayments, order emails, admin panel, logins
Content to supplyOne page of copy and photosEvery product or screen
ApprovalsOne page to sign offMany screens, each with feedback
TestingOne page across devicesPayments and uploads as well

Elementor says a simple request such as "I want user logins" can add weeks, and that a store is closer to a software application than a brochure. The studio's own process adds two rounds of feedback to a build, so each extra screen is also an extra decision.

My reading, not a measured rate: the 1 to 2 week figure holds when the site is one page, the copy exists and one person approves. Remove any of the three and the project drifts toward the next band.

Why Published Timelines Disagree: Four Sources Added Up

Published timelines disagree mainly because each source counts different stages, and adding their stage ranges shows it. The table sums each source's stages as read on 17 November 2026; where a source gives months, I count a month as four weeks.

SourceStages countedTotal weeks
Forbes Advisor India (page audited 12 July 2023)Planning, content, design, testing9 to 14 by my addition; the page says 10 to 14
Fasthosts, DIY builderPlanning, builder choice, content, layout, customising, testing8 to 19
Fasthosts, hired developerConcept, hiring, brief, mockups, build, review9 to 23
Elementor, business site, professionalStrategy, design, build, content, launch8 (its range is 4 to 8)
This site, business websiteNot stated in the data2–4 weeks

The arithmetic: Forbes gives planning 2 to 3, content 4 to 6, design 2 to 3 and testing 1 to 2, so 2+4+2+1 = 9 and 3+6+3+2 = 14. Fasthosts' builder route is 1+1+2+1+2+1 = 8 to 3+1+6+3+4+2 = 19, and its developer route is 1+1+1+1+4+1 = 9 to 2+4+3+3+8+3 = 23.

The spread has three causes. Fasthosts counts finding a developer, at 1 to 4 weeks, and Forbes treats content as a stage of its own. Elementor schedules content population in week 7 of 8, yet also says all text and images must be ready on day one to launch in four weeks.

So a 4-week answer and a 14-week answer can both be right, from different starting lines. Ask which starting line a quote uses before you compare dates.

What Delays a Website Launch: Content Comes First

Content is the most common reason a website is late: Elementor calls it the number one stall, and Wix says the biggest cause of overrun is copy, photos, logo and legal pages that are not ready. Forbes Advisor's schedule agrees, with content creation at 4 to 6 weeks, longer than any other step. Elementor ranks an undefined scope second.

Wix adds two more causes: deciding the page structure late, which forces layouts to be redone, and several reviewers commenting in separate rounds. Wix goes further and says preparing these three things can turn a two-month build into a two-week one. That is the author's claim, with no data behind it.

In India the content list often includes documents only the owner holds. The studio's industry pages note that food businesses are expected to show an FSSAI licence number, that real estate projects show a RERA number, and that a clinic approves every claim, photo and testimonial against its council's advertising rules. Languages matter too: multiple languages are scoped as a separate option, because every page is written again.

What goes wrongCauseFix

Layouts are redonePage list changed late (Wix)Sign off the page list first
Feedback dragsSeveral reviewers, separate rounds (Wix)One decision maker, an agreed reply time
Features arrive mid-buildUndefined scope (Elementor)A "not in version one" list

Worked Example: One Approval Cycle Turns 4 Weeks Into 7

A slow approval cycle can add three weeks to a four-week plan, and the arithmetic below shows how. Elementor says a decisive client can finalise designs in a week, while a large committee can sit in revisions for a month.

Take a business website planned at four weeks, the top of the usual 2–4 weeks, with design as week 1 as in the studio's process. The table changes only the approval time.

ScenarioDesign approvalLaunch
One decision maker1 weekWeek 4
Committee in revisions4 weeksWeek 7

The extra time is 4 minus 1 = 3 weeks, so the launch moves from week 4 to week 4 + 3 = week 7. This is an illustration built from Elementor's description, not a measured result, and it treats approval time as the whole of the design stage. Naming one decision maker is worth up to three weeks in this example.

Website Builder, AI or Developer: What Each Route Saves

A builder or AI tool saves the most calendar time on a simple site, and a developer saves the most of your own hours on anything with payments, booking or logins. The table lists published figures, read on 17 November 2026.

RoutePublished timeSource
AI builder, simple siteMinutes to hours; under 1 hour of your timeWix
DIY builder, small business siteDays to weeks; about 20 to 40 hours of your timeWix
DIY, 5 to 15 pages1 to 2 weeksElementor
Professional developer2 to 5 monthsWix
Professional, small business site4 to 8 weeksElementor
Professional, averageA couple of months; up to six if complexFasthosts

Wix, Elementor and Fasthosts all sell website tools, so their DIY and AI figures come from sellers. Forbes Advisor states that it may earn a commission on partner links. Fasthosts adds that an AI builder shortens the build but you still plan content and review everything.

  • Choose an AI builder or template when the site is one or two simple pages, you have the hours, and nothing needs wiring in, such as booking or payments.
  • Choose a developer or studio when the site takes payments, booking or logins, needs WhatsApp and tracking set up, or your own hours are the scarce thing.

For who to hire, the freelancer, agency or small studio comparison sets out the trade-offs. The fastest way to build a website is a template or AI builder on a simple page, at the cost of doing the content and editing yourself. With a studio, the web development page lists how fast you need it live among the things that move the price, so ask for the rush cost in writing.

How to Shorten a Website Build Without Cutting Corners

You shorten a build by removing waiting time, not by skipping steps, and the checklist below covers most of it. Copy it into a document and tick it before the first call.

  • [ ] Final copy for every page sits in one shared document.
  • [ ] Photos and logo files sit in one folder.
  • [ ] The page list is written and signed off before design starts.
  • [ ] One person decides, and everyone else sends notes to that person.
  • [ ] Licence and registration numbers your trade must display are gathered, and claims are approved.
  • [ ] Domain and hosting logins are to hand.
  • [ ] A "not in version one" list holds every extra idea.
  • [ ] The builder has answered in writing: what does this date assume?

Elementor says doing its first item puts you ahead of 90% of website projects. It gives no data for that figure, so read it as a rule of thumb. The website brief template has a field for each item above.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to build a website?

A landing page takes about 1–2 weeks, a business website 2–4 weeks and a store or web app 4–8 weeks at Elyx. Published guides range from days for a DIY page to six months or more for a complex custom build. Late content is the usual reason a date slips.

How long does it take to build a website from scratch?

Building from scratch with custom code is the slowest path: Elementor calls custom code the most time-consuming route, and Wix puts a professional build at 2 to 5 months. A studio working from a defined scope can be faster, with 4 to 8 weeks for a store or web app on this site's draft data.

How long does it take to build a website with WordPress?

Elementor says WordPress with its visual builder needs a few hours of setup, then 1 to 2 weeks for a DIY small business site and 4 to 8 weeks for a professional one. Elementor sells that builder, so read the DIY figures as a seller's.

How long does it take to build a website on Wix or Squarespace?

On Wix, an AI-built site takes minutes, a template site hours to weeks, and the Wix author's own editor builds took four weeks to three months. My research has no Squarespace figure, so check Squarespace's own pages. Elementor describes both as fast, managed platforms with limits on features.

How long does it take to build a website with AI?

A simple AI-generated site can be live in under an hour of your time, according to Wix, which sells an AI builder. Fasthosts says the build gets shorter but you still plan content and review everything. Content and approvals are the steps AI does not remove.

How many hours does it take to build a website?

Wix estimates roughly 20 to 40 hours for a small business site you build yourself, spread over a few weeks, and hundreds of hours for a custom developer build. Those are one vendor's estimates, not measured averages. Hours of your own time fall sharply if a studio does the build.

How much does it cost to build a website?

Elyx engagements start from ₹9,999, excluding GST, and a landing page or a business website is quoted to your requirements, in writing, after a call. The pricing page explains how.

How long does a website last?

Forbes Advisor says the average lifespan of a website is two years and seven months, and it ties early abandonment to stale content, bugs, poor user experience and weak SEO. It does not name the data behind the figure, so treat it as a secondary claim. Planning for updates at the start extends a site's life.

How can I build a website quickly?

Have final copy, photos and the page list ready before design starts, and name one person to approve. For a simple page, a template or AI builder is quickest. For anything with payments or booking, ask a studio for the date in writing and what it assumes.
