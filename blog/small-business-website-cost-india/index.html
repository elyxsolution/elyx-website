Small business website cost in India runs from about 7.5k for a basic few-page site to 1.5 lakh for a custom-designed business site, according to the vendor price lists read for this article on 16 November 2026. What you pay decides how many pages you get, who writes them and whether search setup and tracking are included. The qualification is that every figure comes from a vendor selling websites, and 18% GST is often charged on top.

  • The market. Six of seven vendor ranges for a business site cover 50k to 75k, with bottoms from 10k to 50k and tops from 50k to 150k.
  • 10k. A few template pages, your own copy, and little or no search setup.
  • 30k. Five to eight pages or a revamp, with copywriting, a CMS and SEO usually extra.
  • 1 lakh. A custom-designed site of 10 to 20 pages, where the risk shifts to paying for scope you do not need.
  • Tax and upkeep. 18% GST turns a 30k budget into about 25.4k of website, and running costs follow every launch.

What Moves a Website Quote From Cheap to Expensive

Web Eventuals names six drivers: pages and content, functionality, design, who writes the content, search setup, and hosting and maintenance. Page count alone misleads. The same page says ten short pages you have already written cost less than five pages that each need research, copywriting and custom graphics.

Budget Arithmetic: What GST Takes Out of Each Amount

A budget stated all in has 18% GST inside it, so a 30k budget buys about 25.4k of website once tax is removed. Aero Business Solutions says most freelancer and agency quotes in India exclude 18% GST unless they state otherwise. Its example is a 75k quote that becomes 88.5k on the invoice, because 75 × 1.18 = 88.5.

Dividing by 1.18 reverses the tax. The table applies it to the three budgets.

Budget, all inDivide by 1.18Website price before tax, thousand rupees
10k10 ÷ 1.188.47
30k30 ÷ 1.1825.42
1 lakh100 ÷ 1.1884.75

Ask whether a number includes tax before comparing quotes. Aero warns that a cheaper-looking quote without tax can end up costing more than one that includes it, and Web Eventuals advises asking for the price with tax shown. This is a business lens on pricing, not tax advice: whether you can recover GST depends on your registration, so ask your accountant.

Where Corners Get Cut at Each Budget

Corners get cut in different places at each budget: search setup and ownership at 10k, copy and extras at 30k, and scope at 1 lakh. Each tier below gives what the sources say it buys, what is usually cut, and when it is the right choice.

At 10k: a few template pages and your own copy

At 10k, expect one to five pages on a template with your own words and photos. Aero Business Solutions puts a basic static site of four or five pages at 7.5k to 25k, for portfolios and digital visiting cards. Spidery Web describes its 5k to 15k tier as a generic template, text you write or copy, usually no SEO, a contact form only, and ownership sometimes held by the developer.

The cuts are search setup, speed, lead tracking and ownership. Choose this tier for a short event or a test of demand: Spidery says a cheap site can be enough for those, but costs more in lost leads if it must bring enquiries for years.

At 30k: a brochure site or a revamp

At 30k, expect five to eight pages or a revamp, with design lightly customised and copy mostly yours. Web Eventuals puts a five-page site near 35k, and Spidery Web puts an 8-page site at 35k and a revamp at 20k to 40k. MilesWeb says small businesses often start with a professional site averaging 25k to 30k, including visuals, text and a logo.

Vendors disagree here. Aero Business Solutions says 25k to 60k covers an 8 to 15 page CMS site with WhatsApp, Maps and basic on-page SEO, a good deal more than the others describe at 30k.

The usual cuts are copywriting, a CMS and SEO. Choose this tier when your text and photos are ready and you need local enquiries, for example for a clinic, tutor or salon.

At 1 lakh: a custom-designed business site

At 1 lakh, expect a custom-designed site of 10 to 20 pages with a CMS, writing and SEO foundations. Web Eventuals puts a twelve-page site with a CMS you can edit at about 75k, and a larger site with proper on-page SEO near 1.5 lakh. It also puts a Shopify or WooCommerce store at 45k to 1.5 lakh, with payment processing taking roughly 2 to 3 percent of each transaction.

The risk at this level is paying for scope you do not need, so Aero advises reserving custom code for the one or two features that need it. Its payback example spreads a 1.5 lakh site over five years: 150 ÷ 60 months = 2.5k a month. On the same arithmetic a 1 lakh site is about 1.7k a month before running costs, and the payoff assumes extra customers that no page can promise.

Applied to a 30k site, 15 to 20 percent is 30 × 0.15 = 4.5k to 30 × 0.20 = 6k a year. For a 1 lakh site it is 15k to 20k a year, about 1.25k to 1.7k a month. Secuodsoft gives a wider 10 to 20 percent, so treat the rule as a budgeting guide.

Watch the renewal price. Five Online says a hosting plan advertised near 250 rupees a month can renew at roughly 675 to 845 rupees. Maintenance is optional for a small site, and Web Eventuals notes that many run on fixes billed as they arise.

DIY, Freelancer or Studio: Who Should Build at Your Budget

DIY suits a side project, a freelancer suits a small site with content ready, and a studio suits a site the business depends on for enquiries. The table uses Web Eventuals' figures.

RouteTypical costMain risk
DIY builderA few hundred to a few thousand rupees a monthYour own time, and a template look
Freelancer15k to 60k for a small siteOne person to depend on after launch
Studio or agency40k to 1.5 lakh for most business sitesPaying for scope you do not need

Use these decision rules:

  • Choose DIY when the site is a basic presence, you can give the time, and a free tier's limits do not matter.
  • Choose a freelancer when the scope is small, your text and photos are finished, and you can name a backup if they go quiet.
  • Choose a studio when enquiries depend on the site and you want design, copy, search setup and development under one roof.

What Usually Goes Wrong, and a Quote Checklist to Catch It

Most cheap-quote disasters trace to an unwritten scope, so the fix is a quote that names pages, features, copy, ownership and running costs. These failures come from Aero Business Solutions, Spidery Web and Web Eventuals:

  • Vague inclusions. A bare "website" quote lets 15k quietly become 45k as extras are billed, Aero warns. Fix it by listing pages, features and revision rounds in writing.
  • Full payment first. Aero flags payment demanded before any design review as a red flag. Fix it by paying in milestones.
  • Nobody writes the copy. Web Eventuals says a 20k quote almost always assumes you supply finished text. Fix it by asking who writes it before you compare prices.
  • Ownership gaps. Spidery notes some developers hold the domain and logins. Fix it by registering both in your name.

Copy this checklist before you pay:

  1. Are pages, design level and features each written down?
  2. Who writes the copy, and is search setup included?
  3. Is tax shown, and is the price before or after it?
  4. Do domain, hosting and logins stay in your name?
  5. Is year-one hosting and support inside the price?
  6. Are tracking events for calls, WhatsApp and forms set up?
  7. Can you see a site built over a year ago?

What to Do Next

Start by writing down the one action the site must produce, then turn your budget into a pre-tax figure and ask for written quotes on that basis. Elyx Solutions can scope this through its web development service, but the steps work with any vendor.

  1. Name the job: enquiries by WhatsApp, calls, bookings or online orders.
  2. Divide your all-in budget by 1.18 to get the pre-tax amount.
  3. List pages, features, who writes the copy and who owns the domain.
  4. Ask two or three vendors for written quotes, and compare them line by line.
  5. Before launch, test the result with the website launch checklist.

Frequently asked questions

How much for a small business website?

Seven vendor pages put a small business website between 10k and 150k at the extremes, and six of their ranges cover 50k to 75k. Page count, design, features and who writes the copy decide where you land.

How much does a small website cost?

A small site of one to five pages is quoted at roughly 7.5k to 40k. Aero Business Solutions gives 7.5k to 25k for four or five static pages, and Web Eventuals gives 15k to 40k for up to five.

How much does a website cost in India?

Aero Business Solutions says a website in India can cost anything from about 7.5k for a basic static site to 25 lakh and more for a custom web application. Small business sites sit near the low end of that span.

How much does it cost to maintain a website for a small business?

Spidery Web puts light maintenance at 1.5k to 5k a month, while Web Eventuals prices care plans at 6k to 20k a month. MilesWeb and Five Online suggest budgeting 15 to 20 percent of the build cost each year. Many small sites need less and pay for fixes as they come up.

Is a 5k to 10k website enough for a small business?

A 5k to 10k website is enough for a short event or a test, not for years of enquiries. Spidery Web says a cheap site can serve an event or test, but costs more in lost leads if it must bring business over time. Expect a template, your own copy and little search setup.

Is a website quote in India inclusive of GST?

Usually not: Aero Business Solutions says most freelancer and agency quotes exclude 18% GST unless stated. Its example is a 75k quote that becomes 88.5k on the invoice. Ask for the price with tax shown, and confirm treatment with your accountant.

Can I get a free website for my small business?

Yes, with a free-tier builder or a no-cost platform on free hosting, according to Web Eventuals. You then take on the design, copywriting and maintenance yourself, and free tiers usually have real limits. It works for a basic presence, not for a site the business depends on.

How long does a small business website take to build?

Web Eventuals says a simple small business site takes two to six weeks, and a custom site with several integrated features six to twelve. Spidery Web says three to five weeks after the content is approved. Ask for the date to be written on the quote.
