Websites for CA Firms and Tax Consultants: Trust, Services and Client Portals

A website for chartered accountants in India: ICAI-aware service pages, partner profiles, document upload options and GST and ITR season pages, with costs.

A website for chartered accountants in India has to prove the firm is real and current, give each service its own page, and make contact easy, all inside ICAI's limits on advertising and solicitation. Two parts are barely covered by the four top-ranking pages the research read: none has a GST or income tax season page, and only one mentions a document upload area. The qualification that matters is regulation: the research held vendor summaries of the ICAI guidelines, not ICAI's own text, so the rules section is a business checklist and not professional advice.

  • Rules. Vendor summaries say a CA site may inform but not advertise: no fees, client names, superlatives or offers. Confirm against ICAI's Code of Ethics.
  • Pages. One page per service, partner profiles with membership numbers, an address and a working contact route.
  • Season pages. One permanent page per filing, updated each cycle, with the due date copied from the official portal and the date you checked it.
  • Documents. Pick the lightest upload route your firm can run and secure, and stop receiving PAN scans as plain email attachments.

ICAI Rules First: Inform, Do Not Solicit

ICAI guidance, as vendors summarise it, lets a CA firm publish factual information on a website but not advertise or solicit clients, and that shapes every page below. The White Cat, a web agency selling CA sites, states it that way in its own FAQ. SAG Infotech's CA Portal blog, last updated on 13 August 2026, says the guidelines were recalled at the ICAI Council's 388th meeting on 6 and 7 February 2020 and sit on page 143 of Volume II of the Code of Ethics, 2020.

The same blog says ignoring the Council's guidelines is treated as professional misconduct. It also says a new site's address should be reported to ICAI within 30 days and that the site should show a last-updated date. The table below pulls the content rules from Webtel and SAG Infotech together. Both are vendors, so treat it as a checklist to verify.

Content itemWhat the summaries saySource
Client names and logosNot allowedWebtel
Professional feesNot to be mentionedWebtel, SAG Infotech
"Leading", "Best", "Top" firmNot allowedWebtel
PhotographsPassport-style only, no event galleriesWebtel

Offers, schemes, advertisementsNot allowedSAG Infotech
TestimonialsSources differ: see belowWebtel, SAG Infotech

On testimonials, Webtel's list of prohibited items does not name them, while a vendor reply in SAG Infotech's comments says they can be added. I could not settle that from ICAI text, so ask ICAI, or its Ethical Standards Board, before publishing one.

Webtel's page carries a 2022 title and SAG Infotech cites the 2020 guidelines, so neither proves what applies today. I checked both on 16 November 2026. Read the current text at esb.icai.org, which SAG Infotech names, and on ICAI's own site, which carries its announcements.

Searches for a chartered accountants website also return Irish, Australian and UK bodies. The ICAEW, ICAS and Chartered Accountants Ireland audit booklet (October 2026) covers UK registered auditors, not Indian firms' sites.

Chartered Accountant Firm Websites: What Prospective Clients Check

A prospective client decides within about a minute whether a firm exists properly, does the specific work they need and is easy to reach. That is Finexo's description, and Finexo sells CA site templates, so read it as a vendor's view. It also says most CA enquiries still begin with a referral, and the site's job is what happens after someone searches the firm's name.

Finexo's completeness test has five parts, which work as a checklist:

  • A clear statement of what the firm does.
  • A separate page for each substantial service.
  • The names and credentials of the people who do the work.
  • A physical address.
  • A contact route that reaches a person.

The White Cat adds a risk: a client who finds nothing, or a page untouched since 2016, quietly calls the next firm. That is a vendor claim with no data behind it. SAG Infotech's list of expected elements adds year of establishment, head office and branch addresses, phone, email, partner details, news and blogs.

Service Pages: One Page for Each Service

Each substantial service needs its own page, because a visitor searching for GST help is not looking for a page that lists everything. The White Cat suggests separate pages for audit, direct tax, GST, ROC compliance and advisory, and says people search "GST registration consultant", not "chartered accountant". Building such pages is routine web development work.

The table below is my own plan, built from those sources and from the practice areas on the studio's CA and law firms page.

PageQuestion the visitor hasWhat to put on it
GSTCan you register me and file my returns?Who it suits, what you handle, documents to bring
Income taxCan you file my return?Individuals, firms, companies, how an engagement starts
AuditDo you do statutory or tax audits?Types of audit, team, what you need from the client
ROC and company lawCan you handle company filings?Forms you file, documents, who signs
AdvisoryCan I talk through a decision first?Topics you advise on, how a consultation works

Every page follows the same shape: who it is for, what the firm does, what to bring, how it starts and a short FAQ. Leave out fee amounts, because the summaries above list fees as content to avoid. Explain instead how fees are agreed, and have the firm check that wording.

Partner Profiles: Credentials a Client Can Verify

Each partner profile should show name, qualification, membership number, specialisation and a real photograph, which The White Cat calls the first thing a prospective client checks. Webtel's summary adds that photographs should be passport-style, with no event galleries. SAG Infotech asks for full partner details such as name, qualification and contact.

A short checklist for one profile:

  • Name as registered with ICAI.
  • Qualification and year.
  • Membership number.
  • Specialisation, in plain words.
  • Passport-style photograph.
  • A direct phone or email route.

The membership number is also what lets a visitor confirm the person is a chartered accountant, a question people ask. The research did not read ICAI's verification tool, so I do not describe its steps: link to ICAI and let the visitor check there. SAG Infotech says an official link to ICAI is permitted on a CA site.

GST and ITR Season Landing Pages: What to Build

A season page is a permanent page for one recurring filing, such as GST returns or income tax returns, that you update each cycle instead of replacing. The idea follows from the brief: tax deadlines recur every year, so the same page earns its keep repeatedly. None of the four top pages read for this article covers it, which is the research's own finding of a gap.

I have typed no due date in this article, because the research held no official calendar. Dates get revised, so copy them from the official portal each time. The template below is my own, built from the sources on service pages and ICAI content rules:

  • URL and title. No year in the address, so the page can be reused.
  • What it covers. Who needs the filing and what the firm handles.
  • Due date box. The date, the official page it came from, and the day you checked it.
  • Documents to gather. A checklist the client can tick.
  • How it works. Steps from first message to filing, with no fee amounts.
  • Upload and contact. One upload route, one WhatsApp number, one phone.
  • FAQ. Four or five plain questions.
  • Last updated. A visible date, as SAG Infotech says ICAI expects.

Leave out offers, countdown banners and "file with us now" lines. SAG Infotech's summary rules out offers, schemes and advertisements, and says site content should reach people only in response to a request, a "pull" model. That reading would also apply to mass emails or WhatsApp broadcasts of site content, so ask before sending any.

Keeping Season Pages Current: A Yearly Cycle

A season page stays accurate when someone named owns a four-step cycle, because a stale due date does more harm than no page. The steps below are my own plan, not a sourced method.

  1. Before the season. Refresh the document checklist and FAQ, and confirm partner names and numbers.
  2. At the start. Paste the due date from the official page, with the page named and today's date beside it.
  3. During the season. Re-check the date weekly and change the last-updated line each time.
  4. After the season. Remove stale notices, keep the page and record what changed for next year.

Name one partner as owner and one staff member as editor. With a simple content editor, which the studio builds where it makes sense, the editor can do steps two to four without a developer.

Document Upload Portals: Email, Shared Folder or Your Own Portal

Choose the lightest upload route your firm can run and protect every day, because an unwatched portal is worse than a clear email process. That is my reading. The White Cat sells an encrypted upload area so clients stop emailing balance sheets and PAN scans as plain attachments, and SAG Infotech lists a login among expected elements.

RouteChoose it whenWhat it needs from you
Email attachmentFew clients, small filesA rule on what may be sent
Shared-folder linkSeveral clients, no custom buildPer-client folders, link settings, someone who audits access
Own portal with loginMany recurring clientsA build, user accounts, a named owner, a privacy notice

Whatever you choose, answer these before launch: who can see each client's files, how long files stay, who is alerted on an upload, and what happens when a client leaves. Ask any vendor what it stores and where. Elyx's CA page refers to a privacy notice aligned to India's DPDP Act, and the research held no text of the Act, so take professional advice on your duties.

The studio's price data lists a custom web app or portal with logins and dashboards. The nearest proof in its case studies is Malnad Stories, where customers upload photos and pay online. That is not a tax-document portal, and I do not claim one. Also read the Income Tax e-filing portal's help pages, "My CA FAQ" and "Work with My CA FAQ". The research found them but did not read their text, so I leave their process to the official source.

Find a Chartered Accountant: Local Search and Google Business Profile

A client looking for a CA usually searches by city and service, so the firm needs a Google Business Profile and a service page for each city it serves. The White Cat says most CA enquiries begin as a "near me" search on a phone, which is a vendor claim. Finexo says no site builder can promise a ranking position, and that is true of any build.

Keep the firm name, address and phone identical on the profile, the site and listings. A free Google Business Profile checker scores the profile. Searches such as "chartered accountants near me fees" meet the fee problem again: explain how fees are agreed and leave amounts off.

An insights section helps both search and trust. The studio's CA page suggests short notes on tax and compliance changes, and SAG Infotech expects news, blogs and a visible updated date. Date each note and say who wrote it.

What to Do Next: Seven Steps

Start with the rules and the partner details, because both are free and every page depends on them.

  1. Read the rules. Open the Code of Ethics, Volume II, and esb.icai.org, and note the date.
  2. Collect partner details. Name, qualification, membership number, specialisation, passport-style photo.
  3. List the services. One page each, with the documents a client brings.
  4. Choose the upload route. Use the table and your team's real capacity.
  5. Draft one season page. Use the template, with a checked-on date.
  6. Fix the Google profile. Match name, address and phone everywhere.
  7. Build, test, review. Check on a phone over mobile data, then review quarterly.

Frequently asked questions

Can a chartered accountant have a website in India?

Yes, according to the vendor summaries of ICAI guidance read for this article. A CA may publish factual information such as services, qualifications and contact details but may not advertise or solicit. Confirm the current rules with ICAI before you publish.

What should a website for chartered accountants include?

It should include a statement of services, a page for each service, partner names and credentials, an address and a working contact route. SAG Infotech adds the year of establishment and a last-updated date.

Can a CA website show fees, client names or testimonials?

The vendor summaries list professional fees and client names as content to avoid. On testimonials they disagree: Webtel's prohibited list does not name them, and a SAG Infotech vendor reply says they are allowed. Check with ICAI before publishing any of the three.

How do I check whether an accountant is chartered?

Ask for the ICAI membership number and check it with ICAI. The White Cat says partner profiles should show that number. The research did not read ICAI's verification tool, so use ICAI's official site for the steps.

What do chartered accountants do?

The White Cat lists the services CA firms offer as audit, direct tax, GST, ROC compliance and advisory. Webtel's summary adds that members in practice may act as financial advisers under Regulation 191 of the Chartered Accountants Regulations, 1988.

Can a chartered accountant do business?

The research did not contain the rule, so I cannot answer it. Webtel's summary says a CA site should not mention other businesses or occupations, which is a limit on the site's content. Ask ICAI about what a member may do.

How much does a CA firm website cost in India?

On Elyx's draft, indicative price data, a landing page is ₹9,999–₹24,999, a business website is ₹24,999–₹59,999 and a custom portal is ₹75,000–₹3,00,000, all excluding GST. A fixed quote is confirmed in writing after a call.

How long does a CA firm website take?

On the same data, a business website typically takes 2–4 weeks and a custom web app or portal 4–8 weeks. The White Cat quotes 3 to 5 weeks for a standard CA site and 6 to 8 with a portal, which is its own claim.

Does a CA firm need a client portal?

No, a portal suits firms with many recurring clients and someone to run it. A firm with few clients can start with a clear email rule or a shared-folder link.
