WordPress vs Webflow vs Next.js: Which Is Right for Your Business Site?

WordPress vs Webflow vs Next.js compared on cost, speed, editing and scaling, with a verdict by business type and sourced figures dated November 2026.

WordPress vs Webflow vs Next.js comes down to three questions: who edits the site, how fast it must load, and who looks after it once it is live. For a small Indian business with five to ten pages and no developer on staff, Webflow or a well-kept WordPress site is usually the safer start, and Next.js earns its higher build cost when the site is your main source of enquiries or needs custom features. The qualification that matters is that every source I read sells one of these platforms, so the cost and speed figures here are agency and vendor claims, not independent tests.

  • Cost. In one agency's five-year table the totals overlap: Webflow is cheapest at the low end and Next.js dearest at the high end.
  • Speed. The sources rank a well-built Next.js site fastest, but none is a controlled test.
  • Editing. Webflow and WordPress suit non-developers; Next.js needs a content system added.
  • Verdict. A table by business type names the first pick and when another option wins.

WordPress vs Webflow vs Next.js: What Each Platform Is

WordPress is free open-source software that you host yourself, Webflow is a paid visual builder with hosting included, and Next.js is a coding framework that needs a separate content system. Studio Melamed, a Next.js agency, adds that a Webflow site on a custom domain needs a paid Site plan, and that Next.js is a stack of framework, content system and host rather than one subscription. Pryce Digital sums up the bets: WordPress favours editor flexibility, Webflow designer control, and Next.js developer control.

Market share tells you less than it seems. W3Techs data cited by Vezert (survey of 20 August 2026) has WordPress on 40.8% of all websites and Webflow on 0.8%, with 30.9% running no detectable CMS, a group that includes custom Next.js builds. Vezert's own point is that share shows help is easy to find, not that a platform fits your business.

The Criteria Used and Who Wrote the Sources

I compared the platforms on five criteria: five-year cost, speed, editing, scaling and upkeep, and I name who wrote each source because most of them sell one platform. Studio Melamed, Pryce Digital, Vezert, 404MEDIA and Plinth Studio each say they build on Next.js, and Webflow's own page compares itself with WordPress. Instabizweb says it ships all three, while Modus Venture and Quesiono give no verdict but also no prices or speed figures. Read every number below as a claim from a party with a stake, dated 17 November 2026, not as a fact about your site.

Elyx Solutions is in the same position. Its web development page says it uses React, Next.js and TypeScript for most front ends and adds a headless CMS (a content system that feeds a separate front end) where it makes sense, so discount its view accordingly. The page also says it avoids proprietary lock-in that would make a client depend on the studio.

Speed: What the Sources Claim and What Google Measures

The sources put a well-built Next.js site fastest, Webflow in the middle and WordPress lowest and most variable, but none of the numbers comes from a controlled test. Google's Core Web Vitals, as Studio Melamed summarises them, call a page good when Largest Contentful Paint (when the main content appears) is 2.5 seconds or less, Interaction to Next Paint (response to a tap) 200 milliseconds or less and Cumulative Layout Shift (layout jumping) 0.1 or less, on the 75th percentile of real visits.

PlatformLCP, Pryce DigitalLCP, Instabizweb
Next.js, well builtUnder 1.5 seconds1.2 to 1.8 seconds
Webflow1.8 to 2.5 seconds1.5 to 2.4 seconds
WordPress2.5 to 3.5 seconds1.8 to 3.5 seconds with caching

Instabizweb adds that WordPress without caching, or built on a page builder such as Elementor, can run 4 to 8 seconds, and says its figures come from sites it audited, which a reader cannot check. Field data is thinner: Vezert cites the HTTP Archive for August 2026, with 48.8% of WordPress origins passing all three vitals on mobile, while Plinth Studio cites 40% from the 2024 Web Almanac. Vezert says it could not verify matching figures for Webflow or Next.js.

Studio Melamed says speed is an engineering choice, and Modus Venture says SEO depends more on how a site is built than on its platform. The studio's web development page says most visitors arrive on a phone, often on patchy 4G, so ask each bidder for a live site they built and test it on your own phone with Google's PageSpeed Insights.

Editing: Who Can Change What Without a Developer

Webflow and WordPress let non-developers edit content from day one, while a Next.js site needs a separate CMS before anyone outside engineering can change a word. The table is my summary of Studio Melamed, Instabizweb and Webflow's own page, so its view of WordPress is a vendor's.

TaskWordPressWebflowNext.js with a CMS
Change text, price or photoBlock editorOn the page or in the CMSIn the CMS, once set up
New page layoutTheme or builder, sometimes a developerDesigner, visual canvasDeveloper
New content typePlugin or developerNew CMS CollectionDeveloper plus CMS model

Studio Melamed's rule is the clearest: with Next.js and Sanity your team manages planned content, and changes to layout or behaviour stay development work. Plinth Studio says pairing Next.js with a CMS adds two to four days of engineering. A coaching centre that changes batch dates and fees every week, a need the studio's industry page lists, should test that exact task before signing, as Quesiono advises for any editor.

WordPress for Blogging vs a Business Website

WordPress is strongest when the site is mostly publishing, and the case weakens for a ten-page business site with a form and a map. Vezert calls its editorial workflow, revision history and role permissions the most mature of the three. Plinth Studio points WordPress or Next.js with Sanity at sites planning 200 or more articles, and Instabizweb rates WordPress best past 1,000 posts, while for one or two posts a month any of the three works.

Scaling: When Each Platform Stops Being Enough

Webflow reaches a ceiling first, and the sources place it at about 10,000 pages or complex commerce, while WordPress and Next.js scale with more engineering. Instabizweb says Webflow caps near 10,000 pages, Pryce Digital names limits of 10,000 CMS items and 50 reference fields, and Vezert says Premium allows 20,000 items, so check Webflow's current limit. Pryce Digital prices a headless WordPress with a Next.js front end at 1.5 to 2 times a pure Next.js build, because there are two codebases.

WordPress vs Custom Web Development

A Next.js site is custom web development, so every feature is built rather than installed. Pryce Digital says that pays off when you need custom logic, when the site is a three-year commercial asset, and when you can fund a build of $8,000 or more. It says WordPress is right when one existing plugin solves your problem, such as WooCommerce for a store.

Security, Upkeep and Lock-In

WordPress needs a named person for updates, Webflow runs platform updates itself, and Next.js needs a developer to own its dependencies. Vezert cites plugin counts per WordPress site: 25.2 installed and 18.3 active from Freemius, medians of 25 at Kinsta and 22 at WP Engine, and 21 on average from Patchstack. Each plugin has its own maintainer and its own security surface.

Webflow's page says you never update plugins, core or PHP, and that it backs up every 10 minutes or 10 changes, which is a vendor claim. Pryce Digital says a WordPress site left for three years needs real remedial work, a Webflow site looks dated but keeps working, and a Next.js site runs but sits behind its framework.

Lock-in runs the other way. 404MEDIA says Webflow's code export gives static HTML, CSS and JavaScript, but CMS content, e-commerce and forms do not come along. WordPress is open source and Next.js code can run on any compatible host. What keeping a site healthy costs in India is covered in website maintenance charges.

  • Webflow is stronger when a marketing team wants to design and publish alone, with no plugins to patch, under about 50 pages (Instabizweb).
  • Next.js is stronger when speed, custom logic or owning the code drive revenue and you can fund a developer.

Switching Platforms Without Losing Rankings

Moving platforms is a rebuild plus a redirect plan, and the one source with timelines puts it at 4 to 12 weeks. Instabizweb lists WordPress to Next.js at 4 to 12 weeks, WordPress to Webflow at 4 to 8, and Webflow to Next.js at 6 to 12. To convert WordPress to Webflow, Webflow's page says CMS content can be imported by CSV.

404MEDIA and Modus Venture both say a move keeps rankings only with a complete 301 redirect plan (a permanent forward from each old address). Neither tests the outcome. My outline for any move:

  1. List every old URL that gets traffic or enquiries.
  2. Map each one to its new address.
  3. Rebuild the content and forms, including WhatsApp and call buttons.
  4. Check a staging copy, then launch with redirects live and watch Search Console.

What Usually Goes Wrong

Most platform regrets come from choosing on launch price or popularity instead of on who edits and who maintains. Vezert says the platform that felt easiest in month one is rarely the cheapest by month eighteen.

What to Do Next

Write down who edits the site, how often, and what it must do beyond pages and a form, then price five years, not launch day. Copy this checklist:

  • [ ] I named who edits the site and how often.
  • [ ] I listed payments, booking, logins and languages as needed or not.
  • [ ] I compared five-year totals, not launch prices.
  • [ ] I tested a builder's live site on my phone.
  • [ ] I asked who owns updates and backups after launch.

If your list is mostly pages and a form, Webflow or managed WordPress is likely enough. If it includes booking, payments, logins or several languages, a Next.js quote deserves a look. Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper: WordPress, Webflow or Next.js?

Webflow had the lowest five-year range and Next.js the highest in Pryce Digital's table: 9,800 to 47,500 for Webflow, 12,400 to 54,800 for WordPress and 14,000 to 87,800 for Next.js. The page prints dollars and mixes very different sites at the top end.

Is WordPress hosting cheaper than Webflow?

Not reliably. Vezert puts WordPress hosting and plugins at €50 to €300 a month in year two against €15 to €25 for Webflow, but a Webflow bill covers hosting and CMS while a WordPress bill leaves out the person who maintains the site.

Is Next.js better than WordPress?

It is better for some sites and worse for others. Next.js is stronger on speed, custom logic and owning your code, while WordPress is stronger on publishing workflow and plugins. 404MEDIA, a Next.js agency, says WordPress is still fine for a simple blog.

Is Webflow good for SEO?

Webflow has built-in SEO fields, an automatic sitemap and robots.txt, per Instabizweb, though schema markup needs custom code. Modus Venture says SEO depends more on how a site is built and what it says than on the platform.

Can I edit a Next.js website myself?

Yes, if the build includes a CMS such as Sanity. Studio Melamed says your team can change planned content there, while layout or behaviour changes remain development work.

Can Webflow or WordPress handle a complex project?

Both can, within limits. Instabizweb puts Webflow's ceiling near 10,000 pages or complex commerce and says WordPress needs serious engineering at enterprise scale.

Will I lose rankings if I switch platforms?

Not necessarily. 404MEDIA says a complete 301 redirect plan avoids it, and Modus Venture says a move needs content transfer, redirects and technical SEO checks. No source tests the outcome, so it is a risk to manage, not a guarantee.

Do AI crawlers read JavaScript websites?

According to a December 2024 analysis by Vercel and MERJ, reported by 404MEDIA, the crawlers behind ChatGPT and Claude do not execute JavaScript. Your text must be in the HTML the server sends, which WordPress, Webflow and server-rendered Next.js all provide. I have not read the original analysis.
