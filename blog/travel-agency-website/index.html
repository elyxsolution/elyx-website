Travel and Trek Operators: Websites That Sell Trips (What We Learned Building Malnad Stories)

A travel agency website for Indian trek operators: itinerary pages, bookings, payments, galleries, post-trip upsells and the Malnad Stories build.

A travel agency website sells trips when every trip has its own page with a day-by-day itinerary, dates, a price and a way to hold a seat, and when the buyer can see who is behind the business. The honest qualification is that the five pages our research read cover design and examples, not itineraries, payments or galleries, and the studio's Malnad Stories case study documents a photo-album store, not a booking engine. So the Malnad Stories material below is limited to what the studio has published, and everything else is my reading, labelled as such.

  • Itineraries. Give each trip its own page, written as text, with dates, inclusions and a cancellation rule.
  • Bookings. Pick enquiry, seat request or instant booking by how many departures you run and how fast seats sell.
  • Payments. Take a deposit by UPI or card, state the refund rule before the pay button, and confirm every gateway feature before you promise it.
  • Post-trip. The documented upsell is the photo album: Malnad Stories turns trek photographs into printed albums.

The travel agency websites we could read sell with photography, a stated specialty and an easy way to make contact, and they say little about itineraries, payments or galleries. The table is my summary of five pages read on 16 November 2026, from the research for this article.

Two cautions apply. Site Builder Report says its work is supported by affiliate commissions, and TravelLive's figure of over 2,000 travellers in three years is its own claim, so treat both as opinion and self-reporting.

SOTC does one thing worth copying: its listings carry prices, with cruises described as starting at ₹22,000 for two nights and running past ₹11 lakh for a 14-day Antarctic trip. A buyer who sees a price can decide whether to ask. A trip page with no price makes them call first, and many will not.

The NIDHI directory, run by the Ministry of Tourism, lists registered travel businesses with their registered addresses. For an operator, the practical point is trust: the name and address on your site should match what you registered. What the Malnad Stories Build Documents

Malnad Stories is a photo-album store for trek and travel memories, so it documents the post-trip half of a travel business, not the booking half. Per the studio's Malnad Stories case study, the platform turns trek and travel photographs into printed albums.

What the case study states:

  • The journey. Customers upload their own photos, design an album, pay online and expect a finished product to arrive.
  • The build. A custom printing flow, online checkout built around secure payment handling for Indian customers, cloud storage for photographs, and admin tooling, delivered launch ready by one team.
  • The process. The studio mapped the journey from upload to delivery, agreed the data model, storage and checkout before building, and tested payments and uploads before launch.

The case study gives no build time, stack, order count or conversion figure, so this article gives none. It also does not say Malnad Stories sells treks. My reading is that the useful lesson is the order of work: map the whole customer journey first, then build the parts that handle money and files, then test those two before launch.

Itinerary Pages That Sell a Trek

An itinerary page sells when it answers, in order, what the trip is, what each day involves, what it costs and what happens if the buyer cannot go. The studio's industry pages recommend one page per treatment, per course or per project so each can rank for its own search, and I apply the same rule to trips.

Here is the page order I would use, as my own template and not a sourced standard:

  1. Headline facts. Trip name, number of days, start and end point, difficulty, group size and the next departure dates.
  2. Day by day. One short block per day: where you sleep, what you walk or drive, what meals are covered.
  3. Inclusions and exclusions. Two plain lists, so nobody finds out at the pickup point that train tickets are extra.
  4. Fitness and packing. What a first-timer needs to be ready for and carry.
  5. Price and deposit. The per-person price, the deposit to hold a seat and the date the balance falls due.
  6. Cancellation and weather rule. In words, above the booking button.
  7. Two buttons. Hold a seat, and WhatsApp with the trip name pre-filled.

Publish this as page text, never a PDF. The studio's restaurant page makes the same point about menus: a PDF that takes ten seconds to open on 4G loses the visitor. SOTC also lets buyers browse by season, and Virtuoso's menu groups content by interest, which suggests a trips index filtered by month and difficulty.

Bookings: Enquiry, Seat Request or Instant Booking

Choose the simplest booking model that matches how fast your seats sell, because a live seat counter that is wrong costs more trust than no counter at all. The studio lists a booking feature and a WhatsApp lead flow among the things it builds, and the table below is my decision framework.

ModelChoose it whenWatch for
WhatsApp enquiryFew departures, each handled by phoneSlow replies lose the buyer
Seat request plus depositBatches with a minimum group sizeSeats you must confirm by hand
Instant bookingMany departures, steady demandA seat count that drifts from reality

Site Builder Report notes that one template in its list has a direct booking site that connects the agent with the chosen trip. That is a single secondary example, not evidence about conversion.

A worked example, with assumed numbers, shows what the page should state. Take a batch of 12 seats that runs only with 8, with 5 seats booked. Then 12 - 5 = 7 seats remain, and 8 - 5 = 3 more bookings confirm the departure, so show both numbers.

The studio sets up GA4 events for form submissions, WhatsApp clicks and calls, tagged by page. Use them to see which trip page produces enquiries.

Payments: UPI, Cards and Deposits

Take a deposit online by UPI or card to hold the seat and collect the balance on a date the page states, because a paid seat is a commitment and an enquiry is not. This is my reading, and none of the five pages we read documents payment flows.

What the studio documents is narrower. It builds UPI and card payments through a payment gateway, and its SaaS page names a gateway that supports Indian cards, UPI and international customers. Malnad Stories uses checkout built around secure payment handling for Indian customers.

Ask your gateway, in writing, before you promise anything on the page:

  • Does it support UPI, cards and international cards, if foreign guests book?
  • How are refunds issued and how long do they take?
  • Can it take a deposit now and a balance later?
  • What receipt does the customer get?

The studio's prices are exclusive of GST. How GST applies to your own trip prices and invoices is a question for your chartered accountant, and I have not checked the rules for this article. Put the refund rule on the page above the pay button.

Galleries That Load on a Hill-Station Connection

A trip gallery works when it loads fast on a patchy phone connection and shows your own batches, because buyers on 4G leave slow pages. The studio designs mobile-first for that screen and tunes for Core Web Vitals with compressed images and lean code.

Site Builder Report calls high-end photography a must, and it describes Avenue H as using full-screen video with minimal copy. That suits a luxury brand with a fast connection. For a trek operator whose buyers browse on a phone, my reading is a still image first, with video only on tap.

The studio's salon page advises curated images over a live Instagram feed because live feeds can slow a site. Apply that here and pick the best photos per trip.

Gallery checklist:

  • Use photos from your own batches, not stock.
  • Caption each with the place and the month.
  • Ask guests before you show their faces.
  • One hero image per trip page, compressed before upload.
  • No autoplay video above the booking button.

Post-Trip Upsells: Where the Malnad Stories Model Fits

The post-trip upsell this article can document is the photo album, because Malnad Stories exists to turn trek and travel photographs into printed albums. The case study says customers upload their photos, design an album and pay online.

The table is my sequence for an operator, with timings as suggestions and not data.

MomentWhat to sendWhere it lands
Return dayThank-you and a photo-upload linkWhatsApp
First weekAlbum or print offer from the batch photosWhatsApp or email
Following weeksA request for a Google reviewGoogle Business Profile
Before next seasonNext batch datesWhatsApp

The studio's SEO service lists a reviews process for Google Business Profile among its local work, so the review step has a place in a site build. The case study reports no figures on how many travellers buy albums, so I will not claim any.

Choose by volume. A few batches a year can send guests to an existing album store; a business that expects steady album orders has a reason to build the upload, print and checkout flow Malnad Stories documents. That decision is yours and depends on how many guests you carry.

Travel Agency Website Templates, Free Builders and Custom Builds

A template or free builder is enough when you sell a handful of fixed trips by enquiry, and it stops being enough when you need live seat counts, deposits or an album flow. When we searched "travel agency website" on 16 November 2026, the results listed template pages from Squarespace, Wix, Figma, Framer and Webflow, but we read titles only, so we checked no features or plan terms.

Site Builder Report says many of its example sites use Squarespace, and it earns affiliate commissions, so weigh that as a lead and not a verdict.

OptionChoose it whenLimit
Free builderTesting a first trek with a few friendsCheck branding, domain and payment terms yourself
Paid templateOne trip type, enquiry-ledYour trip logic must fit its layout
Custom buildSeats, deposits, albums or many trip pagesCosts more and takes longer

For the free route, check the plan terms of any builder yourself, since we did not verify them. A template gives you a layout. It does not give you the itinerary, price and cancellation logic described above.

What to Do Next

Start with one trip page and one booking path, then add the rest once you see which enquiries arrive.

  1. Write the itinerary for your most popular trip using the seven-part order above.
  2. Choose enquiry, seat request or instant booking from the table.
  3. Ask your gateway the four questions and write down the answers.
  4. Photograph or select ten images from your own batches and compress them.
  5. Draft the post-trip messages and decide whether to build or link out for albums.
  6. Get an indicative range from the pricing page, then a written quote.

Frequently asked questions

How do I create a travel agency website?

Start with one page per trip, each with an itinerary, dates, a price and a way to hold a seat, then add an about page, a contact page and a trips index. Build the booking and payment path last, after the trip pages read well on a phone. The steps above give the order.

How much does a travel agency website cost?

The studio's indicative ranges are ₹9,999–₹24,999 for a landing page, ₹24,999–₹59,999 for a business website and ₹59,999–₹1,50,000 for an e-commerce store. These are a draft, exclusive of GST, and a fixed quote is confirmed in writing after a call. Booking and payments are add-ons in the calculator.

How can I create a travel agency website for free?

You can use a free builder or template, but we did not check any builder's plan terms, so confirm branding, domain and payment limits yourself. A free site suits testing one trek by enquiry. It will not give you seat counts or deposits.

What websites do travel agents use?

Our research does not measure which tools agents use, so no tool ranking is given here. The pages we read show agencies' own sites, a Ministry of Tourism directory of registered businesses and Virtuoso, a luxury advisor network.

Does a trek operator need online payments?

Not always: an operator with a few departures can take enquiries on WhatsApp and collect payment by hand. Online UPI or card deposits help once seats sell without a phone call. Confirm your gateway's refund and receipt features first.

How do I show a travel agency is legitimate?

Show your registered business name and address, matching your registration, plus real photos, a stated price and a cancellation rule. The NIDHI directory lists registered travel businesses with addresses. Beyond that, keep contact details visible and consistent.

Should a travel agency use a template?

A template is enough for a few fixed trips sold by enquiry. It is not enough once you need live seat counts, deposits or an album flow. Check what a template includes before you rely on it, since we read titles only.

How many pages does a trek operator website need?

Plan one page per trip plus a home page, a trips index, an about page and a contact page. The studio's business website tier covers 5 to 10 pages, so an operator with more trips should check the extra-page line in the cost calculator.

Can a travel website sell photo albums after the trip?

Yes, and Malnad Stories is the documented case: customers upload photos, design an album and pay online. The case study gives no sales figures. Build the flow only if you expect steady orders, or link out to an existing store.
