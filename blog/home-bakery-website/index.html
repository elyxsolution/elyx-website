Home Bakeries and Cloud Kitchens: Take Orders Online Without Aggregator Fees

A home bakery website for India: take orders by UPI and WhatsApp, publish delivery areas and compare the cost against aggregator commission.

A home bakery website takes orders without aggregator fees when one link does four jobs: it shows this week's menu and prices, says when and where you deliver or hand over, takes the payment and sends the order to you in one place. You then pay a flat plan or a payment fee, not a share of every order.

The honest qualification is that our sources are mostly US and UK tools priced in dollars, and none documents UPI, FSSAI rules or Indian aggregator commission rates. So the UPI, WhatsApp and delivery advice is my reading, built from the studio's own facts, and every rate in a worked example is an assumption to replace.

  • Commission. Compare the percentage an aggregator takes with the flat fee of your own link, using your own order statement.
  • Payment. Confirm UPI support with any tool or gateway before you build around it.
  • WhatsApp. Keep it for conversation, and let the website hold the menu, the rules and the payment.
  • Delivery. Publish areas, notice, fee and minimum order as plainly as your menu.

Aggregator Commission Versus Your Own Ordering Link

An aggregator takes a percentage of every order, while your own ordering link charges a flat plan or a payment fee, so the gap widens with each customer you win yourself. The studio's restaurants and cafés page is written for cloud-kitchen owners who want more direct orders and less dependence on aggregator apps. Its FAQ says a WhatsApp click-to-chat order takes a tap and pays no commission to a platform.

Our sources hold no Indian aggregator rates, so read your own partner statement for the current figure. They do show that percentage cuts exist among ordering tools. BakeProfit says Castiron took 10 percent while it takes 0, and Findhomegrown lists Hotplate at 5% plus $0.55 an order (29 September 2026).

This worked example uses assumed inputs: 40 orders a month at 1,000 rupees each, so 40,000 rupees of sales. The rates are not any platform's rate.

Assumed commissionCost on 40 orders (rupees)Left from 40,000 (rupees)
10%4,00036,000
20%8,00032,000
30%12,00028,000

Each cost is 40,000 multiplied by the rate. A flat plan stays the same at 40 orders or 80, and only the payment fee grows with sales.

An aggregator can still win on discovery, because strangers search there and a direct link does not bring them by itself. Findhomegrown says to plan on most orders coming from people you send to your own link, and Bakingsubs warns that a good site nobody visits earns nothing. My reading is to meet new customers on the aggregator and take repeat orders on your own link.

What a Home Bakery Website Needs to Do

A home bakery website needs to answer the four questions customers already text you: what is on offer, what it costs, when can I get it and how do I pay. Findhomegrown gives a simple test: if a customer can go from your link to a paid order with a pickup time without messaging you, the site works. Otherwise you have a menu, not an ordering page.

One scrolling page is enough for a few products. Findhomegrown reports that 89 of 184 bakery vendors on its platform list 4 to 10 products (29 September 2026), and Cornerrr says five or six things baked well beat a long list. Your bakery website home page should lead with the menu, the next bake day and one order button.

The studio's restaurants page warns against a PDF menu that takes ten seconds to open on 4G, so write the menu as text. These are the sections I would build:

  • Menu and prices. Photos, sizes as options and a price for each.
  • How to order. Notice needed, cut-off time and the order button.
  • Delivery and pickup. Areas, windows, fee and minimum.
  • Ingredients and allergens. The same details as on your label.
  • About and licence. Who bakes, your FSSAI number, hours and a map.
  • Custom cakes. A starting price, a lead time and a request form.

Leave off a blog you will not update, a gallery of every cake and a contact form as the only way to order, as Findhomegrown advises. BakeProfit describes a custom-cake form that collects date, budget and reference photos, so requests stop living in a DM thread.

Bakingsubs adds that the free plan shows Square branding. Every tool above is priced for US or UK sellers, so ask each whether it is offered in India and accepts UPI.

UPI Payments on a Home Bakery Website

UPI is the payment method most Indian customers will expect, yet none of the pages we read documents it, so confirm UPI support before you pick a tool. The studio's coaching page says it can add UPI and card payments through a payment gateway, with receipts. Its calculator lists UPI and card payments as a separate option.

This table is my framework for choosing how to collect money, not a sourced benchmark.

Way to collectChoose it whenWatch for
UPI QR sent on WhatsAppYou take a few orders a weekMatching payers to orders
Gateway checkout on the siteYou want orders paid before bakingFees, settlement time
Deposit, balance at handoverCustom or celebration cakesRefund terms
Pay at pickup or deliveryRegular, trusted customersNo-shows, wasted bakes

BakeProfit documents deposits: a percentage up front to hold the date, the rest on pickup. Ask a gateway these questions in writing:

  1. Does it accept UPI on a website checkout?
  2. What fee applies to each UPI and card payment?
  3. How many days until money reaches your bank?
  4. How are refunds handled?
  5. What receipt does the customer get?

This article gives no tax or payment-rule advice. Our sources do not cover UPI or merchant-payment rules, so check them with your gateway or bank, and ask a chartered accountant whether and how GST applies to your sales.

WhatsApp Orders and Catalogues

WhatsApp works best as the conversation channel that confirms orders, while the website holds the menu, the rules and the payment. The studio's restaurants page describes click-to-chat buttons with a pre-filled message, so ordering takes one tap. Pan i Bulka, a Valencia home bakery, takes orders online, on Instagram or by WhatsApp, then agrees the pickup time by message.

A Bangalore bakery in our results offers an order button and WhatsApp, and asks for 24 to 48 hours' notice. HomeBakers lists verified bakers who reply on WhatsApp with designs and prices, which shows how normal chat ordering is for Indian home bakers.

A WhatsApp catalogue is a menu that lives inside the chat app. Our sources do not describe its features or limits, so check Meta's current WhatsApp Business help. My reading is that a catalogue suits a handful of chat orders, and you should test whether it can enforce notice periods, daily limits and payment. Where it cannot, the website does that job.

Make the pre-filled message do the intake work. Ask it to carry:

  • the item and quantity
  • the date wanted
  • pickup or delivery
  • the area or PIN code
  • the customer's name

The calculator prices a WhatsApp lead flow as an option.

Delivery Areas, Pickup Windows and Lead Times

Publish delivery rules as precisely as you apply them in your kitchen: which areas, what notice, what fee, what minimum and which days. The top pages say little about delivery areas. Bakingsubs tips you to show yours, and Cornerrr says to state exactly when and where you deliver and how much notice you need.

RuleWhat to publishExample in sources
NoticeHours or days aheadPastries 4 hours, bread 2 days
Pickup windowDays and hoursDaily 11:00 to 21:00
Fee and minimumDelivery fee, minimum orderSet by the baker at checkout
Daily capOrders or items per dayItem sells out at its limit
Closed datesHolidays, weeks awayBlocked on the calendar

The examples come from Pan i Bulka and BakeProfit. The area rule is my reading, since the sources give no Indian examples: list the neighbourhoods or PIN codes you serve, and ask for the PIN code at checkout.

Choose pickup only when you bake alone and one handover point saves your day. Choose local delivery in named areas when repeat customers sit within a short ride. Pan i Bulka agrees the exact meeting spot by message and describes a landmark, not a house number, and Findhomegrown also covers keeping a home address private.

FSSAI, Labels and Google Maps: The Trust Details

Show your FSSAI licence number, your ingredients and allergens, your hours and a map, because a stranger checks these before paying a home kitchen. The studio's restaurants page says food businesses in India are expected to display their FSSAI licence number, and it places the number in the footer and on menu pages. Our sources do not say which licence or registration a home kitchen needs, so confirm that with FSSAI itself.

Cornerrr notes that rules for home food sellers vary by place and often ask for ingredients, allergens, a name and address and a home-kitchen statement. Findhomegrown reports that Texas requires label information on the website before payment. That is a US rule, but it is a reason to put label details on every product page.

For being found, Bakingsubs advises setting up a Google Business Profile and showing your location and delivery area. The studio's SEO page says the profile, reviews and consistent address details matter as much as the website for local trade.

What to Do Next

Start with one short menu, one order link and one way to pay, then add the rest after a month of real orders. These steps and their order are mine.

  1. Count. Note your orders, average order value and what each channel takes, over four weeks.
  2. Write the rules. Notice, cap, areas, fee, minimum and closed dates, on one page.
  3. Choose a route. Use the build table above and the five gateway questions.
  4. Test one order. Place a paid order yourself, from a phone on 4G.
  5. Share the link. Bio, WhatsApp status, Google Business Profile and boxes.

Copy this checklist before launch:

  • [ ] My menu is text, with prices and allergens.
  • [ ] My notice, daily cap and closed dates are published.
  • [ ] My delivery areas, fee and minimum are on the page.
  • [ ] I confirmed UPI, fees and settlement with my gateway.
  • [ ] My FSSAI number, hours and map are visible.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make a home bakery website?

Write your rules first, then pick a tool that shows the menu, takes payment and records the order. Put the menu, notice period, delivery areas and an order button on one page. Test a paid order from your phone before you share the link.

How do I start a home bakery online?

Start with a short menu, one way to order and one way to pay, and confirm your licence needs with FSSAI before you sell. Our sources do not cover Indian registration rules, so check them with the authority.

Do I need a website for my home bakery or can I just use social media?

You can start on social media, but a website takes the order and the payment, which a DM thread does not. Findhomegrown says a site that only shows the menu and says to DM to order leaves you taking orders by hand.

Can I take online orders through my home bakery website?

Yes, an ordering storefront, a builder or a custom build can take orders with a cart and a pickup or delivery date. Check each for notice rules, daily limits and payment. The studio quotes direct ordering after a call.

Can customers pay by UPI on a home bakery website?

Yes, if your payment gateway supports it, and the studio says it adds UPI and card payments through a gateway. None of the tool pages we read documents UPI, so ask in writing.

Can I use a WhatsApp catalogue instead of a website?

A catalogue can show your items, but our sources do not document its limits. Check whether it enforces notice periods, daily caps and payment. If it cannot, a website covers those and WhatsApp stays the place to confirm.

What pages should a home bakery website have?

It needs a menu with prices, how to order, delivery and pickup, ingredients and allergens, an about section with your FSSAI number, and contact details. Add a custom cake form if you take them.

How much should a home baker spend on a website?

A builder is the lower-cost start: Bakingsubs expects $0 to $30 a month, before payment fees. The studio's draft ranges are ₹9,999–₹24,999 for a landing page and ₹59,999–₹1,50,000 for a store, excluding GST. These are indicative, never a quote.

Is Square Online really free for home bakers?

Square Online has a free plan with no monthly fee, but it charges a fee on each online card payment and shows Square branding. Bakingsubs and Findhomegrown give different rates, so check Square's pricing page and confirm it is offered in India.
