Can Lawyers in India Have a Website? What Bar Council Rules Allow

An advocate website India's Bar Council rules permit: the particulars Rule 36 allows, what to leave out, a safe page structure and a template to copy.

Yes: the advocate website India allows is a factual information page, not a marketing site. Rule 36 of the Bar Council of India Rules bars soliciting work and advertising, and a 2008 proviso lets advocates publish website information limited to particulars set out in a Schedule. The qualification is that the proviso is conditional: Weps Technologies, citing a 2017 Lok Sabha answer, says the particulars must be furnished under intimation to, and as approved by, the Bar Council of India.

This is a business checklist for people building or commissioning a site, not legal advice. As of 16 November 2026 the research behind it held no copy of the Rule or the Schedule from the Bar Council itself, so every statement of their wording comes from law-firm and agency pages that reproduce them. Confirm the current text with your State Bar Council before you publish.

  • Allowed. Name, contact details, enrolment details, State Bar Council, bar association, qualifications and areas of practice, as the Schedule is summarised.
  • Not allowed. Testimonials, case results, "best" claims, paid promotion and "hire us now" buttons.
  • Approval. Ask your State Bar Council what intimation and approval it expects before launch.
  • Grey areas. Photos, courts, years of practice and articles are not in the summaries, and the sources treat them differently.

Advocate Website India: What Rule 36 Says and What the Proviso Changes

Rule 36 forbids an advocate to solicit work or advertise, directly or indirectly, and the website proviso is a narrow exception to that ban. B.K. Singh & Associates, a Delhi NCR firm, says the Rule names circulars, advertisements, touts, personal approaches, interviews not warranted by personal relations, newspaper comments and photographs published in connection with cases. Weps Technologies places it in Part VI, Chapter II, Section IV of the Bar Council of India Rules, framed under the Advocates Act, 1961.

The Rule binds the advocate personally and anything done on the advocate's behalf, according to B.K. Singh. That includes an agency or a social media page, so a studio building the site stands in the advocate's shoes. Under section 35 of the Act, a State Bar Council can send a misconduct complaint to its disciplinary committee, which may reprimand, suspend or remove a name from the roll, and a Rule 36 breach can amount to misconduct.

The table sets three sources side by side by how each describes the proviso, as read on 16 November 2026.

SourceOn the provisoIts emphasis
B.K. Singh & AssociatesAllowed within the permitted particularsGoing beyond is a breach
Weps TechnologiesAllowed for Schedule particulars onlyIntimation and approval needed
DVM TechnoAllowed for Schedule particulars onlyExtra input is deemed a violation

All three are practitioners, not the regulator. Weps adds that commentary dates the proviso to 2008 and links it to the Supreme Court case V.B. Joshi v. Union of India, so check dated sources for any wording you rely on.

What an Advocate Website May Carry: The Permitted Particulars

Summaries of the Schedule list six kinds of particulars, and a site built on them is the safest starting point. DVM Techno, relying on an iPleaders summary, gives this list:

  • Contact. Name, address, telephone numbers and email ID.
  • Enrolment. Enrolment number and date of enrolment.
  • Councils. The State Bar Council of original enrolment and the one on whose roll the name now stands.
  • Association. The bar association of which you are a member.
  • Qualifications. Professional and academic qualifications.
  • Practice. Areas of practice, stated plainly and without superlatives.

Weps Technologies adds a seventh item: a declaration, tied to the Schedule, that the information is true. These are summaries, so read the complete current Schedule wording yourself.

What an Advocate Website Must Leave Out

Leave out anything that praises, compares, promises or invites work, because the sources treat each as advertising or solicitation. The table below combines DVM Techno's list with B.K. Singh's own practice and Weps' warning, and marks each item as no or avoid.

ElementOn the site?Reason given
Client testimonials, star ratingsNoSelf-promotion through others
Case wins, success rates, countersNoPublicity about cases is what Rule 36 targets
"Best", "top", "No. 1", "expert"NoAdvertising and comparison
"Free consultation", "Hire now" buttonsNoSoliciting language
Photos with clients or at case winsNoCase photographs are named in the Rule
Paid ads and paid listingsAvoidAdvertising; Madras High Court, 2024
Google reviews copied to the siteAvoidNot a listed particular
Fees, discounts, offersAvoidB.K. Singh agrees fees at a meeting only

Weps adds that Rule 36 does not name each of these items. The risk comes from the general ban, and calling a site informational does not settle the question. Many templates built for overseas law firms carry these features, so strip them before launch, as DVM Techno advises.

Photos, Courts, Years of Practice and Articles: Where Sources Differ

Four common items sit outside the Schedule summaries, and the sources handle them differently. The table shows what each source does, as read on 16 November 2026.

ItemIn the Schedule summaries?What the sources do
Portrait photoNoB.K. Singh shows portraits; DVM says ask the Bar Council first
Courts you appear inNoDVM and B.K. Singh both name courts; Weps is silent
Years of practiceNoB.K. Singh states them; the summaries do not list them
Short legal articlesNoA reported July 2026 circular allows factual awareness content

On articles, Weps reports, citing SCC Online of 18 July 2026, a Bar Council circular dated 17 July 2026 that advises against promotional content on social media while allowing factual legal-awareness content. I have not read the circular, so check it before relying on it.

My reading, not a sourced finding: one firm publishing an item is not approval of it. If an item is not in the Schedule, ask your State Bar Council in writing and keep the reply. Copying a large firm's homepage is no safer, because one top-ranking firm's homepage, read on 16 November 2026, shows an awards section and a feed of mandates, and I do not know what approval it holds.

Intimation and Approval: The Step Most Templates Skip

Weps Technologies says the website proviso covers the prescribed particulars only when they are furnished under intimation to, and as approved by, the Bar Council of India. It cites Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 1583 of 27 December 2017, which records that wording. DVM Techno quotes the same condition, while B.K. Singh's disclaimer does not mention it.

None of the three sources describes the procedure: the form, the fee, or whether the State Bar Council or the Bar Council of India receives the intimation. The research found no official page that does. So the practical advice is a short routine, drawn from Weps' own "before you publish" list:

  1. Draft. Write the exact content you want live, particular by particular.
  2. Ask. Ask your State Bar Council how intimation is made and who approves.
  3. Record. Keep the approved version and every reply.
  4. Recheck. Review the content again whenever you change it.

Enforcement has tightened, according to Weps. In July 2024 the Madras High Court, in P.N. Vignesh v. Bar Council of India, upheld the restriction on lawyer advertising, and the Bar Council then issued directions to State Bar Councils that cover online platforms; Weps has not verified the status of Sulekha.com's Supreme Court appeal. A Bar Council press release of 17 March 2025 also condemned unethical legal advertising, including by so-called legal influencers.

Disclaimer Page: Useful, Not a Shield

A disclaimer helps the reader understand what the site is, but it does not make a site compliant. Weps Technologies is direct that no disclaimer, consent popup or template substitutes for the Rules or the approval process. DVM Techno found no rule requiring one, though most Indian law-firm sites carry one.

B.K. Singh's site shows what a full version covers. It asks the visitor to confirm they came of their own accord, says the content is general information and not advice, and says no advocate-client relationship starts until terms are agreed in writing and, for court matters, a vakalatnama is signed. It also promises no result.

DVM Techno's build rules: a few plain sentences, a real accessible button, the choice remembered after acceptance, and an overlay so the page underneath can still be indexed.

Top Advocate in India Searches, Directories and Getting Found

An advocate cannot win "top advocate in India" or "best advocate in India" searches by claiming the title, because the sources treat "best", "top" and "No. 1" as advertising and comparison. Those searches are answered by third-party pages. The research did not cover who compiles such lists, the types of advocate, or any all-India advocate list, so this article says nothing about them.

Directories are a separate question. AdvocateKhoj, read on 16 November 2026, describes itself as a network of advocates across 850+ locations with a case-post system for growing a practice. In P.N. Vignesh, per DVM Techno, the Madras High Court held that advocates soliciting work through online listing platforms breached the rules. So joining a lead-selling listing is a question for your Bar Council, not a web-design choice.

A November 2025 thread on r/LegalAdviceIndia asked whether there is a Practo for lawyers. Doctors' listings are covered in the website for doctors in India article, but my reading is that the Rule 36 sources here point the other way for advocates.

What the sources do support is accurate local presence. DVM Techno says Google's guidelines treat lawyers as individual practitioners who may hold a profile if they are public-facing and reachable at the verified address during stated hours. Use your real name only, with no keywords or taglines, do not ask for or copy reviews, and skip a page for every locality. Elyx has a free Google Business Profile checker for the details.

A Compliant Template You Can Copy

Use this template as a drafting aid: every field is a particular the sources list, and every bracket is yours to fill and verify. It is not approved by any Bar Council. The wording in the last block is mine.

Profile block

  • Name as enrolled: [full name]
  • Address, telephone, email: [chamber or office]
  • Enrolment number and date: [number], [date]
  • State Bar Council: [original], [current roll]
  • Bar association: [name]
  • Qualifications: [degrees and institutions]
  • Areas of practice: [plain list, no ranking words]
  • Declaration: [wording your Bar Council requires]

Disclaimer outline

  • You are visiting of your own accord; nobody invited you.
  • The page is general information, not advice on your facts.
  • Reading it or writing in creates no advocate-client relationship.
  • No outcome is promised.
  • Rules and the law change, so confirm before you rely on anything.

Contact form. Ask for name, phone and the type of matter, never the facts of the case, as DVM Techno advises. Add a privacy note, because B.K. Singh and DVM Techno both say the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules were notified in November 2025 with a phased start. The research held no text of the Act, so take advice on your duties.

Before you publish

  • Every particular matches your enrolment records.
  • Nothing praises, compares, promises or invites work.
  • No fees, offers, testimonials, reviews, results or client names.
  • Photos, courts and years are confirmed in writing or left out.
  • The approved version is saved, with its date.
  • No chat widget, ad pixel or remarketing tag, as DVM Techno advises.

Frequently asked questions

Can a lawyer in India have a website?

Yes, an advocate may have a website limited to the particulars the Bar Council of India Schedule permits. Rule 36 still bars advertising and soliciting, so the site must be a factual information page. Check current text with your State Bar Council.

Can advocates advertise in India?

No, Rule 36 bars an advocate from soliciting work or advertising, directly or indirectly. The website proviso is a narrow exception. Weps Technologies and DVM Techno both treat paid ads and online promotion as risks.

What can an advocate put on a website in India?

Summaries of the Schedule list name, contact details, enrolment number and date, State Bar Councils, bar association, qualifications and areas of practice. Anything beyond that is treated as a risk. Read the full current Schedule yourself.

Can an advocate show client testimonials or Google reviews on a website?

It is safest not to. Testimonials and ratings are not among the listed particulars, and DVM Techno and Weps Technologies both say they risk being treated as self-promotion. Do not ask clients for reviews either.

Does an advocate website need a disclaimer?

DVM Techno found no rule requiring one, but it is common practice on Indian law-firm sites. A disclaimer records that the visitor came of their own accord and that the page is not advice. Weps Technologies says it never replaces approval.

Is there a Practo for lawyers in India?

Directories exist, such as AdvocateKhoj, but joining one that sells leads is a Rule 36 question. DVM Techno reports a 2024 Madras High Court holding against soliciting work through online listing platforms. Ask your Bar Council before joining.

Does an advocate need Bar Council approval before launching a website?

According to Weps Technologies, the proviso covers the particulars only when furnished under intimation to, and as approved by, the Bar Council of India. The research found no procedure, so ask your State Bar Council what to file.

How much does an advocate website cost in India?

On Elyx's draft, indicative price data, a landing page is ₹9,999–₹24,999 and a business website is ₹24,999–₹59,999, excluding GST. A fixed quote is confirmed in writing after a call.

How do I become an advocate in India?

Advocates are enrolled on the roll of a State Bar Council, as the Schedule's enrolment particulars imply. The research did not cover eligibility or enrolment steps, so this article does not describe them. Ask the Bar Council of India or your State Bar Council.
