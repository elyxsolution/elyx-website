School and Preschool Websites Parents Trust at Admission Time

School website design for admission season: what the admissions page, fee table, safety information, gallery and enquiry form should contain.

School website design works at admission time when a parent can find the admission steps, the fees, the safety arrangements and a way to ask a question within a minute of landing on the site. The honest qualification is that the research behind this article is thin: the results are galleries, template pages and lists of example sites, and the one list we could read covers private schools in the US, UK, France and Switzerland. None of it covers fees, safety or enquiry forms, so the advice below is my reading, built from the studio's own facts for clinics and coaching centres, and every figure is either dated and sourced or labelled as an assumption.

  • Admissions page. One page that lists steps, dates and documents, and ends in one action.
  • Fees. A table a parent can add up, dated for the academic year, never a scanned circular.
  • Safety. Publish only what the school can show proof of, and keep the proof on file.
  • Enquiry form. Few fields, a WhatsApp button and a stated reply time, because the form collects a child's details.

The Admissions Page: Steps, Dates and Documents

An admissions page should let a parent see in one scroll who can apply, how, by when and with which documents, and then offer one action. The studio's coaching page makes the matching point for demo classes: show the next step plainly and filter out wrong-fit leads. Build it around the questions a parent has before a first call:

Parent's questionWhat the page showsWho supplies it
Is my child eligible?Classes offered and the age rule for eachAdmissions office
What happens next?Numbered steps from enquiry to offerAdmissions office
By when?Dates for this academic yearAdmissions office
What do I bring?A document checklistAdmissions office
Whom do I ask?One phone number and one WhatsApp numberPrincipal's office

I have no cited source for any board's or state's age rules or admission calendar, and they differ between schools. Take every date and eligibility rule from your board and your admissions office, and put the academic year in the page heading so an old page cannot pass for a new one.

Fee Structure: Publish a Table a Parent Can Add Up

A fee page earns trust when a parent can total the first year without phoning the office. The studio's coaching page says showing fees and schedules plainly builds trust and filters out wrong-fit leads, and its clinic page says answering "how much does it cost" on the page saves calls. Those are the studio's observations for coaching centres and clinics, not school data, but the logic carries over.

Use a text table, not a scanned circular. The studio's restaurant page warns that a PDF menu can take ten seconds to open on 4G, and a PDF fee sheet has the same problem and cannot be edited by your staff.

Worked Example: Adding Up the First Year

The first-year cost is a sum, so show it as one. Using letters for your own figures: first-year payable = registration (R) + admission fee (A) + tuition per term (T) × number of terms (n) + transport per term (X) × n + uniform and books (U).

For a school with three terms and transport, that is R + A + 3T + 3X + U. Print this total on the page, and state beside it which parts return to the family if the child does not join. Add a line saying which academic year the fees apply to and when the table was last updated, and change it every year.

Safety Information: What to Publish and What to Keep on File

Safety information on a school website should state only arrangements the school can prove, and each statement should point to a document the admissions office can show on request. The research package has no source on which safety measures any Indian board or state requires, so I do not state requirements. Ask your board and education department what they expect to see.

This is my checklist of topics parents commonly want addressed, to be published only where true:

TopicWhat to publishProof to keep
AffiliationBoard name and affiliation detailsThe affiliation letter
TransportRoute handling, pick-up and drop rulesVehicle and driver records
VisitorsHow a visitor is checked inThe written policy
StaffHow staff are vettedThe hiring policy
HealthFirst-aid arrangements, emergency contactNurse or doctor details
ComplaintsWho a parent contactsA named person and number

A vague claim such as "a safe campus" does less than a specific arrangement with a date. The studio's coaching page makes the same point about results: claims should be provable.

Photo Galleries: Show the Place Without Exposing the Children

A school gallery should show the classrooms, grounds, labs and routines a parent will actually meet, using real photographs. The studio's dental case study built a gallery of real photography so nervous first-time patients know what to expect. A parent on a first visit to a school's site is nervous in the same way.

My reading is that three rules matter more than the layout:

  • Consent. Get written permission from parents before a child's photo goes online, and keep the form.
  • Names. Do not caption a child with a full name. A class and an activity are enough.
  • Weight. Compress images so the page loads on 4G. UBIQ Education recommends videos and virtual tours, and those suit a school with fast hosting and time to maintain them.

Enquiry Forms: Few Fields, WhatsApp and a Reply Time

An enquiry form should ask for the least you need to call back, and sit beside a WhatsApp button, because the studio's coaching page recommends exactly that pairing above the fold. This is also the part where the site handles a child's details, so keep it short and say what happens next.

A copyable field list for the first form:

  • [ ] Parent's name
  • [ ] Mobile number, with a tick for WhatsApp
  • [ ] Child's first name
  • [ ] Class applying for and academic year
  • [ ] Preferred time for a call or visit
  • [ ] A line saying who will reply and by when

Leave date of birth, documents and medical details for the later admission form. The studio's coaching page says it sets up GA4 events for form submissions, WhatsApp clicks and calls. For a school, an extra step beats analytics: ask every family on the first call how they found you, and write it down.

The studio's CA-firm page says it can add a privacy notice that reflects India's DPDP Act to a contact form. This article does not interpret that law and the research contains no primary text of it. Ask a lawyer what consent and notice a form that collects a child's details needs.

Preschools: Book a Visit, Not Just a Form

For a preschool, the main action is a booked visit, because parents of very young children usually want to see the room and meet the teacher before they decide. This is my reading, drawn from the nervous-first-visit logic in the studio's clinic case. Give the visit its own button, list visiting hours, show a typical day as a short timetable, and state the pick-up routine.

School Website Templates, Figma Files and HTML: When They Are Enough

A template or Figma file is enough for a simple information site, and not enough once fees, admission steps and enquiries need to be edited every year by non-developers. The GitHub description of one HTML template, built with Bootstrap, lists sections for announcements, events, faculty, courses, gallery and contact. It lists no fees, admission steps or safety page, which fits the pattern above: a template mirrors a general school, not an admission decision. Finalsite and Greenhouse School Websites also sell school templates, and Figma Community hosts free school designs.

OptionGives youChoose it when

A Figma design is a picture, so it still needs building before it can take a form submission. Whichever route you take, register the domain and hosting in the school's own name, so a change of developer never puts the site out of reach.

What a School Website Costs, in Indicative Ranges

A school website with admission, fee, safety, gallery and enquiry pages fits the studio's business-website range, and a single admissions landing page fits the smaller one. These figures come from this site's price data, which is a draft: every range is indicative, never a quote, and a fixed quote is confirmed in writing after a call.

  • Landing page. One admissions page with an enquiry form and WhatsApp button: ₹9,999–₹24,999, usually 1–2 weeks.
  • Business website. Up to 10 pages with a CMS and Google Maps: ₹24,999–₹59,999, usually 2–4 weeks.

Ranges exclude 18% GST. The price data also lists multiple languages, online booking and writing the copy as separate options, so list what you need before the call. Kannada or Hindi versions of the fee and admission pages are the ones to price first, in my reading. The pricing page lists every range and the website cost calculator shows the options.

Plan the Build Backwards From Admission Season

Plan the build from the day admissions open, with a margin, because a site that launches mid-season misses the weeks it was built for. The plan behind this article assumes admission season returns every year, but our sources contain no calendar or demand data, so take your dates from your own admissions office and enquiry log.

The studio's web development page lists discovery, a scoped proposal, design, build with two feedback rounds, then QA, staging and launch. A website is typically 2–4 weeks and a landing page 1–2 weeks, indicative and never promised dates.

Your side takes time too: approve the fee figures, take consented photos, check each safety statement against its proof and confirm admission dates with the board and your office.

What to Do Next

Start with the fee table and the admissions page, then add safety, gallery and the enquiry form. Work through this list:

  • [ ] I wrote the first-year fee total and what is refundable.
  • [ ] My admissions page shows steps, dates and documents for this year.
  • [ ] Each safety statement has a document behind it.
  • [ ] I have written consent for every child's photo.
  • [ ] My enquiry form has six fields and a WhatsApp button.
  • [ ] A lawyer has checked the form's consent and notice.
  • [ ] The domain and hosting are in the school's name.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to build a school website?

A school website's indicative range is ₹9,999–₹24,999 for an admissions landing page or ₹24,999–₹59,999 for a business site of up to 10 pages, excluding 18% GST. Options such as multiple languages and online booking are priced separately. These are draft ranges, never a quote, and a fixed quote is confirmed in writing after a call.

How to design a school website?

Start with what a parent needs to decide, then design around it: admissions steps, fees, safety information, a gallery and an enquiry form. List the content first, because the layout follows from it.

How to make a website for a school?

Gather the fee table, admission dates, safety documents and consented photos, then choose a template or a custom build. Register the domain in the school's name, and set up a way for staff to edit fees and dates. Launch before admissions open.

What should a school website include?

A school website should include an admissions page, a dated fee table, safety information, a photo gallery, an enquiry form with a WhatsApp number, a map and contact details. Add faculty and events pages only if you can keep them current.

What is the best school website design?

There is no single best design, because the lists that rank schools judge visuals and interactivity. UBIQ Education's 2026 list, for example, features private schools abroad and none in India. Judge a design by whether a parent can find fees, steps and a contact in a minute.

Are free school website templates good enough?

Free templates are good enough for a simple information site and weak on fees, admission steps and safety. One GitHub template lists announcements, events, faculty, courses, gallery and contact, but no fees page. Use one when someone on staff can edit the code.

Do I need to code a Figma design?

Yes, a Figma file is a design, not a working site, so someone must build it before it can take a form submission or show an editable fee table. Budget for the build as well as the design.

Is a driving school website different?

Yes, a driving school website serves adult learners and sells lessons, so it needs lesson packages, licence-test details and a booking flow, not admission steps. This article covers schools for children.
