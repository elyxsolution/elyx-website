An AI chatbot for a website helps when it answers repeat questions correctly from content you approved and hands everything else to a person. It annoys customers when it guesses, traps them in a loop or hides the human. The honest qualification is that my research holds vendor pages with large claims and no independent survey of how visitors feel, so the rules below come from how vendors describe their own tools plus my reading, and you should test any chatbot on your real questions before trusting a percentage.

  • Evidence. Every conversion figure I found comes from a company selling chatbots, and none states a method.
  • Helps. Repeat factual questions, after-hours replies, lead capture and booking are the jobs that fit.
  • Hurts. Wrong answers, dead ends, no route to a person and the wrong language cause most of the annoyance.
  • Test. Run one for 14 days on real traffic, with a switch-off rule written before you start.

What Is an AI Chatbot for a Website, and How Is It Different From a Rule-Based Bot?

An AI chatbot for a website is a chat window that reads your pages and documents and writes an answer to a visitor's question in the visitor's own words. Google Cloud describes the difference plainly: non-AI chatbots follow scripted dialogue and cannot produce an answer that was not pre-programmed, while AI chatbots use large language models (LLMs, the software behind modern chat assistants) to generate replies.

Inletbase says older scripted bots only worked when a visitor typed the expected wording, and blames that for much of the past frustration with chatbots. Google Cloud also describes retrieval augmented generation (RAG): the bot searches your documents when a question arrives, which is the idea behind "learns from your website".

A chatbot replies when asked; an agent carries out tasks, which What Are AI Agents? covers.

When an AI Chatbot Helps: Four Jobs That Fit

A chatbot helps most where the question repeats, the answer is already written down and a wrong answer is cheap to fix. Four jobs meet that test in the sources:

  1. Repeat factual questions. Inletbase lists pricing, hours, shipping, returns and product details. The studio's own chatbot page makes the same point: most customer messages are the same handful of questions.
  2. After-hours replies. ChatBot.com's page describes a customer pairing live chat in business hours with an AI agent after hours.
  3. Lead capture. Inletbase describes a bot asking for a name or email partway through a chat, and NextLevel describes scoring leads and sending them to a CRM (customer relationship management tool).
  4. Booking and handover. NextLevel describes checking calendar availability and booking, and both it and ChatBot.com describe passing a hot lead to a person with the conversation attached.

My reading of "converts": a chatbot converts when it removes a step between a question and an enquiry, such as the visitor no longer waiting until morning for a fee. It does not create demand on its own.

Elyx Solutions builds AI chatbots on that principle: answers come only from information you approve, the bot says when it is not sure, and sensitive chats pass to your team with the details already collected.

Should Your Website Have a Chatbot? A Decision Table

Add a chatbot when your inbox holds the same ten questions and you already have written answers, and wait when enquiries are few or the questions are one-off. The rules below are my framework, not a sourced benchmark.

Your situationBetter choice
The same questions arrive daily and answers are written downAn AI chatbot trained on approved content
Only a few fixed questions, such as hours and addressA clear FAQ section. Inletbase says rule-based bots still suit very small sites.
Visitors mainly want to bookA booking page or a WhatsApp button. The Ashirwad Dental case lets patients start a booking in one tap.
You need exact details, such as payments or documentsA form. Inletbase says forms stay better for this, and the two work together.
The topic is health, legal or tax adviceLogistics only (hours, location, booking), with a human for advice
Few visitors, a slow or outdated siteFix the site first, using signs a site needs a redesign and real build timelines

How to Add an AI Chatbot to Your Website, Step by Step

You add an AI chatbot by listing your questions, giving it your content, installing one snippet or plugin, testing it and then reading real chats. The vendor sources agree on the middle steps, and the first and last are my additions.

  1. List 20 real questions. Pull them from your WhatsApp, inbox and call notes.
  2. Gather the content. Inletbase names website pages, a PDF price list, an FAQ document and terms and conditions as the usual material.
  3. Connect it. Use a plugin if your platform has one. Otherwise paste the short code once, and the chat bubble loads on every page, as Inletbase and ChatBot.com describe.
  4. Test it yourself. Ask the 20 questions, plus a few trick ones such as a price you do not list, and fix every wrong answer.
  5. Set the handover and a consent notice. ChatBot.com describes options for obtaining user consent and linking your policies.
  6. Watch real chats. Inletbase and NextLevel both describe reading what visitors asked to find gaps.

The vendors say setup takes minutes, which I did not test. NextLevel's trial includes an agent page link as well as an install script, so a hosted chat page you link to, instead of embedding, exists at least there.

The Indian Context: WhatsApp, Hindi and Kannada, and Customer Data

In India the chatbot question is often a WhatsApp question, because that is where many customers already message businesses. None of the three vendor pages I read names WhatsApp in the text I extracted, so confirm it before paying. WhatsApp has its own rules, as the studio's service pages summarise them: customers must have agreed to receive messages, and business-started messages use approved templates.

On language, test Hindi or Kannada with real phrasing, not translations of your English FAQ, because only real messages show where a bot struggles.

On data, a chat that asks for a name and phone number collects personal data. ChatBot.com describes consent options, while NextLevel cites GDPR, CCPA and SOC 2. I did not research India's rules for this page, so ask your advisor what applies before you collect numbers.

A 14-Day Test: How to Tell Whether a Chatbot Helps Your Business

Run the chatbot for 14 days on real traffic, then judge it on four counts, because two of the three vendors I read advertise 14-day trials. The measures are my suggestion, not an industry standard.

  • Wrong answers. Count any wrong price, timing or policy. My rule: one that stays unfixed after a day means narrowing or switching the bot off.
  • Handover share. The share of chats passed to a person. A very high share means the content is thin.
  • Unanswered questions. List what it could not answer, and add those answers to the site.
  • Enquiries per 100 visitors. Compare the 14 days with the previous 14 across form, WhatsApp and calls.

Then check whether it pays in time. Say a front desk answers 30 repeat questions a day at 2 minutes each: 30 × 2 = 60 minutes a day, and over 26 working days that is 1,560 minutes, or 26 hours. If the bot fully handles half, you save 13 hours. At Inletbase's claimed 80%, it would be 20.8 hours, but treat that as an upper bound until your own chats show it.

What to Do Next

Start with the list of 20 questions, because it decides whether you need a chatbot at all. Then work through this checklist:

  • [ ] I listed the 20 questions customers ask most.
  • [ ] I wrote the answers down in one place and checked them.
  • [ ] I decided what the bot must never answer, such as medical, legal or tax advice.
  • [ ] I chose a handover route to a person.
  • [ ] I tested Hindi or Kannada phrasing if my customers use it.
  • [ ] I set the 14-day measures and a switch-off rule before launch.

Frequently asked questions

How do I add an AI chatbot to my website?

Give the tool your site address or documents, then install its WordPress or Framer plugin or paste its short code snippet. Test it with your own questions before going live. The step-by-step section above has the full sequence.

How do I build an AI chatbot for my website?

You can build one yourself on a cloud platform such as Google Cloud's Conversational Agents, which needs a developer, or use a hosted tool that builds it from your content. A studio can also build and test it for you. The setup options table compares the routes.

What can an AI chatbot do on a website?

It can answer questions about pricing, hours, shipping and returns, collect a visitor's details and book appointments, according to Inletbase and NextLevel. It can also pass a chat to a person. It should say when it does not know.

Is a chatbot an AI?

Not always. Google Cloud says non-AI chatbots use scripted dialogue, while AI chatbots use large language models to generate replies.

Is there a free AI chatbot for a website?

There are free trials and limited free plans. ChatBot.com and NextLevel advertise 14-day trials, and Inletbase advertises a free plan, all as of 20 November 2026. Check the limits first.

Can I add a chatbot without embedding it in my page?

Sometimes. NextLevel says its trial includes an agent page link alongside an install script, so you can link to a hosted chat page. I did not verify this for other vendors.

Will an AI chatbot make things up?

It can. Even a consumer chat app in my research warns that AI can make mistakes, so limit the bot to approved content, let it say it is not sure and test it with real questions before launch.

Does a chatbot replace my team?

No. It handles repeat questions so your team can spend time on conversations that need a person, and it should pass those over with the details collected.

Can an AI chatbot work on WhatsApp?

Yes, when it is connected to the WhatsApp Business Platform. WhatsApp requires customer consent and approved templates for business-started messages. The three hosted vendors I read do not name WhatsApp in the text I extracted, so ask them directly.
