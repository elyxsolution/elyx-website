Gym and Fitness Studio Websites: Trials, Memberships and Class Schedules

Gym website design for India: how to build a free-trial page, take membership payments, show a class timetable and write trainer pages.

Gym website design works when one page takes a stranger from a Google search or an Instagram ad to a booked free trial, and the rest of the site answers what stops them joining: what a membership costs, when classes run and who the trainers are. The honest qualification is that the sources behind this article cover templates and design galleries, and contain no figures on trial conversion, membership payments or seasonal demand. So every number below is a worked example with stated assumptions, and the advice is my reading, built from the studio's own facts for clinics, coaching centres and salons.

  • Free trial. Treat it as a funnel with six steps, because the visitor can be lost at any of them.
  • Payments. Put plan purchase online first, and confirm recurring autopay with your gateway before you promise it.
  • Timetable. Publish it as text your team can edit, never as a PDF or an image.
  • Trainers. Give each trainer a short page with proof you can show, since a first-timer is choosing a person as much as a gym.

Free-Trial Funnel: From First Tap to First Visit

A free-trial funnel is the shortest path from "I might try this gym" to a named person at your front desk on a set day. The studio's coaching page makes the same point for demo classes: a short form and a WhatsApp button above the fold, and for ad traffic a dedicated landing page that does one thing only.

This is my sequence, built from those facts:

  1. Entry. Google Maps listing, Instagram bio link or an ad, each pointing to the trial page and not the home page.
  2. Landing page. One offer, the price of a membership visible, real photos of your floor, timings, and two buttons: book a trial and WhatsApp.
  3. Form. Name, mobile number and preferred slot. Every extra field costs sign-ups.
  4. Confirmation. An instant WhatsApp or SMS with the slot, address, map link and what to bring.
  5. Reminder. A message the day before. The salon booking article covers what vendors claim about reminders and why the only figure is unproven.
  6. Follow-up. A same-day message after the visit with the membership page link.

The studio's coaching page says it sets up GA4 events for form submissions, WhatsApp clicks and calls, tagged by page and campaign. Add a front-desk tally of trials booked, attended and joined, because the site cannot see who walked in.

Worked Example: Where the Funnel Leaks

A funnel is a chain of multiplications, so a small gain at any step lifts the final number. Every input here is assumed, so replace each with your own count.

  • 1,000 visits to the trial page.
  • 5 percent book a trial: 1,000 × 0.05 = 50 bookings.
  • 60 percent attend: 50 × 0.60 = 30 visits.
  • 30 percent of attendees join: 30 × 0.30 = 9 members.

If a reminder lifted attendance from 60 to 70 percent, 50 × 0.70 = 35 visits and 35 × 0.30 = 10.5 members. That is 1.5 more per 1,000 visits. Divide your ad spend by the members, not the form fills, to see what a joiner really costs.

Membership Payments: What to Put Online and What Not To

Put plan purchase online first, because it is simple to build and to explain, and leave recurring autopay to your gym software or gateway until you have confirmed what they support. The studio's coaching page says it can add UPI and card payments through a payment gateway, with receipts. Its SaaS page lists subscriptions, trials and failed-payment handling as part of a product build, which is a much larger job than a payment button.

Where payment happensChoose it whenWatch for
Front desk onlyYou are starting outNothing online to convert on
Gateway checkout on the sitePlans are fixed and prepaidRefund and freeze terms
Your gym software's payment pageIt already bills membersVisitors leave your site
Recurring autopayMonthly billing is your modelFailed payments, consent rules

Show a plan table on the page: plan length, price, joining fee, whether the price includes tax, freeze rule and cancellation rule. My reading is that unclear terms cost more sign-ups than a higher price does.

This article gives no tax or payment-rule advice. Our sources do not cover RBI rules for recurring payments, so confirm autopay with your gateway. Ask a chartered accountant how GST applies to memberships and what your invoice must show.

Class Timetable: Three Ways to Show It

A class timetable should be text on the page that your team can change without a developer, because timetables change more often than any other page. The studio's coaching page says it adds a simple CMS so batch dates and fees can be edited without a developer, and its restaurant page warns against a PDF menu that takes ten seconds to open on 4G. A PDF timetable has the same problem.

ApproachChoose it whenWatch for
Text table edited in a CMSClasses are fixed weeklyNobody updates it
Embedded booking toolMembers must reserve a spotTool cost, tool look
Custom class bookingCapacity and member logins matterLongest build

For each class, store the same fields: name, day, start time, length, level, trainer, spots and how to book. On a phone, show one day at a time with day tabs, and print "last updated" near the top so members trust it. The calculator lists online booking as a separate option, and a custom build with logins is closest to what the studio prices as a web app.

Trainer Pages That Help a Nervous First-Timer

A trainer page should show who the person is, what they teach and what they can prove, because a first-timer is deciding whom to walk in and meet. The studio's dental page makes a parallel case: real photos and credentials turn a nervous visitor into a booking. My reading is that the same holds for a gym floor.

Each page needs:

  • Real photo. The trainer on your own floor, not stock.
  • Speciality. Strength, weight loss, yoga, rehabilitation or batch classes, in plain words.
  • Proof. Certificates you can show on request, with the awarding body named.
  • Languages. English, Kannada or Hindi, if you coach in them.
  • Classes. Links to their timetable slots and one button to message them on WhatsApp.

Keep claims to what you can prove, and avoid promised results. I have no cited rule on fitness advertising, so ask your lawyer or association before publishing before-and-after photos or health claims.

Timing the Build Around the New-Year and Summer Rush

Plan the build backwards from the busiest weeks, with a margin, because a site that goes live mid-rush misses the traffic it was built for. The plan behind this article assumes demand spikes every new year and summer. Our sources contain no trend data, so check your own enquiry log and Search Console for the months your searches rise.

The studio's process runs as follows, as stated on its web development page: discovery call, Day 1 to 2; scoped proposal, Day 2 to 3; design, Week 1; build and feedback, Week 1 to 3; QA, staging and launch, final week. A website is typically 2–4 weeks and a landing page 1–2 weeks, and these are indicative, never promised dates.

Count back from your target date:

  • Launch date. Choose it before the first busy week, not during it.
  • Final week. Staging review and fixes.
  • Build. The weeks above, with two feedback rounds.
  • Your side, before kickoff. Photos, trainer bios, plan prices and the timetable.

The Launch tier includes 30 days of post-launch support, which covers the first busy month. Update your Google Business Profile hours before the rush too.

What to Do Next

Start with the trial page and a published timetable, then add payments and trainer pages. Work through this checklist:

  • [ ] I counted trials booked, attended and joined for four weeks.
  • [ ] My trial form has three fields and a WhatsApp button.
  • [ ] My plan table shows length, price, tax, freeze and cancellation terms.
  • [ ] My timetable is text, with one named owner.
  • [ ] Each trainer has a real photo and proof I can show.
  • [ ] I confirmed autopay and GST questions with my gateway and accountant.
  • [ ] I picked a launch date before the first busy week.

Frequently asked questions

What makes good gym website design?

Good gym website design gets a visitor to a booked trial in a few taps and answers three questions on the page: what it costs, when classes run and who coaches. Looks matter less than those answers. Add real photos of your own floor and fast loading on a phone.

How do I design a website for fitness?

Start with the one action you want, usually a free trial, then build the plan table, timetable and trainer pages around it. List your classes and prices first, then choose a template or a custom build. Test the page on a phone before launch.

What should a gym website include?

A gym website should include a trial booking form, a WhatsApp button, plan prices, a class timetable, trainer pages, photos, opening hours and a map. The studio's other industry pages add Google Maps and local search details. Add a policy page for freezes and cancellation.

How does a free-trial funnel work for a gym?

A free-trial funnel moves a visitor from a landing page to a booked slot, a visit and a membership offer. Each step has a confirmation or follow-up message. Count bookings, attendance and joins separately.

Can members pay for a gym membership online in India?

Yes, a gateway can take UPI and card payments for plans, and the studio says it adds these with receipts. Recurring autopay needs separate confirmation from your gateway, since our sources do not cover the rules. Ask your accountant about GST.

How should a gym show its class schedule online?

Show it as an editable text table, one day at a time on phones, with a last-updated date. Avoid PDFs and images, which load slowly and go stale. Add booking only if members must reserve a spot.

What should a trainer page say?

A trainer page should say who the trainer is, what they teach, which certificates they can show, which languages they coach in and when they teach. Use a real photo. Skip results you cannot prove.

Are free gym website templates good enough?

Free templates are good enough for a one-page brochure and weak on trials, payments and timetables. They give a layout and nothing about local payments or search. Use one when you can code and have a single location.

How much does a gym website cost in India?

A gym website's indicative range is ₹9,999–₹24,999 for a landing page or ₹24,999–₹59,999 for a business site, excluding 18% GST. Booking and UPI payments are priced as options in the calculator. These are draft ranges, never a quote, and a fixed quote is confirmed in writing after a call.
