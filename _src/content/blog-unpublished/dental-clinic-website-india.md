Most dental websites look the same: a stock photo of a smiling couple, a list of services and a phone number in the footer. Patients do not choose a dentist from that. They choose from what they can verify in about thirty seconds on their phone: does this clinic look real, can I see what it costs or what to expect, and can I book without a phone call.

This is what we built into the redesign of [Ashirwad Dental Clinics](/work/ashirwad-dental/), and what we would put on any clinic site.

## Make booking a tap, not a form

Dental patients are often in a hurry, anxious or in pain. A long form is the worst possible interface for them. The booking flow on the Ashirwad site runs through WhatsApp: a sticky button on every page opens a pre-filled message to the clinic. Patients already use WhatsApp daily, the clinic already answers it, and the conversation that follows feels personal rather than transactional.

Keep a call button next to it. Some patients, especially older ones, will always prefer to phone.

## One page per treatment

"Dental implants in Bengaluru" and "root canal treatment cost" are searches a patient makes when they are close to booking. A single services page cannot rank for each of them, because Google ignores everything after a `#` anchor. Give each major treatment its own page with:

- What the treatment is, in plain language
- Who it is for and what to expect on the day
- How long it takes and how many visits
- Honest guidance on cost (or at least what drives it)
- Two or three questions patients actually ask, such as "does it hurt?"

Pages like this are also the kind of content that AI answers tend to cite, because they are specific and written from experience.

## Build trust before the first visit

Nervous first-time patients are looking for reasons to trust you. The strongest ones are cheap to provide:

- **Real photographs** of the clinic, the equipment and the team, not stock images
- **Doctor profiles** with qualifications and registration details
- **A gallery** that shows the space, so there are no surprises on arrival
- **Clear opening hours, address and directions**

On the Ashirwad redesign, the gallery was a deliberate part of the brief: it builds confidence in the clinic before the patient ever walks in.

## Speed matters more than you think

Most clinic searches happen on a phone, often on mobile data. A heavy page that takes eight seconds to load is a patient who never sees your phone number. We build clinic sites mobile-first, compress images properly and avoid heavy plug-ins, so the page is usable almost immediately.

## Connect the website to Google Maps

For "dentist near me", your Google Business Profile does most of the work, and your website supports it. Keep the clinic name, address and phone number identical on both, embed the map, and add local structured data. If you have not set up your profile properly yet, start with our guide to [ranking on Google Maps](/blog/rank-on-google-maps/).

## A note on advertising rules

Medical and dental councils in India restrict how clinics may advertise, including claims, before-and-after photos and patient testimonials. We keep clinic sites informational, and the clinic approves every claim and image before it goes live. Please check your council's current rules before publishing anything promotional.

### Should booking be a form or WhatsApp?

For most clinics, WhatsApp. It is faster for the patient and gives your front desk a conversation to continue. Keep a call button too.

### Do I need a new site if I already have one?

Not always. If your site is slow, hard to use on a phone or has no treatment pages, a redesign usually pays for itself. We can audit what you have and tell you honestly, and we plan redesigns so you do not lose existing Google rankings.
