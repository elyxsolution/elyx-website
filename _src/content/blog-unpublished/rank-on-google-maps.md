For a local business, Google Maps is often the first thing a customer sees. Someone searches "dentist near me" or "salon in Indiranagar" and gets a map with three listings. Being one of the three is worth more than a page-one organic result.

Google explains local ranking as three factors: **relevance** (how well your profile matches the search), **distance** (how close you are to the searcher) and **prominence** (how well known you are, including reviews and links). You cannot change distance, but you control the other two. Here is how.

## 1. Claim and verify your profile

Go to [business.google.com](https://business.google.com) and search for your business. If it already exists, claim it; if not, create it. You will then need to verify that you are really the business, which Google does by one of a few methods depending on your business: postcard, phone or text, email, or a short video of your premises. Do this first, because an unverified profile does not rank well.

If you serve customers at their location and do not have a shopfront customers visit, you can set up as a service-area business and hide your address.

## 2. Pick the right primary category

Your primary category is one of the strongest relevance signals. Be specific: "Dentist" rather than "Health", "Hair salon" rather than "Beauty". Add a few secondary categories for other things you genuinely do, but do not stuff them. Choose the category your best customers would search for.

## 3. Fill in everything

An incomplete profile signals a business that does not care. Complete:

- **Business name:** your real name, exactly as on your sign. Adding keywords ("Best Dentist Bangalore") breaks Google's guidelines and can get your profile suspended.
- **Address or service area, phone and website**
- **Opening hours**, including holidays. Update them when they change.
- **Services or products**, each with a short description
- **A written description** in plain language
- **Attributes** such as accessibility or accepted payment methods

## 4. Add real photos

Profiles with plenty of real photos get more engagement. Upload your logo and cover image, then 10 or more genuine photos: the premises, the team, your work. Stock photos defeat the purpose. Add new photos regularly.

## 5. Collect real reviews, and answer them

Reviews are the biggest prominence signal you can influence. Ask every happy customer, at the moment they are happiest, with a direct link to your review form. A short message on WhatsApp works well in India. Do not buy reviews or offer incentives for positive ones; that violates Google's policies.

Reply to every review, good or bad, politely and specifically. Replies show future customers how you treat people.

## 6. Keep your details consistent

Your business name, address and phone number should be identical on your profile, your website and any directory listing. Inconsistencies confuse Google and customers. On your website, add the same details in the footer and mark them up with local business structured data.

## 7. Make your website support the profile

The profile and the website reinforce each other. A website with a page for each service, your address and a Google Map embed gives Google extra evidence of what you do and where. See our guide on [whether you need a website, a Business Profile or both](/blog/website-or-google-business-profile/).

## 8. Post and answer questions

Posts about offers, news and events keep the profile fresh, and answered questions help customers. Neither is a magic ranking trick, but an active profile outperforms a dead one.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to rank on Google Maps?

A newly verified, fully completed profile can start appearing within days, but ranking for competitive searches takes weeks to months of reviews and activity. There are no instant tricks.

### Do I need a website to rank on Maps?

You can appear without one, but a website gives Google and customers more to trust, and it is where you convert visitors to enquiries. Most businesses should have both.

### Can I hide my address?

If you are a service-area business with no location customers visit, yes. If customers come to you, Google expects a visible address.
