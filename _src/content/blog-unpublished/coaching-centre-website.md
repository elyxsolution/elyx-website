Most coaching centres do not have an enquiry problem. They have a conversion problem. Calls come in, forms arrive, WhatsApp messages pile up, and a large share never become admissions. The website is part of why. Here is what a site should do to help.

## One page for every course

A single "Courses" page with a list cannot rank for "IELTS coaching in Bengaluru" or "GRE classes near me". Give each course its own page covering:

- Who the course is for and what it covers
- Batch timings, duration and mode (classroom, online or hybrid)
- Faculty for that course
- Fees, or what the fee includes
- A clear button: book a demo class

This is also how parents and students shortlist. They compare courses, not institutes, so each course page is a small landing page of its own. We used this approach for the [Pinnacle Inc.](/work/pinnacle-inc/) GRE and IELTS coaching site.

## Show fees and batches plainly

Families compare. If your fee and batch details are hidden behind a form, they will compare with a competitor that shows them. Plain information filters out wrong-fit leads and builds trust with the right ones. If you cannot publish exact fees, publish what drives them.

## Make the demo class the main action

For most coaching, the demo class is the real conversion event. Put "Book a free demo" in the header, in the first screen and at the end of every course page. Keep the form short: name, phone, course and preferred slot. Every extra field costs you leads.

## Put WhatsApp on every page

In India, WhatsApp is how many parents and students prefer to start. A button with a pre-filled message such as "Hi, I'd like details of the IELTS batch" lowers the barrier and gives your counsellor a conversation to continue. Keep a tap-to-call button beside it.

## Be careful and accurate with results

Claims about ranks, scores and selections attract scrutiny, and India has issued guidelines against misleading coaching advertisements. Publish only what you can verify, show it with context (year, batch, the student's consent) and avoid language that sounds like a guarantee. Please check current rules before publishing results.

## Track where admissions come from

You are likely spending on ads, social posts or events. Without tracking you do not know which one works. Set up analytics events for form submissions, WhatsApp clicks and calls, and ask every enquiry how they found you. Log it against the admission. After a couple of months you will know which channel to double down on and which to stop paying for.

## Speed and mobile come first

Parents and students browse on phones, often on mobile data. A heavy site loses them before the page appears. Build mobile-first, compress images and avoid auto-playing videos and heavy sliders.

## Make updating easy

Batch dates and fees change every term. If updating them needs a developer, they will be out of date. Add a simple content management system so your team can edit batches, fees and announcements.

## FAQ

### How many pages should a coaching centre website have?

At minimum: home, one page per course, faculty, fees and batches, results, and contact. Add a blog or announcements later.

### Should fees be on the website?

Whenever you can, yes. At least explain what is included and what changes the fee.

### Can parents pay online?

Yes. We can add UPI and card payments, with receipts, for registration or fees.
