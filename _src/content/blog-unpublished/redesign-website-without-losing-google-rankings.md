A redesign is the moment most sites quietly lose years of search visibility. The new design looks great, the launch goes well, and six weeks later enquiries are down by a third. Almost always the cause is not design. It is that URLs changed without redirects, pages that ranked were deleted, or the new site hides content behind scripts search engines read badly.

Redesigns and migrations are a big part of our work, including the [Ashirwad Dental Clinic redesign](/work/ashirwad-dental/). This is the checklist we plan from.

## Before you touch anything: take a snapshot

You cannot protect what you have not measured.

1. **Crawl the current site** and export every URL, title tag, meta description and H1.
2. **Export from Google Search Console** the pages and queries that bring impressions and clicks over the last 12 months.
3. **Note your top pages by enquiries**, not just traffic. A page with 30 visits that produces calls matters more than one with 3,000 that produces nothing.
4. **Save a copy** of the old site, or at least screenshots, so nothing is lost if you need to refer back.

## Keep URLs wherever you can

The safest URL is the one that does not change. If `/services/implants` ranks today, it should still be `/services/implants` after the redesign. A new design does not need new addresses.

When a URL has to change, for example moving from `services.html` to a cleaner structure, every old URL needs a **301 redirect** to its closest new equivalent. Redirect to the matching page, not to the homepage. A blanket redirect to the homepage is treated like a deleted page.

## Keep the content that earns traffic

Do not "clean up" a page that ranks. Rewriting a 900-word page into a 120-word marketing blurb removes the reasons it ranked. For each ranking page, the new version should contain the same information, ideally better organised, with the same headings and answers to the same questions.

If you want to improve content, do it after the redesign has settled, so you can tell what caused any change.

## Check the technical basics on the new site

Before launch, on a staging copy that is blocked from search:

- **One canonical domain.** Pick www or non-www and redirect the other, with canonicals, sitemap and internal links all agreeing.
- **Mobile-first.** Google indexes the mobile version, so the phone layout must contain everything the desktop one does.
- **Speed.** Compress images, avoid heavy scripts and check Core Web Vitals.
- **Structured data and titles.** Carry across title tags and meta descriptions for pages that rank.
- **Real links.** Navigation and key content should use normal HTML links, not scripts that crawlers cannot follow.

## Launch day

1. Remove the staging block (`noindex` and any password) **before** launch. Forgetting this is the classic disaster.
2. Switch on the redirect map and test a sample of old URLs by hand.
3. Submit the new sitemap in Search Console.
4. Check that the contact form, WhatsApp button and phone links all work.

## The first 30 days

Watch Search Console daily for the first week, then weekly. You are looking for:

- A rise in **404 errors**, which means a redirect was missed
- Pages that dropped out of **indexing**
- Queries whose impressions fall sharply

A small dip in the first one to two weeks is normal while Google reprocesses the site. A steady decline is a signal to investigate. Fix redirects first, then content parity, then speed.

## FAQ

### Will a redesign always cause a traffic drop?

No. A well-planned redesign with preserved URLs, correct redirects and equivalent content usually sees little or no sustained drop. Drops come from avoidable mistakes.

### Should I change my domain at the same time?

Avoid it if you can. Changing the domain and the design together makes it hard to tell what caused a problem. If you must, do it with full redirects and use Search Console's change-of-address tool.

### How long until Google fully picks up the new site?

Usually a few weeks for the main pages. Large sites can take longer. Submitting the sitemap and fixing crawl errors quickly speeds it up.
