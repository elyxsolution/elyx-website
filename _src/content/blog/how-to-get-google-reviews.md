How to get Google reviews comes down to one habit: ask every customer, soon after a good experience, with a direct review link or QR code that opens Google's review form, and offer nothing in return. Google's Business Profile Help says reviews must reflect a genuine experience, and it calls incentives such as free or discounted goods or services in exchange for a review fake and misleading content. The honest qualification is that this page rests on Google's help pages and a few vendor guides, so confirm the current policy text before you build a routine on it.

- **The method.** Make one review link per branch, turn it into a QR code, and send the same link on WhatsApp.
- **The timing.** Ask the same day, and no later than about two days after the visit.
- **The ban list.** No rewards, no asking only happy customers, no reviews from people who did not use you.
- **The routine.** Count asks and new reviews every week, so you know whether a change helped.

## Before You Ask: Why Reviews Matter and What You Need

Reviews help because they sit next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search, but you need a verified profile and customers with a Google Account before any request works. Google's Business Profile Help says you must verify the business before you can reply to reviews, and that customers must be signed in to a Google Account, which a non-Gmail address can create. It also says reviews are available only in certain countries and business categories, so check that your profile shows a reviews section.

On why it is worth the effort, the evidence here comes from vendors, not Google. Podium says Google has described review recency, frequency, quality and quantity as inputs to local ranking, and WordStream says 88% of consumers trust online reviews as much as personal recommendations. No source promises a ranking.

Reviews are one part of local search, which is what the studio's [SEO optimization service](/services/seo-optimization/) covers alongside the profile itself. Online stores should also know that Maps reviews are separate from seller ratings, which Google's Merchant API guide (last updated 26 October 2026) ties to enrolment in the store ratings program in Merchant Center.

## Google Review Link and QR Code: How to Make and Place One

A review link opens Google's review form for your business directly, and a QR code is that same link printed as a square a phone camera can scan. Google's Business Profile Help tells owners to ask customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code, and it keeps the current steps on its [page for creating a review link or QR code](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815?hl=en). I did not retrieve that page's text, so follow it for the menu steps, because menus change.

The link matters because, as WordStream's 2024 guide describes it, a customer without one must open Maps, search for you, open your profile, scroll to the reviews and tap the write button, which is five steps before typing a word.

### Making the link

1. **Get the link from Google.** Use your verified profile and Google's page above.
2. **Or use a generator.** Productmate's free tool builds a review link from any Google Maps URL and says it works even if you do not manage the profile.
3. **Check the branch.** Productmate says to create a separate link for every location and never to copy a Place ID from another branch.
4. **Scan it yourself.** Open the link or QR code on a phone and confirm that the right business's review form appears.
5. **Expect a free choice of stars.** Productmate says no link can preselect five stars, because the customer picks the rating.

If a short link fails, Productmate suggests pasting the full Google Maps URL into the generator, and opening the link in a browser rather than inside the Maps app.

### Placing the QR code

Put the code where the customer is already waiting or paying: the bill folder, the billing counter, a table card, the delivery bag, the invoice PDF and your email signature. WordStream also suggests a review button on your website and a "leave us a review" card handed over with the receipt.

Podium reports that customers who have just had a good experience are 12% more likely to leave a review if a sign asks them to. It does not name its study in the text I read, so take that as a vendor's claim. Add a line such as "Share your honest experience on Google", and nothing about a reward.

### Google Pay for Business

If you take payments through Google Pay for Business, Google's help page describes two built-in routes. You can tap "Request reviews" under your profile's "see all reviews" to generate a link to share, or a customer who pays by scanning your QR code in Google Pay gets a "Rate this place" screen after the payment succeeds. Both need the account connected to your Google Business Profile, and I did not verify which regions the app serves.

## How to Ask for Google Reviews on WhatsApp

Send the request as a short one-to-one WhatsApp message after the visit, with your review link, a plain thank you and no offer. Productmate says WhatsApp messages have high open rates, though it gives no figure, and the customer already has the chat open, so the form is one tap away.

Send it to every customer, not only the ones who looked pleased. Here are two copyable versions in my own wording:

- **After a visit.** "Hi [Name], thank you for coming to [Business] today. If you have a minute, please share your honest experience on Google: [link]"
- **After a job or delivery.** "Hi [Name], your [order or job] is done. We would value your honest feedback on Google: [link]"

Podium suggests a friendly follow-up when someone does not respond. My rule is one reminder at most, because a second one turns a request into pressure.

On consent, Elyx Solutions' own WhatsApp automation page says business-started messages need the customer's consent and approved templates through the WhatsApp Business Platform. Send requests to people who have chatted with you or given their number for the order. I did not retrieve WhatsApp's own policy, so read it before you automate, and see [how WhatsApp templates and fees differ by option](/blog/whatsapp-booking-for-website/).

## When to Ask for a Google Review

Ask on the same day as the service, and no later than about 48 hours after, when the customer is pleased and has a minute free. Podium's guide recommends sending within 24 to 48 hours and picking downtime in the sales process. Productmate says to ask right after a positive interaction, not days later, so the two differ only on how tight the window is.

The table applies those two timing rules to common Indian small businesses. It is my reading, not a tested result.

| Business | Good moment | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| Clinic or lab | After the visit, at the billing desk | QR code, then WhatsApp that evening |
| Restaurant or café | When the bill arrives | QR code on the bill or table card |
| Salon or spa | While paying at the counter | QR code, then WhatsApp |
| Home services | At handover, after the job is checked | WhatsApp link |
| Online store | A day or two after delivery | WhatsApp or email link |

Do not ask a person who is upset, in pain or mid-complaint. For clinics, the studio's [guide to doctors' websites](/blog/website-for-doctors-india/) lists a review routine that asks patients without scripting what they write.

## Which Way of Asking Fits Your Business

Pick the channel that matches where the customer is when you ask: a QR code for people in front of you, WhatsApp for people you already chat with. The table gives my reading of five options, built from the sources cited above.

| Channel | Works best when | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| QR code | Customers are in your shop or clinic | A wrong-branch code printed on many cards |
| WhatsApp link | You already chat with the customer | Consent for business-started messages |
| SMS | You hold numbers but not WhatsApp | Telecom rules on commercial messages |
| Website button | Visitors already like you | Reaches only people who visit |
| Google Pay for Business | Customers pay by scanning your QR | Account must be linked to your profile |

Elyx's call assistant page notes that TRAI regulates commercial calls and messages. I did not retrieve those rules, so confirm them before any bulk SMS.

- **Choose a QR code** when most customers pay or wait at a counter.
- **Choose WhatsApp** when bookings or orders already happen in a chat.
- **Choose a website reviews page** when customers research you first. WordStream says showing reviews as text, not screenshots, lets Google's crawlers read them.
- **Use two channels, not five.** One in person and one digital, with a single link, is easy to track.

## What Google Bans When You Ask for Reviews

Google bans any incentive in exchange for a review, and its Business Profile Help says reviews must reflect a genuine experience. Read on 19 November 2026, the same page says offering free or discounted goods or services to post a review, change a review or remove a negative one is fake and misleading content and strictly prohibited.

| Practice | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Free or discounted goods for a review | Banned | Google Business Profile Help |
| Paying to change or remove a negative review | Banned | Google Business Profile Help |
| Review from someone who never used you | Banned: not genuine | Google Business Profile Help |
| Rewards, gifts or loyalty points | Banned | Podium, matching Google |
| Asking only happy customers (gating) | Avoid | Podium, Productmate |
| Asking every customer, neutrally | Fine | Google, Productmate |

Review gating means screening customers privately and sending only the satisfied ones to Google. Podium lists avoiding it among its key rules, and Productmate tells owners to invite every customer. Google's own text on gating was not in the material I read, so check its content policy, which the Business Profile pages link to.

My reading of "in exchange for" is that a gift you announce, even afterwards, is still an exchange. The safe rule is that nothing you give or promise is tied to whether, or how, someone reviews you. Podium warns that violations often lead to removed reviews or profile penalties, which is the vendor's statement, not a documented Google penalty schedule.

## Can You Pay for Google Reviews? Why Buying Fails

You cannot safely buy Google reviews, because a paid review is not a genuine experience and Google names incentivised reviews as fake and misleading content. Searches for "pay to get Google reviews" and "buy Google reviews India" are common, so the answer is plain: do not.

The useful distinction is between paying for a tool and paying for reviews. Software that sends your requests only helps you ask real customers, while a service that supplies reviews from people who never used you breaks the genuine-experience rule (my reading of Google's wording). I found no Indian legal source on paid reviews in the research package, so this is a Google-policy answer only.

## Replying to Reviews and Getting Bad Ones Removed

You cannot delete a customer's review, but you can reply to it, and you can flag it if it breaks Google's content policies. Google's Business Profile Help says replies are checked against its policies, usually within 10 minutes but sometimes up to 30 days. A reply is public, the reviewer is notified and can change their review, and the review's date then updates.

Google's reply tips are short:

- **Be polite, brief and personal.** Use the reviewer's name and answer what they said.
- **Be conversational, not promotional.** Do not use a reply to offer deals.
- **On negative reviews,** apologise where fair, explain limits honestly, never share private details and move complex cases to a call or email.

Podium suggests replying to negative reviews within two hours and positive ones within 24, which is vendor advice, not a Google rule. Google also says a mix of positive and negative reviews often feels more trustworthy. To report a review that breaks the rules, use Google's [page for reporting inappropriate reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773?hl=en); I did not read it, and no source here says how often flags succeed.

## What Usually Goes Wrong With Review Requests

Most failures come from a broken link, a leading ask or no owner for the routine, and each has a quick fix. The causes come from the sources already cited, and the fixes are mine.

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| QR code opens the wrong branch | Scan every code before printing; one link per branch |
| Short link will not resolve | Paste the full Maps URL into the generator |
| Asking only the happy ones | Ask every customer with the same message |
| Offering a discount or gift | Stop; Google bans it |
| Customers stuck at sign-in | Tell them any email can create a Google Account |
| Asking a week later | Send the same day or within 48 hours |
| Nobody replies to reviews | Give one person the reply task weekly |

## What to Do Next: A Weekly Routine and Checklist

Run the same short routine every week and count what it produces, so each change can be judged. Track one number: new reviews divided by customers asked. A fictional physio clinic that serves 40 patients in a week, asks all 40 and gets 6 new reviews has a rate of 6 ÷ 40 = 0.15, or 15%. The figures are invented to show the arithmetic and are not a benchmark.

The count also exposes gating. If that clinic asked only the 10 patients who seemed pleased and got 6 reviews, the rate would look like 60%, but the ask would be the review gating that Podium and Productmate warn against.

Copy this checklist:

- [ ] Profile verified and showing a reviews section
- [ ] One review link per branch, each scanned on a phone
- [ ] QR code on the bill, counter or table card, with a neutral line
- [ ] A WhatsApp message saved on the phone, with the link and no offer
- [ ] Everyone asked, same day, within 48 hours at most
- [ ] No gifts, discounts or promises tied to reviews
- [ ] One named person replies to every review weekly
- [ ] Weekly count of customers asked and new reviews

## FAQ

### How do I get Google reviews for my business?

Verify your Business Profile, create a review link or QR code, and ask every customer soon after the service. Send the link on WhatsApp or show the code at the counter, and offer nothing in return.

### How do I get a Google review link?

Use the steps on Google's Business Profile Help page for creating a link or QR code to request reviews. A free generator such as Productmate can also build one from your Maps URL, and you should scan it to confirm the right branch opens.

### How do I get a Google reviews QR code?

Create the review link first, then turn it into a QR code, which Google's help page and Productmate's generator both cover. Scan it yourself before printing hundreds.

### Can I offer a discount for a Google review?

No, because Google's Business Profile Help says free or discounted goods or services in exchange for a review are fake and misleading content and strictly prohibited. The ban also covers paying someone to change a review or remove a negative one.

### Can I ask only happy customers for a review?

No, you should ask everyone, because Podium lists avoiding review gating as a core rule and Productmate says to invite every customer. Google's own gating text was not in the material I read, so check its content policy.

### Is it allowed to ask for Google reviews on WhatsApp?

Yes, sending the review link in a WhatsApp message is an ordinary way to ask, provided it is neutral and offers nothing. Elyx's own page says business-started messages need consent and approved templates, so read WhatsApp's policy before automating.

### Can I buy Google reviews in India?

You should not, because a purchased review is not a genuine experience and Google's help page names incentivised reviews as prohibited. Paying for software that sends your own requests is different from paying for reviews written by non-customers.

### How can I get a Google review removed?

You cannot delete it yourself, but you can flag a review that breaks Google's content policies through its page for reporting inappropriate reviews. A fair negative review stays, so reply to it politely.

### Do customers need a Gmail account to leave a review?

No, they need a Google Account, which they can create with any email address. Google's Business Profile Help says customers must be signed in to leave a review and that a non-Gmail address works.
