A website for a manufacturing company in India earns international enquiries when a buyer who cannot visit your plant can check your capacity, certificates and product specifications in a few minutes, then send a request for quotation (RFQ) without friction. The honest qualification is that every source behind this article is an agency page, a trade-media list or a directory, with no primary data on export enquiries. So the advice below is my reading of those pages, and each claim is attributed to the page that makes it.

- **Catalogue.** Publish specifications as web pages and offer the PDF datasheet as a download, not the other way round.
- **RFQ.** Ask only for the fields you need to price a job, show a phone number and reply fast.
- **Languages.** Translate the pages that carry the sale, for the countries you really sell to, before the rest.
- **Trust.** Put capacity, certificates and export record on pages a buyer reaches in one click.

## What a Website for a Manufacturing Company in India Must Prove

An industrial buyer shortlists on proof, so your site must answer the checks a procurement team runs before anyone picks up a phone. DigiStreet Media, an agency, lists them in its page on industrial sites: capacity, experience, associations, quality standards and specifications. The page also adds category leadership, meaning whether you have a view on where your sector is heading.

| Buyer check | What to publish | Per DigiStreet Media |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity | Plants, lines, floor area, annual output | Most common gap on Indian industrial sites |
| Experience | Years in operation, on the home page | A proxy for survival and reliability |
| Association | OEM clients, export markets, listings | In B2B the customer list is the credential |
| Quality standards | ISO 9001, ISO 14001, IATF 16949, testing, rejection rates | Gate criteria for procurement |
| Specifications | Grades, tolerances, materials, dimensions | Should be HTML, not PDF |

DigiStreet gives one example: an automotive components maker it is rebuilding had no mention of ISO or IATF certification on its old site, though the credentials were never missing from the business. That is one agency's account with no method published, so treat it as an illustration. A buyer abroad sees only what you have published.

## Product Catalogue Pages Buyers Can Search and Share

A product catalogue works best as crawlable pages with the specification written into the page, plus a downloadable datasheet for the buyer's own file. DigiStreet says datasheets locked behind downloads are invisible to search and to AI answers, and that specifications published as HTML become an industrial site's highest-intent pages. Industrial Marketer, a trade-media site, scores sites on the opposite need: easy-to-download data sheets and 3D CAD models that engineers pass to procurement.

Do both. Give each product or product family these fields:

- **Name and code.** The part number a buyer would quote in an RFQ.
- **Specification.** Material, grade, dimensions, tolerance and standard, as text.
- **Applications.** The industries and uses, in the words buyers search.
- **Downloads.** Datasheet PDF, and a drawing or CAD file where you have one.
- **Action.** A Request a Quote button that carries the product name into the form.

### Gate Downloads or Leave Them Open?

Leave datasheets and catalogues open, and gate only material that cost you real effort to write. Industrial Marketer counts gated white papers as a plus, yet it criticises one maker that gated even brochures, because a busy engineer may leave rather than fill a form. That is the reviewer's opinion, not measured data.

## RFQ Forms That Get Completed

An RFQ form gets completed when it asks for the five things you need to price a job and offers a quicker route beside it. Industrial Marketer names Request a Quote and Talk to an Engineer as the calls to action it looks for, and praises one site for a phone number in the top navigation. It also rewards sites with a way to reach a real person, such as a chat tool or phone, backed by staff who answer.

The studio's industry pages give two parallels from other trades. Its real estate page says forms should ask the questions you need and send every lead to WhatsApp or email at once. Its coaching page says it tags form, WhatsApp and call events in GA4 by page and campaign.

Here is my template for a first RFQ form:

1. **Product.** Prefilled from the page the buyer came from.
2. **Quantity and unit.** One field, with the unit chosen from a list.
3. **Company and country.** Two fields, so you can price freight and check the buyer.
4. **Email and WhatsApp number.** The number with country code.
5. **Notes.** Drawing, standard, target delivery.

Ask for confidential drawings in your first reply, not on the form. Put office hours in India Standard Time beside the phone number, since a buyer in another time zone needs to know when you answer. Set an internal reply rule; I suggest the same working day, though no source gives a benchmark.

## Multilingual Pages: When Translation Pays Back

Translate when a language belongs to a market you sell to, and not before. DigiStreet says that if Europe, Japan or the Gulf are real markets, thin content in the buyer's language reads as a thin business, and that it implements hreflang so translated pages count as alternates, not duplicates. Hreflang is a tag that tells Google which page serves which language.

The sources do not say which languages buyers use or how much translation lifts enquiries, so the table is my decision rule, not a benchmark.

| Your situation | Choose | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Buyers mostly use English | English only, written well | Casual spelling and units |
| One or two countries send orders | Translate home, category, trust and RFQ pages | Technical terms |
| Many countries, little data | English site, then read your enquiry log | Translating blind |

Have a native speaker who knows your trade check technical terms, because a wrong term on a specification page costs more than no translation. The calculator lists multiple languages as a separate priced option, so fix the language list before you request a quote.

## Trust Pages: Capacity, Certificates and Export Record

Trust pages are the pages that let a buyer verify you without a visit, and they are the part of this topic the ranking pages say least about. DigiStreet calls certifications a first filter and complains that many sites bury them one or two clicks deep. Industrial Marketer describes one hardware supplier that leads with nearly 30 years of expertise and more than 100,000,000 units supplied a year, which shows how a number can carry a claim.

| Page | What goes on it | Proof a buyer can check |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity and plant | Lines, floor area, annual output, photos | Dated figures, real photographs |
| Quality and certificates | Certificate, issuer, number, validity date | The certificate scan |
| Clients and export markets | Named clients you may name, countries served | Permission to publish |
| Company facts | Registered name, address, years, named contact | Registrations you can show |

DigiStreet says capacity, plant count, certifications and export markets are not sensitive for most manufacturers, since competitors already know them, while pricing, customer-specific volumes and process detail stay private. That is an agency's view, so decide each item yourself. Publish only what is true and current, because a lapsed certificate on a trust page does more harm than a blank one.

The [doctors article](/blog/website-for-doctors-india/) makes the same case for another trade: credentials a visitor can verify turn a stranger into a contact. The studio's industry pages show registration numbers the same way, RERA for builders and FSSAI for food businesses.

## What Usually Goes Wrong on Manufacturer Websites

Most manufacturer sites fail because they describe the company instead of qualifying it, which is DigiStreet's diagnosis, and the fixes below are mostly mine.

| What goes wrong | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer cannot verify capacity | Site lists products and a mission | Capacity page with dated figures |
| Certificates missing or buried | Kept in a brochure or deep menu | Certificates page, linked from home |
| Specifications unreadable | Locked inside a PDF | HTML specification, PDF as extra |
| Every download gated | Lead-form habit | Open datasheets, gate white papers |
| No person to reach | Form only | Phone, WhatsApp, named contact |
| Judged on monthly leads | Short reporting cycle | Review per quarter, by country |

## FAQ

### What is a manufacturer website and what should it include?

A manufacturer website is the company's own site where buyers verify it and request a quote. It should include specification pages, an RFQ form, a capacity page, certificates and a named contact.

### How do I get international enquiries through a manufacturing website?

Make the site answer a buyer's checks, then make the next step easy: capacity, certificates and specifications as readable pages, an RFQ form and a contact route that works across time zones. Expect months, because industrial buying is slow.

### What is an RFQ form and what should it ask?

An RFQ form is a request for quotation: the buyer names a product and quantity and asks for a price. Ask for the product, quantity and unit, company and country, email and WhatsApp number, and notes. Fewer fields get more completed forms.

### Should my manufacturing website be multilingual?

Make it multilingual only for markets you really sell to. DigiStreet Media says thin content in a buyer's language reads as a thin business for markets such as Europe, Japan or the Gulf. Start with home, category, trust and RFQ pages, and check technical terms.

### Do I need my own website if I am listed on Exporters India or MSME Mart?

Yes, because a directory finds buyers and your own site is where they verify you. Neither directory shows enquiry figures we could read, so treat a listing as a way in, not as your proof.

### Should product datasheets be PDFs or web pages?

Publish the specification as a web page and offer the PDF as a download. DigiStreet Media says PDFs are effectively invisible to search engines and AI assistants, while Industrial Marketer values downloadable data sheets for procurement teams.

### Which certifications should a manufacturer show on its website?

Show only the certifications you hold, with the issuer, number and validity date. DigiStreet Media names ISO 9001, ISO 14001 and IATF 16949 as examples buyers check. Keep certificate scans ready for buyers who ask.
