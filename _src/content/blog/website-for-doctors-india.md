A website for doctors in India has four jobs: show what you treat, prove you are a registered doctor, let a patient book in one tap, and appear on Google Maps when someone searches nearby. Practo and similar listings can do part of that, but only your own site lets you control the page, the wording and the booking path. The qualification that matters is regulation: the research behind this article held no primary text of the medical council or data-protection rules, so the privacy and advertising sections are a business checklist, not legal advice.

- **Pages.** Eight pages cover a clinic site, led by doctor profiles with registration numbers and one clear way to book.
- **Appointment tools.** Start with a call button and WhatsApp click-to-chat, and add online scheduling only when your front desk can run it daily.
- **Practo.** A listing helps new patients find you; your own site holds your wording, booking path and Google presence. Most practices benefit from both.
- **Privacy.** Collect only what booking needs, keep symptoms out of forms and WhatsApp, and confirm council and data rules with a professional.

## Must-Have Pages for a Website for Doctors in India

A clinic website needs eight pages, and each one answers a question a patient asks before booking. The table below is my own page plan, built from the four jobs above and from the page needs Elyx lists for clinics. It is analysis, not data from a study.

| Page | What goes on it | Patient's question |
|---|---|---|
| Home | Clinic name, specialty, area, one booking button | Is this the right clinic? |
| Doctor profiles | Photo, qualifications, registration number, council | Is this doctor registered? |
| Treatment pages | One page per treatment, plain steps, who it suits | Can you treat my problem? |
| Appointment page | WhatsApp, call, request form, timings | How do I book? |
| Fees and FAQs | Consultation fee, what it includes, common questions | What will it cost? |
| Location and contact | Map, directions, parking, hours, phone | How do I reach you? |
| Privacy notice | What the site collects, why, who sees it | What happens to my details? |
| Gallery | Real photos of the clinic and team | What will it be like? |

Treatment pages are the ones that can rank for what patients search, because a patient who types a treatment plus a locality is looking for exactly that page. Building them is routine [web development](/services/web-development/) work. The part that needs care is the doctor profile, covered next.

## Doctor Profiles: Showing a Registration Number Patients Can Verify

Each doctor profile should show the registration number and the council that issued it, because patients can check both on the National Medical Commission (NMC) register. The NMC's Indian Medical Register page, read on 16 November 2026, says it publishes registered doctors from the State Medical Councils. Visitors can search by name, qualification, registration year, registration number and registered council.

Those five search fields make a ready checklist for a profile: name as registered, qualification, year, number and council. A patient wondering whether a doctor is genuine, one of the questions people ask around this topic, can then confirm in a minute.

The NMC page does not say how complete or current each state council's data is. So a missing search result is a reason to ask the clinic, not proof of anything. What else a profile may claim, such as specialty wording or titles, is set by the council, so check current rules before you publish.

## Appointment Tools: From a WhatsApp Button to Online Booking

Pick the simplest appointment tool your front desk can answer every day, because a booking path nobody monitors is worse than a plain phone number. That is my reading, not a sourced finding. The table lists five tools from least to most work.

| Tool | Choose it when | What it needs from you |
|---|---|---|
| Call button | Most patients already phone | Someone answers during opening hours |
| WhatsApp click-to-chat | Patients prefer messaging | A clinic-owned number and a named replier |
| Request form | You confirm slots by phone | Someone checks it, plus an alert |
| Booking calendar | Several doctors or chairs, fixed slots | Slot rules, leave days, staff training |
| Booking with payment | You want deposits or prepaid consults | A payment gateway and refund rules |

A single doctor with one clinic is usually well served by the first two rows. Add the calendar when double-booking or phone tag becomes the daily problem. Elyx's price data lists online appointment booking as a separate feature from the base site, and its web development page says the price moves with whether you need booking or online payments.

## WhatsApp for Clinics: Click-to-Chat Without Oversharing

A WhatsApp button works for a clinic when it opens a pre-filled message to a number the clinic controls and asks for logistics, not symptoms. Click-to-chat is a link that opens WhatsApp with your number and a ready-typed message, so a patient only taps send. Here is a three-part script to adapt.

- **Pre-filled message.** "Hello, I would like to book an appointment with Dr [name] at [clinic]. My name is ___ and a good time is ___."
- **Clinic reply.** Offer two slots, confirm the patient's name, and ask nothing clinical.
- **If symptoms arrive anyway.** Say the doctor will discuss it in the consultation, and steer back to the slot.

Use a dedicated clinic number rather than a personal phone, and name one person who owns replies on each shift. The research held no text of WhatsApp's business terms, so read them before you send reminders or promotional messages.

## Google Maps and Business Profile: What the Site Can and Cannot Do

Your Google Business Profile, more than your website, decides whether the clinic shows on Google Maps; the site supports it with matching details. Local schema, which is code that tells search engines your name, address and hours, helps only when it agrees with the profile. Keep these in step:

- The same clinic name, address and phone on the profile, the site and every listing.
- The right category, opening hours and holiday notes.
- Real photos of the clinic and team.
- A map embed and directions on the contact page.
- A routine for asking patients for reviews, without scripting what they say.

Elyx has a free [Google Business Profile checker](/tools/google-business-profile-checker/) that scores a profile. For the page itself, Google's Search Central Blog gave plain advice in a December 2010 post about non-profit sites: write your own title, which search engines show at about 60 characters; write a description; use an h1 heading; describe images in alt text. Its percentages (27% of sites misused titles, 54% had description problems, 19% heading problems, 37% alt-text problems) describe those non-profit sites in 2010, so they say nothing about clinics today.

The post's best title example pairs a name with a descriptor. An illustrative clinic version, written by me: "Dr Name, Dentist in Indiranagar, Bengaluru | Clinic Name".

## Practo vs Your Own Website: Five Criteria and a Decision Rule

Practo and your own site do different jobs, so the choice depends on which job you need done first, judged on five criteria: how new patients find you, how booking works, how trust is shown, who controls the page, and what it costs. The table uses Practo's homepage as read on 16 November 2026. Where I could not verify a fact, it says so.

| Criterion | Practo listing | Own website |
|---|---|---|
| Finding you | Patients browse by specialty and neighbourhood | Depends on your Maps profile and local pages |
| Booking | In-clinic booking and video consults on its page | Any tool from the table above |
| Trust | Describes its doctors as verified | You show registration number and real photos |
| Control | Platform's page layout and rules | Your wording, within council rules |
| Cost | Not verified here: read Practo's current terms | One-off build plus care; see pricing |

Practo is stronger where a new practice has no reviews and no Maps history. It gives patients booking, rescheduling and visit history without you building them, and its homepage displays user reviews praising exactly those. Your own site is stronger where you want treatment pages, WhatsApp booking, your own fees page and a page that carries only your name.

- **Choose a listing first** when you are new to an area, need patients before reviews exist, and have no build budget this month.
- **Choose your own site first** when patients already search your name, you want WhatsApp booking, or you want to rank for treatments.
- **Do both** when you can: the listing helps people find you, and the site is where your name leads.

Three things the research does not cover: Practo's fees, what its terms say about patient contact details and reviews, and how many patients either route brings. Read any listing agreement before you sign it.

## Online Doctor Consultation Platforms: What They Are and Are Not

Online doctor platforms in India are not clinic websites: they are marketplaces, telehealth apps or doctor-side tools, and a clinic uses each for a different reason. The table shows what three of them say on their own homepages, read on 16 November 2026. These are the vendors' claims, which I have not verified.

| Platform | Its own page says | Role for a clinic |
|---|---|---|
| Practo | Video consults, in-clinic booking, medicines, tests | Listing and discovery |
| MeraDoc | 200+ verified doctors, 24x7 video or audio, 10,000+ consults a day | A competing telehealth app |
| Medifree | Prescription writing, patient records, paid consultation calls | A record tool for the doctor |

A clinic site is a different product: one practice, its own page, its own booking path. Video consultation is covered by its own medical rules, which the research did not supply, so confirm them before you offer video calls from your site.

## Patient Data Privacy: A Business Checklist, Not Legal Advice

A clinic site should collect only the details needed to book an appointment, because every extra field is patient data the clinic then has to protect and justify. Elyx's pages for professional firms refer to India's Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act. The research for this article did not include the Act's text, so I state none of its duties here. Read the Act on the Government of India's official site, as checked on 16 November 2026, and ask a lawyer how it applies to your clinic.

Medical and dental councils also restrict how clinics may advertise, and the research did not supply their rules. Check the NMC's site and your state council for current text. This list is my reading of careful practice, not a statement of what the law requires:

- **Form fields.** Name, phone and preferred time. No symptoms, diagnoses or reports.
- **WhatsApp.** Logistics only in the pre-filled message, from one clinic number.
- **Privacy notice.** A plain-English page saying what is collected, why, who sees it and how to ask for deletion.
- **Who sees enquiries.** Named staff, not a phone passed around.
- **Photos and testimonials.** Written patient permission, then a council-rules check, before anything goes live.
- **Third-party widgets.** Ask each booking or chat tool what it stores and where.
- **Hosting.** Someone responsible for updates and backups.

Rules change, so confirm each point with a qualified professional before launch.

## What Usually Goes Wrong With Clinic Websites

Most clinic site failures come from one cause: nobody owns the site after launch. The table pairs each common mistake with its cause and fix. It is built from the sources above and the standard page needs, not from a survey.

| Mistake | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Only a marketplace profile | Listing mistaken for a website | Own page, linked from the profile |
| Form asks for symptoms | Wish to triage online | Ask logistics only |
| Maps, site and listings differ | One place edited, others forgotten | One master sheet of details |
| Testimonials or before-and-after photos | Copied from a competitor | Get consent, check council rules |
| Default title and headings | Template left unedited | Own title, description, h1, alt text |
| Wrong timings online | No one owns updates | Name an owner; use a CMS |
| WhatsApp unanswered | Button points to a personal phone | Clinic number, one person per shift |

A CMS is a content management system, the editor that lets your staff change timings and fees without a developer. A care plan covers the rest: monitoring, security updates and backups.

## What to Do Next: A Seven-Step Plan

Start with your Google Business Profile and your registration details, because both are free and both feed every other step.

1. **Check the profile.** Claim it, fix the details and run the checker.
2. **Write the master sheet.** Name, address, phone, hours, registration numbers, council and fees.
3. **Choose the appointment tool.** Use the table above and your front desk's real capacity.
4. **List the treatments.** One page per treatment, in plain words.
5. **Gather photos and permissions.** Real pictures, with written consent where patients appear.
6. **Draft the privacy notice.** Have a professional check it and any advertising claims.
7. **Test, launch, review.** Open the site on a phone over mobile data, then review the details monthly.

## FAQ

### How can I check whether a doctor in India is registered?

Search the National Medical Commission's Indian Medical Register. The page lets you search by name, qualification, registration year, registration number and registered council. If you find nothing, ask the clinic for the number and council before booking.

### Do doctors need their own website if they are on Practo?

A listing is not a substitute, because a Practo listing and your own site do different jobs. The listing helps patients find you inside a marketplace. Your own site holds your treatment pages, fees, WhatsApp booking and Maps presence.

### What medical website do doctors use?

Doctors use three kinds: marketplaces such as Practo, doctor-side tools such as Medifree for prescriptions and records, and their own clinic site. Each does a different job. Read each vendor's current terms before choosing.

### Can patients book through WhatsApp?

Yes. A click-to-chat button opens WhatsApp with a pre-filled message to your clinic number, so the patient taps once. Keep the message to name and preferred time, and leave symptoms for the consultation.

### How do I show up on Google Maps?

Set up and keep your Google Business Profile accurate, because Maps rankings are driven mainly by it. Your site supports the profile with the same name, address, phone and hours, plus local schema. No one can promise a Maps position.

### Can a clinic website show patient reviews and before-and-after photos?

Technically yes, but council advertising rules apply, so check them first. Get written patient permission and publish only what you are sure is compliant. The research did not supply the rules themselves, so confirm with your council.

### What patient data should a clinic website avoid collecting?

Avoid symptoms, diagnoses, reports and anything the booking does not need. Name, phone and preferred time are enough to book. Read the DPDP Act through official sources and take professional advice on your duties.
