An AI receptionist for clinics is software that answers patient calls or chats, gives practical information you have approved, books, moves or cancels appointments in your schedule, and hands anything clinical or urgent to a person. It earns its place on the gaps a busy front desk cannot cover: lunch hours, overflow and after-hours calls. The qualification that matters is that all eight vendor pages behind this article say it must never give medical advice, and none of them is an Indian regulator, so the privacy section is a business checklist, not legal advice.

- **Scope.** Hours, directions, booking, reminders and message-taking suit automation; symptoms, medicines and urgency do not.
- **Channel.** Voice fits patients who phone, chat fits patients already on your site, and WhatsApp fits reminders.
- **Booking.** An appointment counts only when your scheduling system confirms it, so ask any vendor to prove read and write access.
- **Privacy.** Give the system the least patient data that does the job, and get storage and recording terms in writing.

## What an AI Receptionist for Clinics Does and Does Not Do

An AI receptionist should handle the routine, scripted calls and pass everything that needs judgement to staff. Aivah sorts calls into three groups: answer in full (hours, address, parking, published prices), collect details for the team (appointments, changes, billing questions) and point to a person (symptoms, medicines, anything urgent). Vrisic's list of what is safe to automate matches that split.

The table merges Aivah, VoiFlow and Vrisic, all read on 20 November 2026. The grouping is my own.

| Call type | AI handles | Goes to staff |
|---|---|---|
| Hours, address, parking, forms | Yes, from a list you approve | Anything the list misses |
| Book, move or cancel | Yes, after an identity check | When the check fails |
| Reminders and confirmations | Yes, without clinical detail | Replies that raise a problem |
| Test results | Takes a message, books a callback | Always |
| Symptoms, medicines, urgency | Stops and reads clinician-approved words | Always |

Vendor pages also agree on the exit: a caller who asks for a person gets one, and if nobody is free the system takes a message and says when someone will ring back.

## Voice, Chat or WhatsApp: Which Channel Fits

Choose the channel your patients already use, because an assistant on a channel they ignore handles nothing. The table sets the three options against what each needs. It is my analysis, drawing on Fonix, VoiFlow and Aivah.

| Channel | Fits when | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Voice on your clinic number | Patients phone, calls overflow or arrive after hours | Number forwarding, accents, language mix |
| Chat on your website | Visitors read treatment pages before booking | Answers only as good as your site |
| WhatsApp | You send reminders and take confirm or move replies | Consent and approved templates |

VoiFlow describes reminders that let a patient reply confirm, move or cancel on WhatsApp, with the answer written straight into the booking. Elyx's own chatbot page notes that WhatsApp requires customers to have agreed to messages and business-started messages to use approved templates. For setup choices, see the [WhatsApp booking options](/blog/whatsapp-booking-for-website/) compared.

- **Choose voice first** when missed calls are your visible problem.
- **Choose chat first** when most enquiries already arrive through your site.
- **Add WhatsApp** when no-shows or reschedules cost you slots.

## Booking Integration: Read, Write and Confirm

A booking integration is real only if the assistant can read free slots, write the appointment and receive a success reply from your scheduling system. Fonix says a calendar connection that only reads events is not a complete booking integration. Vrisic adds that "integrates with" can mean a live write, a read-only lookup, or a message a person types in.

The test is a recorded demo of a real booking, a reschedule and a cancel appearing in a test copy of your own schedule. If a vendor cannot show that, Vrisic says to plan on the assistant taking a request and staff confirming it, which is simpler and still useful. Aivah's product works that way: it takes requests and your team confirms.

Fonix lists what to rehearse in the pilot, and the table condenses it.

| Situation | Safe behaviour |
|---|---|
| Calendar lookup fails | Offer a callback, never a guessed slot |
| Chosen slot just taken | Refresh options, announce nothing until saved |
| Reschedule request | Find the existing booking, never lose it |
| Doctor or branch unclear | Ask a short question |
| Call drops mid-booking | Check the result before retrying |

Fonix also says to repeat the doctor, date and time and wait for the caller's yes before writing. VoiFlow adds an identity check before any change, such as name and date of birth plus a one-time code to the number on file.

## Patient Privacy Cautions: A Business Checklist, Not Legal Advice

The first caution is to give the assistant the least patient data that does the job and to know where recordings and transcripts live. As of 20 November 2026 the research behind this article held no Indian regulatory text.

Elyx's pages for professional firms refer to India's Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act. I state none of its duties, so read it on the Government of India's official site and ask a lawyer how it applies.

Four points come from the vendor pages, which are secondary evidence:

- **Collect little.** Aivah says to ask for the type of visit, not a description of symptoms, and never to upload patient records. Vrisic quotes the UK regulator's principle of processing only the personal data you need, and notes HHS applies a similar minimum-necessary idea in the US.
- **US badges are not Indian compliance.** OmniMD and Vrisic discuss HIPAA, a US law. Vrisic says there is no official HIPAA certification for software, so treat such a badge as marketing.
- **Recording and disclosure.** Aivah says to tell callers they are speaking to an AI and to check recording rules first. Elyx recommends the same disclosure by default.
- **Where data sits.** Fonix and Baap both offer on-premise options. Vrisic cites UAE health-data localisation, and I found no Indian equivalent in the research, so ask for storage location in writing.

Two more cautions come from Elyx's own pages. Reminder calls are outbound, and TRAI's commercial-calls rules apply, so confirm what is allowed before enabling them. Medical and dental councils restrict how clinics advertise, so the assistant's treatment answers should stay inside text your clinician approved. Rules change, so confirm with a qualified professional.

## Kannada, Hindi and Mixed-Language Calls

Language coverage is a claim to test, not a feature to trust. Fonix says it supports 14+ Indian languages, including code-switched speech, then adds that coverage is a reason to test a real call, not a substitute for it. Elyx's call-assistant page agrees that quality varies by language, accent and phone line.

Mixed speech is the hard case. Fonix gives the example of a Kannada opening, an English doctor name and a Hindi clarification in one call. Vrisic says to ask a vendor to prove it on recordings of your own patients, with your doctors' names and areas, and to judge the voice as well as the recognition.

- **Prepare phrases.** Bring real caller phrases, spelling variations and interruptions.
- **Test names.** Include an unclear doctor name to see whether a similar one gets booked.
- **Ask for the failure.** Hear what happens when the assistant does not understand.

## Will It Pay Back? A Worked Example With the Arithmetic

Whether it pays back depends on one number you hold and the vendors do not: what an extra appointment is worth to your clinic. The funnel below is Vrisic's illustration for a four-chair practice, not a client result. The price is ConnectAI's published one. Combining them is my own sum.

1. Calls: 1,200 a month, of which 20% go unanswered: 240.
2. Missed calls that are booking requests (25%): 240 × 0.25 = 60.
3. The assistant books half: 60 × 0.5 = 30.
4. Of those, 40% would have rung back anyway: 30 × 0.4 = 12.
5. Extra bookings: 30 minus 12 = 18 a month.
6. Usage: 400 calls × 3 minutes = 1,200 minutes.
7. Monthly cost at ConnectAI's rates: ₹800 + (1,200 × ₹4) = ₹800 + ₹4,800 = ₹5,600.

Break-even is ₹5,600 ÷ 18 = about ₹311 per extra appointment. If only 9 extra bookings arrive, it is ₹5,600 ÷ 9 = about ₹622. Setup, WhatsApp fees, GST (the page does not say) and staff review time are excluded.

Fonix warns against assuming every unanswered call was a lost booking, so count your own calls before using these rates.

## A Pilot Plan: One Gap, Ten Test Calls, One Week of Transcripts

Start with one coverage gap, not the whole phone line. Fonix recommends after-hours or overflow calls, and Aivah says to go live after hours first. The steps below combine both.

1. **Tally a week of calls.** Separate unanswered calls, repeat attempts, scheduling enquiries and staff-only requests.
2. **Pick one gap.** After hours, lunch or overflow.
3. **Write the sheet.** Answer in full, collect details, point to a person. A clinician signs the urgent wording.
4. **Set the booking rule.** The assistant offers slots, repeats the choice, then writes after the caller agrees.
5. **Ring it ten times,** including two urgent-sounding calls, a failed lookup and a human handoff. Read every transcript.
6. **Go live after hours,** and read transcripts for a week.
7. **Measure.** Track booking completion, callback completion, wrong or duplicate bookings and attendance against your baseline.

VoiFlow's tip is to test with your own staff first, including a child booking for a parent and a caller describing chest pain.

## What Usually Goes Wrong

Most failures trace to the assistant doing more than its integration allows. The table pairs each mistake with its cause and fix, drawn from Fonix, Vrisic and Aivah.

| Mistake | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Says "booked" too early | No wait for the system's reply | Announce only after a saved booking |
| Offers a made-up slot | No fallback when lookup fails | Take a callback instead |
| Read-only calendar sold as booking | Vague "integrates" claim | Demand a book, move, cancel demo |
| Stale hours or fees | Nobody owns updates | Name one owner; review monthly |
| Symptoms collected on calls | Open-ended questions | Ask visit type only; scripted urgent words |
| Duplicate appointments | Retry after a dropped call | Check the booking before retrying |
| Every missed call counted as lost revenue | No baseline | Use a week of call records |

## FAQ

### What is an AI receptionist?

An AI receptionist is software that answers calls or chats for a business, gives approved information and books appointments. For a clinic it should also pass clinical, urgent or sensitive requests to staff. Vendor pages describe voice, chat and WhatsApp versions.

### Will AI take over receptionist jobs?

The vendor pages describe it as covering routine calls while staff keep exceptions, clinical questions and callbacks. OmniMD says clinics need fewer people on the desk, but that is a vendor claim. The research held no independent evidence on staffing.

### What is the best AI receptionist for a clinic in India?

No source supplies an independent winner. ConnectAI ranks itself first on five criteria it chose, and Vrisic favours custom builds in some cases. Compare options on language handling, price transparency, booking depth, handoff and total cost.

### Can an AI receptionist book appointments directly?

Some can, and some only take requests. It books directly only when it can read free slots, write the appointment and get a success reply from your system. Ask for a recorded book, move and cancel in a test copy of your schedule.

### Can an AI receptionist give medical advice?

No. Every vendor page in the research says it must not diagnose, interpret results, suggest medicines or judge urgency. Clinical questions and emergencies go to a person, using wording your clinician approved.

### Is it safe to let an AI hear patient details?

It is safe only when you limit what it sees and control where data is stored. Collect the type of visit rather than symptoms, never upload patient records, and ask about recordings and storage. The research held no Indian legal text, so confirm your duties under the DPDP Act with a lawyer.

### Do I have to tell callers it is an AI?

Telling them is the practice every vendor page recommends, and Elyx builds it in by default. VoiFlow adds that the assistant should never deny being an AI when asked. The research did not supply an Indian disclosure rule.

### Can it handle Hindi, Kannada and mixed-language calls?

Often, but quality varies by language, accent and phone line. Fonix claims 14+ Indian languages including code-switched speech. Test with recordings of your own callers, including doctor names and area names.

### How much does an AI receptionist cost?

Published figures vary widely: ConnectAI lists ₹800 a month plus ₹4 a minute, and Vrisic estimates $10,000 to $75,000 for custom builds, both as read on 20 November 2026. Elyx engagements start from {{start}}, excluding GST, and each project is quoted in writing after a call.
