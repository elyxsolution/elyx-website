The AI Overviews impact on small business is real for broad "what is" and "how to" searches and much smaller for searches where someone wants to hire, buy or visit. In a Pew Research Center study of 900 U.S. adults, people clicked a result on 8% of visits that showed an AI summary and 15% of visits that did not, while Google says total organic clicks have been relatively stable. The honest qualification: the best click data is American and from early 2025, so it shows direction, not what your own site will lose.

- **What still clicks.** Service-plus-place, brand and near-me searches trigger a summary far less often than who, what, when and why questions (8% against 60% in Pew's data).
- **Local.** Google says local ranking rests on relevance, distance and popularity, so your Business Profile does work a blog post cannot.
- **What to change.** Protect the pages that bring enquiries, add first-hand proof to thin explainer posts and count enquiries, not rankings.
- **What to skip.** Google says you need no llms.txt file, special schema or AI-specific markup.

## What Google's AI Overviews Are and Whether They Reach India

AI Overviews are AI-written summaries that Google places above the usual results for some searches, with links to supporting pages. Google's documentation says they are shown only when they add something beyond classic Search, which is why they often do not trigger. AI Mode is a separate, chat-style experience for longer questions, and the two can show different links.

India is on Google's own list. Its expansion announcement named the United Kingdom, India, Japan, Indonesia, Mexico and Brazil, with local language support in each. The post does not list which Indian languages, and Nofeck, a Chennai agency, says English came first and Indian languages later, so test your own searches in your customers' language.

## AI Overviews Impact on Small Business Traffic: What the Evidence Says

The evidence gives a range, not one number: independent browsing data shows fewer clicks when a summary appears, Google says total clicks are stable, and agency estimates run from 5% to 60% depending on the search. The table sorts each claim by what kind of source it is, as read on 20 November 2026.

| Source | Kind | What it says | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pew Research Center, July 2025 (reported by Optihole) | Research | Clicks on 8% of visits with a summary, 15% without; 1% on a link inside the summary | 900 U.S. adults, March 2025 |
| Google, The Keyword, August 2025 (reported by Optihole) | Official | Total organic click volume "relatively stable" year over year | Google's own summary, no site-level data |
| Google Search Central | Official | Clicks from pages with AI Overviews are higher quality, with more time on site | No figure given |
| Gartner, 2024 (reported by Nofeck) | Forecast | Search engine volume to fall about 25% by 2026 | A prediction; my sources do not confirm it happened |
| Nofeck | Practitioner | 20% to 60% fewer organic clicks where an overview shows | "SEO-platform studies", unnamed |
| Richwood Marketing | Practitioner | 20% to 40% fewer clicks on informational queries, 5% to 15% on local ones | Ohio agency, no method stated |

Pew's gap between 8% and 15% is not a loss forecast for your site, because your loss depends on how many of your searches show a summary.

### A worked example: Pew's click rates applied to your search mix

The arithmetic here is mine, not Pew's. With a 15% click rate without a summary and 8% with one, clicks per visit fall by (15 - 8) / 15 = 47% when a summary shows.

Now weight by how often a summary shows. If your visitors search like the average Pew user, 18% of searches show one: 0.18 x 8 + 0.82 x 15 = 13.74%, a fall of (15 - 13.74) / 15 = 8%. If every visitor arrives through who, what, when or why questions, where Pew found 60%: 0.60 x 8 + 0.40 x 15 = 10.8%, a fall of 28%. If your searches resemble the one- or two-word bucket (8% showed a summary), which is my assumption for "plumber Mysuru": 0.08 x 8 + 0.92 x 15 = 14.44%, a fall of 4%.

The same click data gives a 4% fall for one business and 28% for another. This also assumes Pew's U.S. rates hold in India, which no source I have confirms.

## What Still Clicks: Searches Where People Want to Hire, Buy or Visit

Short, high-intent searches still send clicks because a summary rarely appears for them and the person wants to act. Pew's data, as reported by Optihole, shows how steeply the trigger rate climbs with the length and form of the search.

| Type of search (Pew, U.S., March 2025) | Share that showed an AI summary |
|---|---|
| One- or two-word searches | 8% |
| All searches in the study | 18% |
| Full sentences (noun and verb) | 36% |
| Searches of 10 or more words | 53% |
| Searches starting with who, what, when or why | 60% |

Intuitive Digital, a U.S. agency, says informational questions may become zero-click searches while high-intent searches still need a click to act, and Richwood rates transactional and branded queries as minimal impact. Both are practitioner opinion, consistent with Pew but not extra proof.

My reading is that your money pages (a service page, a location page, a price page) sit in the low-exposure group, while your "what is" posts sit in the high one. ## Local Results: Maps, Business Profile and the Near-Me Question

Local results run on their own ranking factors, so your Google Business Profile matters whatever an AI Overview does above it. Google Business Profile Help, quoted by Optihole, says local results rest mainly on relevance, distance and popularity and that you cannot request or pay for a better local ranking. Google's own AI guidance says to keep Business Profile and Merchant Center information up to date.

Sources disagree on how often local searches show a summary. Optihole and Richwood say near-me searches trigger one less often (Richwood's estimate is 5% to 15%, for U.S. local service queries, with no method stated), while Nofeck says local business queries increasingly show one and compete with the map pack. I could not settle this, so search your own five main queries.

Richwood adds that summaries take more screen space on mobile, which would make the map pack more valuable. That is its claim, not Google's. It also cites a 41% rise in Business Profile actions that I could not trace to a Google page, so I do not rely on it.

For India, Nofeck names Justdial and IndiaMART listings among the local signals to keep consistent. Also search your own business name and read what the results say about you, including reviews.

## What Google Says You Need, and What You Can Skip

Google says a page needs only to be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet to appear as a supporting link, with no additional technical requirements. Its documentation lists the ordinary fundamentals: crawling allowed in robots.txt and by any CDN or host, content reachable through internal links, a good page experience, important content in text, supporting images and video, structured data that matches the visible text, and current Business Profile and Merchant Center details. The controls `nosnippet`, `data-nosnippet`, `max-snippet` and `noindex` limit what is shown, and more restrictive settings limit how your content is featured.

Advice from agencies and from Google does not always match. The table labels each claim.

| Claim | Label | What the sources say |
|---|---|---|
| Add an llms.txt file | Practitioner (Nofeck) against official | Google, quoted by Optihole, says no AI text files or special schema are needed and Search ignores llms.txt |
| FAQ schema is the most direct signal | Practitioner (Richwood) against official | Google's documentation asks only that markup match the visible page |
| Write unique, non-commodity content | Official | Google Search Central Blog, May 2025 |
| Lead each section with a direct answer | Practitioner | Richwood and Nofeck both advise it; Google gives no rule |
| Make a page for every question variant | Official: do not | Google, quoted by Optihole, calls it scaled content abuse when done to manipulate rankings or AI answers |

## Measure First: A Search Console Check in Four Steps

You can tell whether AI Overviews affect you by comparing clicks and impressions per page in Search Console, because Google counts AI feature traffic in the Performance report under the Web search type. Optihole and Nofeck both suggest the same comparison.

1. **Compare 12 months by page.** Open the Performance report and compare clicks and impressions for your top pages against the same months a year earlier.
2. **Flag the pattern.** Impressions flat or rising while clicks fall is a likely sign of a summary, not proof, since seasonality and ranking moves cause it too.
3. **Check the queries.** See whether the flagged pages earn their impressions from question-style searches or from service-plus-place ones.
4. **Search them yourself.** On a phone, in a private window, run your main searches in English and your customers' language, and note whether a summary shows and who is cited.

Then look at enquiries. Google suggests tracking conversions, such as sales, signups or information lookups about your business, not clicks alone. Set up calls, WhatsApp taps and form submissions as events in GA4.

## What to Change: Six Moves in Priority Order

Change the pages and profiles that bring enquiries first, then rework the explainer posts that a summary can replace.

1. **Complete the money pages.** Make service, product and location pages indexed, fast and complete: areas served, process, FAQs, photos and a call or WhatsApp button. [The pages a small business site needs](/blog/what-pages-should-a-business-website-have/) lists the set.
2. **Tighten the Business Profile.** Check categories, hours, services, photos and review replies, and keep name, address and phone identical on every listing.
3. **Rework thin explainers.** Add what a summary cannot copy, such as your own photos, local prices, cases or an expert view, or merge several thin posts into one stronger page. [Writing website copy](/blog/website-copywriting-tips/) covers answer-first writing.
4. **Show first-hand experience.** Name the person who did the work and describe your process. Google, as reported by Optihole, says people increasingly click for first-hand perspectives.
5. **Fix the phone experience.** Google asks for a good page experience across devices, and [a phone-first build](/blog/mobile-friendly-website/) is where that starts.
6. **Count enquiries.** Report calls, WhatsApp taps and forms per month, so a drop in clicks is judged against leads.

## Ads in AI Overviews: What Changes If You Run Google Ads

If you already run Search, Shopping or Performance Max campaigns, your ads can appear above or below AI Overviews with no new setup. Google's announcement confirms ads above or below. Intuitive Digital adds that text and Shopping ads can show inside the summary, that sensitive categories including healthcare are not eligible there, and that it has no long-term data on whether costs per click will rise.

My sources do not say whether the inside-summary placement is live in India, so check your Google Ads account. Intuitive Digital's two cautions hold regardless: keep geographic targeting inside the area you really serve, and write landing pages for visitors who are still researching.

## FAQ

### How do AI Overviews affect small businesses?

AI Overviews cut clicks mainly on broad question searches and affect searches where people want to hire, buy or visit much less. Pew found clicks on 8% of visits with a summary against 15% without, in U.S. data from March 2025. The effect on your site depends on your search mix, so check Search Console.

### Do AI Overviews reduce website traffic?

For some searches, yes. Pew's U.S. data shows fewer clicks when a summary appears, while Google says total organic click volume has been relatively stable.

### Can a small business appear inside AI Overviews?

Yes, if the page is indexed and eligible to show with a snippet, which Google says is the only technical requirement. Small sites are cited when they answer a specific question better than others. Nobody can guarantee a citation.

### Do I need llms.txt or special schema for AI Overviews?

No. Google, as quoted by Optihole, says you need no machine-readable files, AI text files or special schema.org markup, and that Google Search ignores llms.txt. If you use structured data, it must match the visible text on the page.

### Can I see AI Overviews traffic in Search Console?

Yes, but not as a separate line. Google says sites appearing in AI features are counted in the Performance report under the Web search type. Look for pages whose impressions hold while clicks fall.

### Are local searches affected as much as informational ones?

Probably less, but the sources disagree. Optihole and Richwood say near-me searches show a summary less often, while Nofeck says local business queries increasingly show one. Test your own searches and watch your map and call data.

### Should I stop blogging because of AI Overviews?

No, but change what you publish. Optihole advises first-hand experience, local detail and real data, and rework or merging for thin "what is" posts.

### Are AI Overviews available in India and in Indian languages?

Google's announcement named India among six new countries, with local language support in each. It does not list the Indian languages, so search in your customers' language to see what they see.

### Do ads show inside AI Overviews?

Existing Search, Shopping and Performance Max ads can show above or below them, and Intuitive Digital says text and Shopping ads can also show inside the summary, except in sensitive categories such as healthcare. My sources do not confirm the inside placement in India, so check your Google Ads account.
