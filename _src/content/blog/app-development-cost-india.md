The widest gaps are clinic and fintech apps, where one label hides different products. PS Digitise prices a clinic app as appointments plus patient records at ₹2 to ₹5 lakh, while Ahex prices telemedicine with video consultations at ₹40 to ₹90 lakh. Neither is wrong; they quote different scopes, and 125 divided by 2 is a 62.5 times gap.

Ahex says regulated apps cost more because features are reviewed and documented and the app usually needs penetration testing (a security test by outside specialists). Ask a qualified adviser which health-data and payments rules apply, since none of these pages is a legal source.

## What Pushes the Price Up Inside Each Type

Backend work, user roles and real-time features move an app's price more than screens do. Nevatrix calls the server, database and integrations the "backend iceberg", the part a mock-up never shows, and puts it at ₹0.5 to ₹3 lakh.

- **Backend.** Vola says ₹0.4 to ₹2 lakh. Astha says ₹0.5 to ₹1 lakh on Firebase or AWS, and ₹1.5 to ₹4 lakh for a custom one.
- **Real-time features.** Chat and live tracking add ₹0.5 to ₹1.5 lakh (Nevatrix), and Ahex says they can make an app three to four times dearer.
- **AI features.** Nevatrix and Groot both start at ₹1 lakh.
- **Integrations.** Ahex says a payment gateway can work in a sandbox in two days and need a week to be production-ready.

Login, payments and push alerts are smaller levers that you can scope as separate options. Sysfo's typical split of effort is 5 to 10 percent discovery, 10 to 20 design, 35 to 45 app build, 20 to 30 backend and admin, and 10 to 15 testing and launch.
## Android, iOS and Cross-Platform: Where the Savings Are

A cross-platform build for both stores costs about 30 to 40 percent less than two native apps on most pages, and Vola says 35 to 50 percent. Cross-platform means one codebase, in React Native or Flutter, that runs on Android and iOS. The arithmetic shows the pages agree more than they seem to.

- **Vola's example.** A native build at ₹4 lakh against React Native at ₹2 lakh to ₹2.5 lakh saves (4 − 2.5) ÷ 4 = 37.5 percent to (4 − 2) ÷ 4 = 50 percent.
- **Nevatrix's version.** It says two native builds cost 40 to 50 percent more than one cross-platform build. A 30 percent saving makes native 1 ÷ 0.7 = 1.43 times dearer, and a 40 percent saving makes it 1.67 times, so compare savings with savings.

On one platform, the pages disagree. Groot prices a basic Android app at ₹0.5 to ₹1 lakh and the same iOS app at ₹0.8 to ₹1.5 lakh, while Nevatrix says Android or iOS alone costs about the same. Ask bidders to price both.

Nevatrix says Android holds the large majority of India's market, citing StatCounter; PS Digitise says above 95 percent, a vendor claim to check. PS Digitise and Zethic put the React Native and Flutter gap at 5 to 10 percent but disagree on which is cheaper, so it should not decide your build.

- **Choose cross-platform when** it is a business app with forms, payments and notifications, and you want both stores (Nevatrix).
- **Choose native when** you need deep device access, heavy background work, hardware such as POS terminals, AR, or you extend an existing native app (Ahex, Nevatrix).
- **Choose Android first when** your budget allows one store and your customers mostly use Android (Nevatrix, Sysfo).

## App Store Fees and Review Delays

The two store fees are small beside the build: Apple charges $99 a year and Google Play a $25 one-time registration, as of 17 November 2026 per Nevatrix and Astha. PS Digitise and Astha convert the dollars into rupees at different rates, so this article leaves them in dollars. Year one is $99 plus $25, or $124, and Apple's fee recurs.

Confirm both fees on Apple's and Google's own pages before you budget, because the research does not include them. The delays cost more than the fees, and Nevatrix lists four traps:

- **Google Play testing.** A new personal developer account must run a closed test with opted-in testers before publishing, which can add two weeks or more.
- **Review time.** Allow one to two weeks per store and budget for one rejection round.
- **Account deletion.** Apple requires in-app account deletion for apps with sign-up, and its absence is a common rejection reason.
- **Ownership.** Keep the store accounts and source code in your company's name.

## App Maintenance Cost After Launch

Maintenance usually costs 15 to 20 percent of the build price each year (PS Digitise, Sysfo, Groot), although Astha and Zethic say up to 25 percent and Nevatrix prices it by the month. Zethic lists bug fixes, yearly OS compatibility updates, hosting, security patches and feature updates.

Here is the arithmetic on Astha's own example of a ₹5 lakh build:

- **Percentage method.** 15 percent of 5 is ₹0.75 lakh and 25 percent is ₹1.25 lakh a year.
- **Monthly method.** Nevatrix's ₹0.15 to ₹0.4 lakh a month is 12 × 0.15 = ₹1.8 lakh to 12 × 0.4 = ₹4.8 lakh a year, or 36 to 96 percent of the build.

My reading is that the pages count different things, so ask what the number includes and whether hosting and API fees sit inside it. Skipping it is costlier: Nevatrix says an app left alone for 12 to 18 months starts to break or gets flagged by the stores.

One salary buys one person, and an app also needs design, backend and testing.

## A First-Year Budget, Worked Through

A first-year budget is the build price plus maintenance, store fees, hosting and API costs, and a reserve for changes. Nevatrix says these extras often equal 30 to 60 percent of the build in year one. The example uses assumed inputs, not a quote.

1. **Build.** An assumed cross-platform business app at ₹4 lakh.
2. **Maintenance.** 15 to 20 percent (PS Digitise, Sysfo) is 0.15 × 4 = ₹0.6 lakh to 0.20 × 4 = ₹0.8 lakh.
3. **Year-one additions.** PS Digitise advises about 20 percent of the build for requested changes, which is ₹0.8 lakh.
4. **Store fees.** $99 plus $25, or $124.
5. **Hosting, SMS, maps and gateway fees.** Take these from each bidder's quote, as PS Digitise lists them as monthly lines.

The first three lines total 4 + 0.6 + 0.8 = ₹5.4 lakh, or 4 + 0.8 + 0.8 = ₹5.6 lakh. That is 35 to 40 percent above the build, before store fees and hosting, which sits inside Nevatrix's 30 to 60 percent.

## How to Read an App Quote

A quote is comparable only when it lists each phase, names its exclusions and states the support period. Nevatrix and Zethic both say a bare total cannot be compared.

Copy this checklist into your request:

- [ ] Design, app build, backend, testing and store submission are separate lines.
- [ ] Exclusions are named: hosting, third-party fees, user testing, support.
- [ ] The support period is stated. Zethic says 30 to 90 days is common.
- [ ] Source code and documentation are handed over, with code and store accounts in your company's name.
- [ ] The revision policy says what counts as out of scope.
- [ ] Maintenance is its own line, and the same feature list went to three bidders (Nevatrix).

## What Usually Goes Wrong With App Budgets

Most overruns come from a quote that priced less than the founder assumed, then from scope that moved. A total and an hours figure cannot be judged without the scope behind them. The causes come from the pages above, and the fixes are my reading.

| What goes wrong | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Budgeting from the lowest quote | It prices one platform, thin backend, no QA (Ahex) | Ask what is excluded first |
| Forgetting backend and admin | Mock-ups show screens only (Nevatrix, Sysfo) | Price them as lines |
| Changing scope mid-build | Requirements not final (Sysfo) | Price MVP and phase two apart |
| Buying once, not yearly | Yearly OS releases (Nevatrix) | Budget year one, not launch |
| Losing control of code | The vendor holds the accounts (Nevatrix) | Name your company on both |
