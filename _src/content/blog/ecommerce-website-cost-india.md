The ecommerce website cost India agencies quote in 2026 runs from about ₹25,000 for a configured Shopify store to ₹10 lakh or more for a custom build, and the route you choose explains most of the gap. In the four sources that price each route separately, Shopify and WooCommerce stores sit between roughly ₹0.2 lakh and ₹4 lakh, and custom stores start at ₹2 lakh or more. The qualification is that every source is a studio or agency that sells builds, so these ranges are market signals, not quotes.

- **Setup.** Shopify and WooCommerce overlap on build cost, so the real gap is in what each route charges after launch.
- **Monthly fees.** Shopify bills a plan, apps and an extra 2 percent on sales. WooCommerce bills hosting and upkeep. Custom bills hosting and maintenance.
- **Gateway charges.** Expect about 2 percent plus 18 percent GST on that fee on every route, with UPI reported as cheaper.
- **Custom.** In my break-even model it pays back within about two years only above roughly ₹10 lakh a month in sales.

## How the Three Routes Are Compared

Shopify, WooCommerce and a custom build are compared here on five criteria: build cost, monthly platform cost, charges on each sale, upkeep effort and the ceiling on what you can change.

All figures are as reported by the sources on 16 November 2026. Plan, app and gateway prices change, and Shopify's and WooCommerce's own pages were not part of the evidence, so check Shopify's India pricing page and your gateway's page before you decide.

Each route is stronger somewhere. Shopify launches fastest with the least upkeep. WooCommerce has no subscription and no per-sale platform fee. A custom store removes both and has no ceiling on changes.

The ranges overlap heavily, which is why a single "Shopify price" does not exist. NexaEx gives one range for both Shopify and WooCommerce.

The table shows no cheaper platform at build time. Zethic and SiteYatra put WooCommerce's floor above Shopify's, and CodeVibe puts it below. My reading is that the gap is smaller than the gap between two vendors' quotes.

## Monthly and Yearly Running Costs

Running costs differ by route more than build costs do: Shopify charges every month, WooCommerce charges for hosting and upkeep, and a custom store charges mostly for hosting and maintenance. The table gives two sources that publish running figures by route. Numbers are in rupees, and the unit is in the first column.

| Source and unit | Shopify | WooCommerce | Custom |
|---|---|---|---|
| SiteYatra, per year | 24,000 to 45,000 | 8,000 to 30,000 | 30,000 to 1.2 lakh |
| Zethic, per month | 2,000 to 7,000 plus apps | 1,000 to 4,000 hosting | 5,000 to 30,000+ |

Apps are the quiet line on Shopify. SiteYatra's example is four apps at about a thousand rupees each a month, which is ₹48,000 a year. Zethic says most stores add three to eight paid apps, for reviews, email, abandoned carts and upsells.

Maintenance is where the sources disagree most. NexaEx puts it at 15 to 25 percent of the build cost each year and CodeVibe at 15 to 20 percent, which on a ₹6 lakh build is ₹90,000 to ₹1.5 lakh. SiteYatra's monthly figure of ₹2,000 to ₹10,000 works out to ₹24,000 to ₹1.2 lakh a year. Get maintenance priced in writing before launch.

## Payment Gateway Charges for Indian Stores

Indian payment gateways charge about 2 percent of each sale plus 18 percent GST on that fee, according to CodeVibe and SiteYatra, and Shopify adds its own charge on top because Shopify Payments is unavailable in India. Zethic lists Razorpay at 2 percent (citing Razorpay's pricing page), PayU at 1.99 percent and Cashfree at 1.75 percent. Larwell gives 2 to 3 percent and Orange Mantra 1.5 to 3 percent, so rates vary by plan and method.

For Shopify's own fee, CodeVibe reports 2 percent on the Basic plan, 1 percent on Grow and 0.6 percent on Advanced when a third-party gateway is used. Zethic confirms the 2 percent on Basic. WooCommerce and a custom store have no platform fee on sales, only the gateway's.

UPI is where the sources disagree. Zethic says UPI usually costs 0.5 to 1 percent, NexaEx says most merchants pay near zero, and Kode Studio says only that it is often cheaper. Ask your gateway for its current UPI rate in writing, because this is a payments topic and I give no rule here.

The table assumes every sale is paid through the gateway at 2 percent, with GST on the fee. The gateway fee is sales × 0.02 × 1.18 and Shopify's extra fee is sales × 0.02. I added no GST to Shopify's fee because the sources do not say it applies.

| Monthly sales (₹ lakh) | Gateway fee with GST (₹ a month) | Shopify Basic extra fee (₹ a month) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 4,720 | 4,000 |
| 4 | 9,440 | 8,000 |
| 10 | 23,600 | 20,000 |

At ₹10 lakh a month, Zethic puts gateway fees at ₹15,000 to ₹20,000, which matches 2 percent before GST. UPI sales would lower both columns.

## Hidden Costs and What Usually Goes Wrong

Most budget overruns come from items a headline quote leaves out: catalogue entry, photography, GST invoicing, courier setup, cash-on-delivery losses and the traffic budget. The table sets each cause against a fix, with the source that names it.

| What goes wrong | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Catalogue entry overruns | SiteYatra: photography and data entry are the biggest overrun | List SKUs and variants first. Pay per 100 SKUs |
| "Complete store" at ₹25,000 | NexaEx: it is an unmodified theme | Ask for a deliverables list |
| GST invoicing added late | Zethic budgets 15 to 40 thousand rupees to add it | See a live GST invoice and credit-note demo |
| Cash-on-delivery returns | NexaEx: returns can erase the margin | Verify COD orders. Model return losses |
| Whole budget spent on the site | NexaEx: a ₹6 lakh site with no ad budget loses | Split build and traffic money on purpose |
| Launch before a festival | NexaEx: debugging happens under peak traffic | Go live weeks before the peak |
| Slow mobile pages | CodeVibe, citing web.dev: 0.1 second faster lifted conversions 8.4% | Compress images. Test on a mid-range phone |

Time is the other surprise. Kode Studio says a Shopify store takes 10 to 14 working days, NexaEx says 3 to 6 weeks for a platform store and 10 to 16 for custom, and Larwell says 6 to 12 weeks for custom. NexaEx adds that product data (photos, descriptions, weights, HSN codes) is on your critical path, and that gateway KYC for a new business should start in week one.

## When Custom Pays Off: The Break-Even Maths

A custom store pays off against Shopify when the fees you stop paying recover the extra build cost, and in this model that takes about two years at ₹10 lakh a month in sales and not within any realistic time at ₹2 lakh. The model is mine, built from sourced inputs you can replace with your own quote.

- **Build gap.** Custom at ₹6 lakh (the bottom of NexaEx's custom range) minus platform at ₹2.5 lakh (the top of its platform range) is ₹3.5 lakh. That is the narrowest gap in its ranges, so it favours custom.
- **Shopify yearly cost.** Basic plan about ₹24,000 (SiteYatra), four apps ₹48,000 (SiteYatra), and 2 percent of sales as the extra fee. The fixed part is ₹72,000.
- **Custom yearly cost.** Hosting ₹24,000, the low end of NexaEx's range, plus maintenance at 15 percent of ₹6 lakh, which is ₹90,000. The total is ₹1,14,000.
- **Left out.** The gateway fee, which every route pays, and the domain.

The yearly saving is ₹72,000 + (0.24 × monthly sales) − ₹1,14,000, which simplifies to 0.24 × monthly sales − ₹42,000. The fee saving starts at monthly sales of ₹42,000 ÷ 0.24 = ₹1.75 lakh. Years to recover the build gap is ₹3.5 lakh divided by the saving.

| Monthly sales (₹ lakh) | Yearly saving (₹) | Years to recover |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 6,000 | 58 |
| 4 | 54,000 | 6.5 |
| 10 | 1,98,000 | 1.8 |
| 15 | 3,18,000 | 1.1 |

The result is sensitive. At NexaEx's 25 percent maintenance, custom costs ₹1,74,000 a year, the ₹4 lakh case turns negative and the ₹10 lakh case takes about 2.5 years.

The model ignores WooCommerce, which has no platform fee, so against WooCommerce a custom build can only pay off through logic, speed or integrations. It also ignores migration cost.

The sources put the switch point in very different places, and all of them sell builds.

| Source | Where it puts the switch | Note |
|---|---|---|
| NexaEx | Custom wins past ₹10 to 15 lakh a month | At ₹8 lakh it shows ₹6 to 9 lakh platform, ₹11 to 12 lakh custom over three years |
| Zethic | Custom or headless above ₹50 lakh monthly GMV | GMV is gross sales value |
| SiteYatra | Platforms serve D2C brands under ₹5 crore a year | ₹5 crore ÷ 12 is about ₹41.7 lakh a month |
| Kode Studio | Custom is often cheapest over two to three years | No sales figure given |

My reading: the thresholds span about five times, so your own build quote and feature needs matter more than any single number.

## Malnad Stories: What a Custom Build Looked Like

Malnad Stories is the studio's example of a store where custom was the point: customers upload their own photographs, design an album, pay online and expect a finished printed product. The [Malnad Stories case study](/work/malnad-stories/) describes a full-stack photo-album e-commerce platform for trek and travel memories. It lists a custom printing flow, secure payments for Indian customers, cloud storage for photos, and a launch-ready front end, back end, database and admin tooling built by one team.

My reading is that the store itself was the logic. A standard cart sells products someone else photographed, while here the product is made from the customer's upload, so upload, storage and print ordering are the store. That fits SiteYatra's rule that custom makes sense when it models something no plugin supports.

The case page gives no price, order volume or running cost. So it shows a fit, not a cost benchmark, and I make no claim about whether a Shopify or WooCommerce app could have covered it.

Test your own store with three questions:

- **Does the customer create the product?** Uploads, personalisation and design tools point towards custom.
- **Does pricing follow rules a plugin cannot hold?** Buyer-specific tiers, unusual subscriptions and credit terms do (SiteYatra, NexaEx).
- **Must stock sync live with a warehouse or POS?** NexaEx says inventory syncing is where simple projects quietly double.

## Choosing Shopify, WooCommerce or Custom

Choose Shopify for the fastest launch with the least upkeep, WooCommerce when you want to own the stack and publish content, and custom when your business logic is the product. The table restates the sources' advice.

| Route | Choose it when | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Shopify | Under a few hundred SKUs, standard checkout, a non-technical team | Plan, apps and the extra 2% add up |
| WooCommerce | Content-led, SEO-focused brand or an existing WordPress site | You own updates, security and hosting speed |
| Custom | Buyer pricing tiers, odd subscriptions, live stock sync | Highest build cost and the longest timeline |

NexaEx offers one rule: if a platform covers 80 percent of your needs, start there and rebuild custom when workarounds cost real money. Zethic and SiteYatra say most Indian D2C brands are well served by Shopify or WooCommerce.

## What to Do Next

Write down your numbers and answers first, then ask each vendor for an itemised quote you can compare. Copy this checklist:

- [ ] SKUs and variants at launch, and how many are photographed.
- [ ] Payment methods you need: UPI, cards, cash on delivery.
- [ ] Your gateway's current fees by method, in writing.
- [ ] Courier or aggregator, and whether COD needs verification.
- [ ] Accounting or inventory software to connect.
- [ ] Monthly sales you expect, run through the break-even table above.
- [ ] A deliverables list: payment setup, product upload, GST invoice output, training.
- [ ] A bug-fix window (SiteYatra calls two to four weeks normal) and ownership of code and hosting in writing.
- [ ] A launch date weeks before the festival peak, with gateway KYC applied in week one.

## FAQ

### How much does an ecommerce website cost in India?

An ecommerce website in India costs about ₹25,000 for a configured Shopify store and up to ₹10 lakh or more for a custom build, according to SiteYatra's 2026 ranges. The studio's own draft range is {{price:ecommerce}}, indicative and never a quote.

### Is Shopify or WooCommerce cheaper for an Indian store?

Neither is cheaper at build time in the sources: Zethic and SiteYatra put WooCommerce's floor above Shopify's and CodeVibe puts it below. After launch, Shopify charges a plan, apps and an extra fee on sales, while WooCommerce charges for hosting and upkeep.

### What are the ongoing costs of running an ecommerce website?

Ongoing costs are the platform plan or hosting, apps or plugins, maintenance, the gateway fee and the domain. SiteYatra's yearly running figures are 24,000 to 45,000 rupees for Shopify, 8,000 to 30,000 for WooCommerce and 30,000 to 1.2 lakh for custom, before gateway fees.

### How long does it take to build an ecommerce website?

A Shopify store typically takes 10 to 14 working days according to Kode Studio, and a custom storefront 10 to 16 weeks according to NexaEx. Product data and gateway approval are usually the slowest parts. The studio's draft timeline for an e-commerce store is {{weeks:ecommerce}}.

### Why do ecommerce website quotes vary so much?

Quotes vary because vendors price different scopes under the same label. Catalogue size, design depth, integrations, GST invoicing and who builds it all change the number.

### Does Shopify charge extra for payment gateways in India?

Yes, Shopify charges an extra fee when a third-party gateway is used, and Shopify Payments is unavailable in India as of 2026 per Zethic. CodeVibe reports 2 percent on Basic, 1 percent on Grow and 0.6 percent on Advanced. Check Shopify's current India pricing before relying on these.

### When does a custom ecommerce website pay off?

A custom store pays off when your business logic is the product or when fee savings recover the extra build cost. In my model that takes about 1.8 years at ₹10 lakh a month in sales against Shopify. Against WooCommerce there are no platform fees to save.

### What are the biggest ecommerce websites in India?

The sources give no ranked list, and the names they mention are marketplaces and large retailers such as Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho and Myntra. A small brand should not copy their stack.
