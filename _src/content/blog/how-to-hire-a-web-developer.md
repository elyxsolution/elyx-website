How to hire a web developer in India without getting burned: write a one-page brief, ask for live sites and a client you can phone, get a written scope with a price and payment milestones, and make sure the code, domain and hosting accounts are yours from day one. The honest qualification is that the pages we read are all from companies that earn money when you hire, so the guidance below mixes their published advice with my own reading, and none of it is legal advice.

- **Route.** A freelancer suits a focused build, an agency a large programme, and a marketplace only helps you find people.
- **Questions.** Each of the 12 comes with the answer to look for and the warning sign.
- **Money.** Published hourly rates run from about $10 to over $100, so a low rate means little until you know the hours.
- **Contract.** Scope, milestones, ownership and handover go in writing before the first payment.

## How to Hire a Web Developer: Freelancer, Agency or Marketplace

Hire a freelancer for a focused build with a defined scope, an agency when design, development and project management must run in parallel, and treat a marketplace as a place to find candidates, not a guarantee. The freelancer and agency split comes from Freelancer.com's own hiring guide, read on 17 November 2026.

The table sets out what each route says about itself and what you still have to check.

| Route | Its own pitch | What you still check |
|---|---|---|
| Marketplace (Upwork) | Ratings, reviews, pay only for approved work | Whether reviews describe projects like yours |
| Vetted network (Arc) | Candidates pass technical and communication tests | That is Arc's claim; ask for the same proof |
| Agency (SunTec India) | Full-time staff only, no freelancers, NDA and IP terms | Who exactly writes your code |
| Freelancer | Best for focused, budget-conscious builds | What happens if they fall ill or vanish |

Upwork says 89% of first-time clients complete a contract. That is a completion figure, not a satisfaction figure, and the page gives no method.

My reading: platforms lower the cost of finding candidates, not of choosing wrongly.

## Write the Brief and Pick the Right Kind of Developer

A one-page brief decides the quality of every reply you get: Freelancer.com says a specific brief filters out mismatched developers. Its checklist is a good base, and I have added the points that change a brief for an Indian business.

- **Type of build.** Marketing site, online store, web app or API.
- **Platform.** A named stack or CMS (content management system, the screen where you edit text and photos).
- **Designs and references.** Figma files, wireframes or sites you want to match.
- **Integrations.** Payments, CRM, email tools or custom connections.
- **Launch date and budget range.** Both, even if rough.
- **India additions (my reading).** WhatsApp click-to-chat, UPI and card payments, Google Maps and Business Profile details, Kannada or Hindi pages if your customers need them, and a GST invoice.

Upwork and Freelancer.com split developers into the same kinds, and the split tells you who to hire.

- **Front-end.** Builds what visitors see and tap.
- **Back-end.** Builds databases, logins and server logic in tools such as Python, Node.js or PHP.
- **Full-stack.** Does both; Freelancer.com calls this often the most efficient choice for small to mid-sized projects.
- **CMS specialist.** Customises WordPress, Shopify, Wix or Webflow.
- **Designer.** Produces layouts and mockups, while a developer writes the code; say in the brief if you need both.

## The 12 Questions to Ask Before You Hire

Ask all 12 questions in writing, because a developer who answers in a message has also given you something to refer back to. Freelancer.com lists five interview questions but does not say what a good answer sounds like, and that gap is what this section fills.

The answers come from signals Freelancer.com, Upwork and Arc name: live work, code access, speed awareness, scope first, milestones, references. The warning signs and ownership questions are my reading.

### Proof: Questions 1 to 4

The first four questions test whether the work exists and whether this person did it. Freelancer.com calls live sites, a public code repository and clear talk about trade-offs the strongest signals.

| Question | Answer to look for | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Which live sites like mine can I open, and who can I call? | Live links and a client who picks up | Screenshots only, or "under NDA" for all of it |
| 2. What did you personally build on them? | Named pages, features and code | Vague "we" with no role |
| 3. Can I see code or a repository? | A public repo or a sample on request | Refuses any sample |
| 4. How did those sites score on speed? | Lighthouse or Core Web Vitals results and what was fixed | Never measured |

A developer with real work answers questions 1 and 4 in minutes.

### The Build: Questions 5 to 8

Questions 5 to 8 test the plan and the aftercare. Freelancer.com says a strong proposal explains an approach, raises sensible technical questions and suggests a realistic timeline, rather than agreeing to everything.

| Question | Answer to look for | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| 5. What do you build in what order, and what could delay it? | Stages, a fitting timeline, questions about hosting and content | Instant yes, no questions |
| 6. Which platform would you pick for me, and why? | Trade-offs explained in plain words | Same answer for every brief |
| 7. How do I edit the site myself after launch? | A CMS, documentation, a walkthrough | "Call me for every change" |
| 8. What happens if something breaks after launch? | A named support period and how to reach them | No terms, or "free forever" |

### Money and Ownership: Questions 9 to 12

The last four questions decide who holds the risk, and every one should end in a written answer. Upwork says to agree scope before development begins, and the same rule applies off any platform.

| Question | Answer to look for | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| 9. What is the total price, and what counts as extra? | Written scope, price and a change process | A price with no scope |
| 10. How are payments split? | Milestones tied to deliverables, last part on acceptance | 100% upfront |
| 11. Who owns code, designs, domain and hosting? | You do, from day one | Their name on your domain |
| 12. What do you need from me, and by when? | Content, approvals and dates listed | Nothing asked of you |

## How to Check a Portfolio and References

Check a portfolio by opening each live site on your own phone, asking who built which part, and speaking to one past client yourself. Arc lists reference checks as a step before any offer, and I would not skip it.

1. **Open the live link on mobile data, not Wi-Fi.** The studio's own service page notes that most visitors arrive on a phone, often on patchy 4G, so a slow page is a real finding.
2. **Ask for the developer's own speed results** and compare them with a fresh test of the same page.
3. **Ask what they built.** A team site may credit one designer, one developer and one copywriter, and you need to know which one is applying.
4. **Phone one client.** Ask whether the work matched the written scope, how changes were handled and who answered after launch.
5. **On a marketplace, read the written reviews.** Freelancer.com says comments on deadlines, clean handoffs and post-launch support carry the most weight.

One limit applies: ratings and completion rates on a platform are that platform's own data, so treat them as a starting point.

## Contract Checks Before You Pay Anything

A web developer contract should fix the scope, the price, the payment milestones, who owns what and what happens after launch, in one document both sides confirm in writing. Upwork lets you pay hourly or by milestone and says you only pay for approved work, and Freelancer.com's Milestone Payments release funds when agreed deliverables are met. Off a platform, the contract has to do that job.

Copy this checklist into your agreement:

- [ ] **Scope.** Pages, features, integrations, who writes the copy, and what is out of scope.
- [ ] **Price and GST.** A fixed price or an hours cap, and whether GST is included. The studio's price data notes 18% GST on software services as of November 2026, so a quote of 100 becomes an invoice of 118.
- [ ] **Milestones.** Each payment tied to something you can open and test, with the last part due on acceptance.
- [ ] **Ownership.** Code, design files, domain, hosting and analytics accounts registered in your name.
- [ ] **Revisions.** A stated number of feedback rounds and a priced process for changes after that.
- [ ] **Handover.** Passwords, documentation and a walkthrough of the CMS.
- [ ] **Support.** A named period after launch, what it covers and how to reach them.
- [ ] **Exit.** What each side gets if the work stops halfway, including the files so far.

Scope, milestones, handover and support echo the pages we read; ownership, revisions and exit are my reading. We found no primary legal source, so have a lawyer read the final contract, especially the ownership clause.

## Fixed Price or Hourly: A Decision Rule

Choose a fixed price when you can list what is being built, and hourly only when the scope cannot be written down yet. SunTec India offers hourly, monthly or fixed-rate models, and Freelancer.com says freelancers also take retainers or per-task work for maintenance.

| Situation | Choose | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Landing page or 5 to 10 page site with a page list | Fixed price | The scope can be written down |
| Fixes or an idea you are still exploring | Hourly with a weekly cap | The scope is unknown |
| Updates and security after launch | Monthly retainer | Freelancer.com lists these as retainer work |
| Large app with unclear features | Paid scoping, then fixed | Upwork says agree scope before building |

Here is the arithmetic, with an assumed job length that you should replace. At Upwork's general range of $15 to $50 an hour, a 40-hour job costs 40 times 15, which is $600, up to 40 times 50, which is $2,000. If it runs 60 hours instead, the bill is $900 to $3,000.

The 20 extra hours add $300 to $1,000 on an hourly bill. A fixed price puts that overrun on the developer, which is why a good one asks for the scope first.

## Compare Candidates With a Scorecard

A weighted scorecard turns a hunch into a number you can defend. Give the six criteria weights totalling 100, score each candidate from 1 to 5, multiply each score by its weight divided by 5, and add the results. The scores below are made up to show the method, not drawn from any real candidate.

| Criterion (weight) | Candidate A | Candidate B |
|---|---|---|
| Live similar work (25) | 4, gives 20 | 5, gives 25 |
| Written scope and price (20) | 5, gives 20 | 3, gives 12 |
| Communication (15) | 3, gives 9 | 5, gives 15 |
| Ownership and handover (20) | 5, gives 20 | 2, gives 8 |
| References checked (10) | 4, gives 8 | 5, gives 10 |
| Post-launch support (10) | 3, gives 6 | 5, gives 10 |
| Total (100) | 83 | 80 |

B is the more charming talker and lands within three points of A. Add one rule: anyone scoring 2 or lower on scope or ownership is out, whatever the total, which removes B.

## What Usually Goes Wrong When You Hire a Web Developer

Most bad hires trace back to a missing document, not a missing skill. The causes below come from the evidence above and the fixes are my reading.

| What goes wrong | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Work never matches the idea | Scope lived in chat messages | Approve a written scope before work starts |
| Bill keeps growing | Hourly work with no cap | Fixed price or a weekly cap |
| Cannot move hosts or developers | Domain or hosting in their name | Register every account yourself |
| Hired a designer, got a mockup | Developer and designer roles mixed up | Say which roles the brief needs |
| Cheapest bid wins, then stalls | Rate compared, hours ignored | Compare total price and scope |
| Nobody can edit the site | No CMS or handover | Ask Question 7 and get a walkthrough |

WhatsApp is fine for daily talk in India. Move every scope change into a written note the same day.

## What to Do Next

Run the process once, in order, and decide only when you have written answers.

1. Write the one-page brief from the checklist above.
2. Shortlist three candidates by live work, not by rate.
3. Send each the 12 questions in writing.
4. Open their sites on your phone and phone one client each.
5. Score each candidate on the scorecard and apply the rule on scope and ownership.
6. Sign a contract that covers every box in the checklist.

## FAQ

### How much does it cost to hire a web developer in India?

Published hourly rates range from about $10 on general marketplaces to $60 to $100+ through vetted networks, per Upwork and Arc as read on 17 November 2026. The studio's draft price data gives project ranges through the pricing page, which are indicative and never a quote. Ask every candidate for a written total, not just a rate.

### How do I hire a freelance web developer?

Write a brief, shortlist three developers by live work, ask the 12 questions in writing, check one reference each and sign a contract with milestones. Freelancer.com describes a similar arc: clear brief, careful review of bids, shortlist on portfolio evidence.

### How do I find a website developer for hire?

Start with marketplaces, vetted networks, agencies and referrals from business owners you trust. Upwork, Arc, SunTec India and Freelancer.com each run a route, and each is a seller, so verify the work yourself.

### Should I hire a web designer or a web developer?

Hire a developer if you need the site built and working, and a designer if you need layouts and mockups first. Freelancer.com says a designer produces mockups while a developer writes the code, and that some freelancers do both. Say which you need in the brief.

### When should a business hire a web developer?

Hire when the site is missing, slow, outdated or cannot do something your customers need. Arc says a site tends to outgrow its original structure and may need a revamp. If you have no site and a clear offer, start now.

### How long does it take to build a website?

Freelancer.com says a simple landing page can take a few days, a custom WordPress or Shopify site two to six weeks, and a large web app two to six months. Treat any date as an estimate until it is written down.

### What should a contract with a web developer include?

It should include the scope, price, GST treatment, payment milestones, ownership of code, domain and hosting, revision rounds, handover and post-launch support. These come partly from Upwork and Freelancer.com guidance and partly from my reading. Have a lawyer review it before you sign.

### Is it safer to pay by milestone?

Milestone payments are safer than paying everything upfront, because each payment follows something you can test. Upwork and Freelancer.com both describe milestone or approval-based payment. It only protects you if each milestone is defined in writing.
