## What Is a Landing Page, and How Is It Different From a Website?

A landing page is a single page built for one action, while a website is several pages built to inform a general audience. Mailchimp describes it as a page that stands apart from the rest of your website and asks for one type of action. Unbounce puts it as a map and a destination: the homepage is the map, the landing page is where the visitor was heading.

Mailchimp names two common types: click-through pages, which lead towards a purchase or sign-up, and lead-generation pages, which collect contact details in return for a guide or consultation. A coaching centre that runs an "IELTS coaching" ad usually wants the second type: a page that ends in a demo-class request.

## Landing Page Best Practices in 2026: What Four Guides Agree On

The guides agree on six practices: match the ad, keep one goal, make the action specific, show proof, load fast on a phone and keep testing. The table counts which guide names each practice, based on the headings and text I read on 17 November 2026. A fifth result, Wix's page, returned only menu text, so it is not counted.

| Practice | Named by | What to check on your page |
|---|---|---|
| Match the ad | Unbounce, Mailchimp, Framer | Does the headline repeat the words of the ad? |
| One goal, few exits | Unbounce, Mailchimp, HubSpot, Framer | Count the links that leave the page |
| A specific action | Unbounce, Mailchimp, HubSpot, Framer | Does the button say what happens next? |

## Hero and Offer: Pass the Five-Second Test

A hero section works when a stranger can say what you offer and why it matters after five seconds, which is Framer's test for a good page. Framer lists four things that must show without scrolling: a headline, benefit-led copy, believable proof and a low-friction way to act. Unbounce adds that too much above the fold can hide the button.

Message match comes first. Unbounce gives the example of an ad for retirement communities that lands on a page about luxury condos, which sends visitors away. Mailchimp says the page need not copy the ad headline, only tell visitors they are in the right place.

Here is a copyable headline pattern. It is my template, not a tested formula:

1. **Name the thing.** "IELTS coaching in Mysuru" instead of a brand slogan.
2. **Name the reader.** "For working professionals and final-year students."
3. **Name an outcome you can document.** Only what you could show a sceptical parent.
4. **Name the next step.** "Book a free demo class on WhatsApp."

Mailchimp advises leading with benefits to the visitor, not with why you are the best company. HubSpot's author drafts copy as four bullets (problem, solution, proof, call to action) before polishing the voice. Unbounce suggests asking someone unfamiliar with your business to read the headline and say what you offer.

If your ads run in Kannada or Hindi, my reading is that the headline should match the ad's language too. Multiple languages are scoped as a separate option, so a second-language page has its own cost.

## One Action: Call to Action, Form and WhatsApp Button

A landing page works best when it asks for one specific action and makes it easy on a thumb. HubSpot's author reports that specific buttons such as "Get the guide" tend to beat vague ones such as "Learn more", and Framer names "Learn more" as a weak call to action. Mailchimp suggests changing "submit" to something like "let's talk" for a friendlier tone.

The table sets vague buttons beside specific ones for an Indian local business. The right-hand column is my own untested illustration.

| Vague button | Specific button |
|---|---|
| Submit | Book my free demo class |
| Learn more | Check batch dates on WhatsApp |
| Contact us | Get the fee sheet on WhatsApp |

For forms, Unbounce recommends splitting a long form into steps, starting with easy questions, and letting people choose from a list instead of typing. Framer says to put the highest-friction step last, and to add short reassurance near the button, such as "no credit card required". In India the equivalent is a line you can honestly keep, such as "No fee to book the demo".

The studio's coaching-centre page recommends a short form and a WhatsApp button above the fold, and a dedicated page that does one thing for ad traffic.

Tap size matters on a phone. web.dev's sign-in form guidance, which applies to any form, cites Android guidance of 7 to 10 mm per touch target, Apple's 48 by 48 px and the W3C's 44 by 44 CSS pixels. It suggests about 15 px of padding on mobile inputs and buttons, and `type="tel"` on a phone-number field so the numeric keypad appears.

## Proof: Real Examples and What To Borrow

Proof works when it is specific and checkable: a full name, a number with a unit, a real photo or a named customer. Unbounce says reviews from "Jane Doe" and "Satisfied Customer" convince nobody, and recommends names, job titles, places and portraits. Framer lists awards, case studies, logos, ratings and video testimonials, and says to choose the formats your audience trusts.

The table sets six pages described by Mailchimp and Framer beside what a small Indian business can borrow. I did not open those pages myself, so each description is the publisher's.

| Page | What the publisher says it does | Borrow this |
|---|---|---|
| Airbnb (Mailchimp) | Lets visitors search at once, shows real guest comments | Put the action first, then reviews |
| BarkBox (Mailchimp) | Offer upfront, plus "over 2 million dogs" served | Use a count you can verify |
| ClickUp (Mailchimp) | Clear headline on what the software does, customer names | Say what you do in plain words |
| Flighty (Framer) | Passes the five-second test, two awards above the fold | Show one credible mark early |
| Sync (Framer) | Demonstrates results and offers a free try | Show the outcome, not a claim |
| Acctual (Framer) | Concrete example plus "5,000+ businesses" | Pair a story with a count |

Most of those six are global brands, so copy the method and not the scale. A coaching centre has no logo wall, but it can show named faculty, batch sizes and results it can document. The studio's coaching-centre page warns that Indian regulators have issued guidelines on misleading coaching advertisements, including selection and rank claims, so keep every claim provable and confirm the current rules with a qualified professional.

## Speed and Phone Layout: Where Paid Traffic Is Won or Wasted

A landing page must load quickly and read well on a phone, because Unbounce reports that 83% of landing page visits happen on mobile devices, citing its 2024 Conversion Benchmark Report. It also cites its Page Speed Report for Marketers, in which 70% of consumers say loading time affects their desire to buy, and warns that pages taking more than 3 seconds on mobile lose many visitors.

Here is the arithmetic, applied to an example of mine. Suppose an ad sends 1,000 clicks. If Unbounce's 83% held for your page, 1,000 × 0.83 = 830 visitors are on a phone. The share for Indian audiences is not in any source I read, so check your own analytics before trusting that 83%.

The studio's web development page says most visitors arrive on a phone, often over patchy 4G, and Pinnacle's case study records lean pages built for mobile. These checks come from the sources:

- **Load.** Open the page on mobile data, not office Wi-Fi. Unbounce says to drop elements with no purpose and optimise images.
- **Tap.** Press every button with a thumb, as web.dev advises.
- **Text.** web.dev suggests about 16 px on desktop and roughly 20 px on mobile for form text.
- **Button.** HubSpot's author checks that the call to action is visible at once on the phone.

## Navigation, Paid Traffic and SEO: One Page or Two?

Remove the navigation on a page that paid ads send traffic to, and keep it when organic search is the main source. Framer says that if the page is designed for organic traffic you can probably keep the main navbar, but that paid traffic deserves a page with every exit removed. Unbounce says a paid page should be short, direct and focused, while an organic page often covers a topic more fully, which can lower its conversion rate.

The table is my decision rule built from those two statements. It is practitioner opinion, and I found no Google documentation on landing pages in the research.

| Choose this when | Navigation | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Paid ads are the main source | Remove it | Short, one action |
| Search is the main source | Keep a simple menu | Fuller, answers the search |
| Both feed one page | Test two versions | Start with the shorter one |

Unbounce adds that a conversion page can still target keywords that match what searchers want, and Mailchimp says to optimise even ad-led pages for search. Mailchimp and Framer also say to build separate pages for separate audiences, so a coaching centre advertising both GRE and IELTS would build two pages.

## How To Measure and Test a Landing Page

A landing page is a hypothesis, and Framer's measurement list names conversion rate, bounce rate, form abandonment, scroll depth and click-through rate as the numbers to watch. Unbounce also names time on page and form drop-off. HubSpot's author reviews top pages every quarter.

Count before you compare. Take 1,000 ad clicks. At a 2% conversion rate that is 1,000 × 0.02 = 20 enquiries, and at 4% it is 1,000 × 0.04 = 40.

The same ad spend then buys twice as many enquiries, which halves the cost per enquiry. These rates are invented to show the arithmetic and are not benchmarks. Mailchimp reports that landing pages have bounce rates of 60 to 90%, without naming a study.

Mailchimp advises testing one element at a time, with enough views to form an opinion. Framer warns that most tests fail because they change surface details such as button colours and not the reason people act. My reading: a gap of 20 against 25 enquiries on 1,000 clicks is too small to call, so on a small budget change the offer or headline, which can move the count more.

The studio's coaching-centre page says it sets up GA4 events for form submissions, WhatsApp clicks and calls, tagged by page and campaign. Without those events you cannot tell which ad produced which enquiry.

## What Usually Goes Wrong With Landing Pages

Landing pages usually fail in a few repeatable places. The table pairs each failure with its cause and a fix, drawn from the sources and the studio's own pages.

| Mistake | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Visitors leave at once | Headline does not echo the ad | Rewrite it around the ad's words |
| Several competing buttons | Menu, footer links and extras remain | Cut to one action |
| Leads disappear | Form not connected (a week of leads lost, says HubSpot's author) | Submit a test and check it arrives |
| Generic photos | Stock images that explain nothing | Real photos of the place and team |
| Claims nobody can verify | Rank or result claims without proof | Publish only what you can document |
| No idea what worked | No events for form, call and WhatsApp | Track each action by campaign |

## FAQ

### What makes a good landing page?

A good landing page has a headline that matches the click, one specific action, proof the visitor can check and a fast phone layout. The four guides agree on those, and web.dev adds tap-size and text-size rules for forms.

### Are landing pages effective?

Landing pages can be effective because they ask for one action, but the evidence I read comes from vendors that sell them. Mailchimp says the single focus produces high conversion rates, and I found no independent study in my sources.

### Why is a landing page important?

A landing page matters because it lets you match one campaign to one page and measure the result. Framer says it lets you measure ROI per campaign and test messages with a specific audience.

### How do I promote a landing page?

You promote a landing page by sending it traffic from ads, email, social media or organic search, and making the page match whichever source you use. Mailchimp lists ads, email blasts and social media as the usual sources.

### How long should a landing page be?

A landing page should be as long as the decision needs, and Unbounce says most benefit from short copy while some offers need longer pages. HubSpot's author says a free guide can fit in a headline, bullets and a form, whereas a consultation needs more.

### Do landing pages help SEO?

Landing pages can help SEO if they target keywords that match what searchers want, according to Unbounce, and Mailchimp also says to optimise them for search. Both are practitioner guidance, and my research found no Google documentation on the point.

### What conversion rate should I aim for?

No source I read gives a benchmark conversion rate for Indian small businesses, so I will not name one. Mailchimp reports landing page bounce rates of 60 to 90% without citing a study. Record your own rate for a month, then try to beat it.
