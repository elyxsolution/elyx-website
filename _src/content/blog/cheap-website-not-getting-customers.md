## Is a Slow Page Costing You Customers?

A slow page loses the visitor before the offer is read, and three of the five sources list speed as a cause. Design Sphere says visitors leave if the content takes more than a few seconds to appear, and MyDailyLink names large images, unnecessary scripts and poor hosting as the usual culprits.

The studio's own pages describe the Indian reality: most visitors arrive on a phone, often on patchy 4G, and one 6 MB photo can slow a page. None of the sources gives a load-time threshold or measures cheap sites, so run this test yourself:

1. Switch off Wi-Fi and open your homepage on mobile data.
2. Count the seconds until the headline and the contact button are both visible.
3. Ask what size your largest image files are, and which plug-ins you no longer use.

Design Sphere's fixes are the cheap ones: compress images, use faster hosting and remove unneeded plug-ins or scripts. If those do not help, the page itself may need rebuilding.

## No Call to Action: The Gap Most Top Pages Skim

A call to action is the button or link that tells a visitor what to do next, and a site without a clear one gives interested people nothing to act on. All five pages mention it, yet none shows how to test it.

Bibin Chandru, a freelance marketer in Palakkad, says a visitor who must hunt for the offer and contact details will mostly leave. His headline formula is the offer, plus where, plus why, plus what happens next. MyDailyLink advises contact details in the header, footer and contact page.

Exposure Ninja adds two failures that look fine on a desktop: a sidebar contact form that drops below long copy on a phone, and form emails caught by a spam filter. Run this test on your own site:

- **Ten seconds.** A stranger can name the action within ten seconds of the page opening.
- **One job.** Each page asks for one thing, not four.
- **Tap to call.** The number dials when tapped, and WhatsApp opens in two taps or fewer.
- **Placement.** The action appears on the first screen and again at the end.
- **Delivery.** Submit your own form and check the email arrives, spam folder included.

The next table is an invented illustration of the rewrite, not a client case. It applies Bibin Chandru's formula to a coaching centre.

| Element | Brochure version | Action version |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | Welcome to Sharma Classes | IELTS coaching in Jayanagar, Bengaluru: book a free demo class |
| Button | Submit | Book a demo on WhatsApp |
| Phone number | Image in the footer | Tap-to-call in the header |
| Form position | Right sidebar | Directly under the first screen |

## No Local SEO: Why Nearby Customers Cannot Find You

A site is invisible to nearby customers when it repeats a city name but carries none of the real local signals. Bibin Chandru calls repeating "Palakkad" twenty times keyword insertion, and lists four real signals instead: your actual service area, the same name, address and phone details everywhere, a well-kept Google Business Profile, and genuine reviews.

He also separates searches by intent: "modern kitchen design ideas" may come from someone wanting inspiration, while "interior designer in Palakkad" comes from someone closer to hiring. The studio's SEO page adds that the Google Business Profile is where most small businesses are found, and its clinic page says Maps rankings come mainly from that profile.

Expect months, not days. The studio's SEO page says technical fixes show in weeks while competitive local rankings take a few months, and nobody can promise a ranking.

- **Profile.** Claim it, fill every field and add real photos.
- **Consistency.** Match the name, address and phone number on the site, the profile and any listing.
- **Pages.** Give each service its own page that names the area you serve.
- **Reviews.** Ask for real feedback after each job.

The [Google Business Profile checker](/tools/google-business-profile-checker/) shows what yours is missing.

## Count Real Visitors Before You Blame the Design

Enquiries depend on real visitors multiplied by the share who act, so a site with 36 real visitors cannot be judged on design alone. Exposure Ninja gives the example: a site launched with 60 visitors in its first month, of whom 20 were friends and family and 4 were the owner testing on different devices.

The arithmetic is 60 minus 20 minus 4, which is 36 real visitors. Exposure Ninja's rule of thumb is that there is almost always a way to reach 1% conversion, and 1% of 36 is 0.36 of an enquiry, so no sales would be normal. At 500 real visitors, the same 1% gives 5 enquiries.

Bibin Chandru's illustrative example shows why quality beats quantity. The table adds the rates, which are my division of his figures.

| Scenario | Funnel | Enquiry rate | Customers per 100 visitors |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: 300 visitors | 15 enquiries, 5 customers | 15 / 300 = 5% | 5 / 300 x 100 = 1.7 |
| B: 1,000 visitors | 8 enquiries, 2 customers | 8 / 1,000 = 0.8% | 2 / 1,000 x 100 = 0.2 |

Both examples are illustrations, and the 1% figure is a practitioner's rule of thumb, not a benchmark for your trade. Replace them with your own counts.

## Does Your First Screen Earn a Stranger's Trust?

A visitor decides on the first screen whether you can solve their problem, serve their area, be trusted and be reached now, and a brochure-style page answers none of those. Those four questions come from Bibin Chandru, who contrasts them with openings such as "Welcome to ABC Enterprises. We have served customers since 2015."

Design Sphere and MyDailyLink both list trust signals: testimonials, reviews, case studies and examples of past work. Bibin Chandru adds real project photos, the owner's qualifications and a step-by-step description of how you work, and says testimonials must never be made up.

In some trades the claims are regulated. The studio's industry pages note that medical and dental councils restrict clinic advertising and that regulators have issued guidelines on misleading coaching ads, so keep every claim provable and confirm current rules with the relevant body.

## Where Is the Leak? Four Counts and a Diagnosis Table

The leak sits wherever the count drops, so track four numbers: visitors, taps on a contact action, enquiries and customers. Bibin Chandru names three free tools for this. Google Search Console shows which searches bring visitors, GA4 shows what they do on the site, and Microsoft Clarity records sessions and heat maps to show where people leave.

The studio's SEO page describes GA4 events for calls, WhatsApp clicks and form submissions. The table is my reading of how the causes map to the counts.

| What you see | Likely leak | First fix |
|---|---|---|
| Very few visitors | Traffic or local visibility | Profile, service pages, ask for reviews |
| Visitors but no taps | Message or call to action | Rewrite the headline, move the button up |
| Taps but no enquiries | Form, phone or WhatsApp broken | Test every path on a real phone |
| Enquiries but no customers | Slow reply or weak follow-up | Log each lead and reply the same day |

For the last row, Bibin Chandru advises logging each lead's name, contact, requirement and source in a spreadsheet, and the outcome.

## Fix It or Rebuild It: A Decision Rule

Fix the site you have when the audit names a specific leak, and rebuild only when the site cannot carry the fix. Bibin Chandru warns that a new design will not help if traffic is poor, the message is weak or leads are not followed up, and that small changes to a headline or call to action can start working quickly.

MyDailyLink lists the signs a redesign is worth considering: the site looks outdated, performs poorly on mobile, is hard to navigate, is invisible in search, or consistently fails to produce enquiries. Global Webify says a generic template may not fit a business, but it sells custom design, so weigh that as opinion. My reading is below.

- **Fix** when visitors arrive but few tap, the slowness traces to images or plug-ins, or the page ranks for the right search but does not convert.
- **Rebuild** when the phone layout is broken, you cannot edit text yourself, or three fixes in a row changed nothing.
- **Keep** the web addresses and text that already earn visits, whichever you choose.

A site is not a one-off cost. MyDailyLink says it should be reviewed regularly, and the studio's care page notes that plug-ins age and forms stop delivering without announcing it, which is what a monthly care plan covers.

## Who Can Build a Website Cheaply, and What to Ask First

A freelancer, a website-builder product or a studio can all build a cheap website, and the research does not rank them, so judge each by what is delivered. The questions below are my checklist, drawn from the causes above. Ask for the answers in writing before you pay.

- Which of these are included: mobile-first design, an enquiry form, a WhatsApp button, basic SEO setup, Google Maps?
- Who writes the headline and page text, and is that in the fee?
- Can I edit prices and photos myself after launch?
- Will calls, WhatsApp taps and form submissions be tracked?
- Who sets up my Google Business Profile, and who owns the domain and accounts?
- What does support cost after launch, and is the quote fixed and in writing?

Hold every builder to the same list, and walk away from anyone who will not answer in writing.
