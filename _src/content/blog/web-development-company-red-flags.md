The web development company red flags that matter most are a price before any questions, screenshots instead of live sites, no client you can phone, vague ownership, large upfront payments, no phased plan, nobody named to build it, no support terms, security sold as an extra, and pressure to decide fast. The honest qualification is that every page I read is published by a company that sells web work, so the list shows what sellers tell buyers to check, not measured failure rates. Gramosoft counts seven of its own ten as visible before you sign, which is the cheapest time to catch them.

- **The ten.** Each flag has the sources that name it, the green answer to listen for and one question to ask.
- **The tally.** A one-page sheet scores each vendor, with the arithmetic shown on made-up vendors.
- **The money.** Two worked examples: a GST quote comparison and an upfront payment split.
- **The self-test.** The same ten checks applied to our own published process.

## The 10 Web Development Company Red Flags at a Glance

Five guides, read on 17 November 2026, name overlapping red flags, and the table lists the ten I would check, who names each and what a good answer looks like. The five are WRTeam, Gramosoft, Techprosense, Tuesday and Kamboh Associates. I merged near-duplicates, and the grouping is my reading.

| Red flag | Named by | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Price before questions | WRTeam, Gramosoft | Questions first, a written scope, then an estimate |
| 2. Screenshots, no live work | All five | Live links you can open on your phone |
| 3. No client to call | WRTeam, Techprosense, Tuesday, Kamboh | A named client who takes your call |
| 4. Vague ownership | WRTeam, Gramosoft | Code, domain and accounts in your name |
| 5. Large upfront payment | WRTeam, Gramosoft | Payments tied to things you can check |
| 6. No phased plan | WRTeam, Gramosoft, Tuesday | Dated phases and regular demos |
| 7. Unexplained low price | WRTeam, Techprosense | A cheap quote that explains its scope |
| 8. Nobody named, no testing | Gramosoft only | Named builders, a staging site, a test plan |
| 9. No support terms | WRTeam, Gramosoft, Techprosense, Tuesday | Warranty, response times and a monthly cost |
| 10. Pressure and yes to everything | WRTeam, Techprosense, Gramosoft | Honest trade-offs and time to decide |

Flag 8 rests on one source, so treat it as a strong opinion, not a consensus. Three more flags (SEO knowledge, security, plain language) depend on your project and get their own section below.

## Red Flags in the Proposal: Price, Scope and Process

A proposal is a red flag when it prices before it asks, hides its steps or hides its costs. WRTeam says a quote that arrives within 24 hours, before anyone asks about functions, integrations, content and timeline, is not based on your project. Gramosoft says a web application's cost depends on roles, workflows and integrations that nobody knows after one conversation.

The same two sources describe the fix: a short discovery step that ends in a written scope. For flag 6, Gramosoft's warning is a quote of "2 to 3 months" with no phases. Tuesday adds that a vendor with no task-tracking system or defined revision process leaves you chasing updates.

For flag 7, WRTeam lists four explanations for a price that looks too good: underestimated scope that will be billed later, very junior staff, a project pushed behind better-paying clients, or skipped testing. Techprosense names hidden costs revealed after the project starts. WRTeam adds that the cheapest quote is not always wrong, but the vendor should be able to explain it.

Indian quotes add one trap: GST. The studio's price data says its ranges exclude 18% GST on software services, as of November 2026. Worked example, with made-up numbers: Quote A says 100 plus GST, and Quote B says 112 all-in. A comes to 100 x 1.18 = 118, so B is cheaper by 6, which is 6 / 118 = about 5%.

Use a published range as a sanity check, not a verdict. The studio's own indicative range for a 5 to 10 page business website is {{price:website}} in {{weeks:website}}, excluding GST. That data is a draft, so it is never a quote, and a fixed quote is confirmed in writing after a call. For other project types, the [pricing page](/pricing/) and the [website cost calculator](/tools/website-cost-calculator/) show the rest.

## Red Flags in the Portfolio: Screenshots and No One to Call

A portfolio is a red flag when you cannot open it live or ask the client about it. Kamboh Associates lists the warning signs: screenshots or mock-ups only, broken links, every site looking the same, slow or broken mobile pages, no recent work, famous brands with no detail, no client willing to speak and contact forms that do not work.

WRTeam adds that testimonials on a vendor's own site prove nothing, third-party reviews are stronger and a direct reference call is strongest.

Kamboh describes a 15-minute check that I would run on every vendor. These are its steps, in my words:

1. Pick three live sites closest to your project type.
2. Open each on your phone using mobile data, not Wi-Fi.
3. Run Google PageSpeed Insights on each homepage and note the mobile result.
4. Test a contact form or WhatsApp button on at least one site.
5. Look in the footer for a developer credit, and check the business still uses the site.
6. Check the Wayback Machine for when the current design first appeared.

New developers are a special case. Kamboh says few projects is not a red flag by itself and suggests their best two pieces of work, a short paid test task or a smaller first milestone.

## Red Flags in the Contract: Ownership and Payment Terms

A contract is a red flag when it is silent on ownership or asks you to pay most of the money before you see work. WRTeam says a proper agreement covers scope, payment schedule, timeline with milestones, ownership, scope changes and post-launch support. Some contracts keep IP with the agency until ongoing fees are paid, or licence the work instead of transferring it.

Gramosoft lists what you should hold: the domain, cloud account, code repository, database, admin logins, design files and documentation. It also warns about a builder or framework only the vendor uses. If the vendor holds the keys, leaving means starting again.

This is not legal advice, and Gramosoft says the same, so have a lawyer read the ownership clause.

Both WRTeam and Gramosoft flag a payment of 50% or more before work is shown. The split below is my illustration, not a standard, and Gramosoft also calls its own example split an example.

| Payment point | Vendor A (upfront) | Vendor B (milestones) |
|---|---|---|
| On signing | 50 | 20 |
| Design sign-off | 0 | 25 |
| Acceptance testing | 25 | 30 |
| Go-live and short warranty | 25 | 25 |

If the vendor disappears before you see anything working, A has taken 50 of your 100 and B has taken 20. The difference is 50 - 20 = 30 of your budget at risk. Choose the structure where every payment follows something you can open and test.

## Red Flags in the Team: Who Builds It and How It Is Tested

A team is a red flag when nobody is named for delivery or nobody can explain how work is tested. Gramosoft says a senior person on the sales call with no one named for the build, or a hint that work goes to "partners", weakens accountability and widens who sees your data. Its green answer is names and roles in writing, plus a promise not to subcontract without your consent.

Testing is the same story. Gramosoft's red answer is "we test everything" with no staging site, no test plan and your own team finding the bugs after launch. The green answer is a staging environment you can use, code review and acceptance testing on your own data before go-live.

## Red Flags After Launch: Support, Security and Updates

No written support terms is among the most-cited red flags: four of the five guides name it. WRTeam says a site is not finished at launch, because bugs, browser updates and security holes appear afterwards. Tuesday says to ask for defined response times and proactive monitoring, and to watch for vendors who charge heavily for basic fixes or vanish.

Gramosoft's green answer has five parts: a bug-fix warranty after go-live, response times by severity, a monthly cost, security updates and who renews domains, certificates and cloud services. It also says security cannot be an add-on, and that under India's DPDP Act your business stays responsible for personal data a vendor processes for you. For a rule-level reading, ask a lawyer.

Get the monthly figure before you sign, not after. The [cost of website maintenance in India](/blog/website-maintenance-charges/) and the [hidden costs of owning a website](/blog/website-hidden-costs/) show what to expect line by line.

## Pressure to Decide and Yes to Everything

Pressure and constant agreement are both red flags, because both replace honest scoping with a closed sale. WRTeam names limited-time pricing, filling slots and offers that expire this week, and says a credible vendor welcomes a buyer who checks references. Techprosense adds exaggerated promises about time and results.

Gramosoft names the opposite behaviour: every feature promised, any deadline accepted, "no problem" to every change. Its green answer is a trade-off, such as finishing by a date if two reports are dropped, with a priced change process.

A useful test is to ask what the vendor would remove from your list. A vendor with no answer has not read it.

## Red Flags That Depend on Your Project: SEO, Security and Plain Language

Three more flags matter only for some projects, and which ones you weight depends on what you are building. Tuesday says a vendor who never raises page speed, crawlability, structured data or clean code is a risk when the site must rank, and its page is written for SEO agencies, so apply it with that in mind. WRTeam says a vendor who answers technical questions with jargon or "trust us" will also communicate badly once problems start.

| Project | Weigh these flags most | Why (my reading) |
|---|---|---|
| Landing page or brochure site | 2, 3, 7, 9 | Little to build, so proof and aftercare decide it |
| Local business site | 2, 9, SEO knowledge | Phone speed and Google Maps visibility drive enquiries |
| Online store or web app | 4, 5, 8, security | Logins, payments and customer data raise the stakes |

Gramosoft says it is not a fit for a brochure site at a template price, and that a small studio will serve you better. That is a vendor admitting where it is weaker, which is itself a green flag.

## Red Flags in a Company: A Tally Sheet to Score Every Vendor

A tally sheet turns ten flags into one number per vendor, so you compare answers rather than impressions. The two thresholds I found disagree slightly. WRTeam says more than two or three flags makes the risk real, and Gramosoft says three or more means walk away while one means ask for the answer in writing.

My method: ask all ten questions in writing, then mark each answer green (0), amber (1) or red (2).

| Vendor (made up) | Reds | Ambers | Score (reds x 2 + ambers) |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | 3 | 2 | 3 x 2 + 2 = 8 |
| Y | 1 | 3 | 1 x 2 + 3 = 5 |
| Z | 0 | 2 | 0 x 2 + 2 = 2 |

By the sources' thresholds, X is out and Y must put its one red answer in writing before you continue. Add one rule of mine: a red on flag 4 or 5, ownership or payment, removes a vendor whatever the score. The scores are illustrations, not data on any real company.

## What Good Looks Like: The Same Ten Checks Applied to Us

Applying the test to ourselves is fair, and our published pages answer several of the ten. Per the [web development service page](/services/web-development/), the studio sends a written scope with a timeline and a fixed price before work begins, shows wireframes before any code, builds in weekly increments with two feedback rounds, and includes 30 days of post-launch support on the Launch tier. The three founders do every project, so the person you brief on the first call designs and writes the code.

On ownership, the SaaS page says the client owns the code, and the web page says it avoids proprietary lock-in. For proof, the [Ashirwad Dental case study](/work/ashirwad-dental/) records 8 weeks from kickoff to launch, and according to the clinic, two earlier agencies had not delivered what it needed in a year.

Now the awkward part. The SaaS page states payment as 50% upfront and 50% on final delivery, which sits at the level WRTeam and Gramosoft flag, so ask any vendor, us included, what you see before the first payment. A claim of ours is worth what the signed contract says.

## What Usually Goes Wrong When a Red Flag Is Ignored

Ignoring a flag usually costs you at a later stage than the flag appeared, which is why they feel harmless on the sales call. The consequences below come from the sources, and the fixes are my reading.

| Flag ignored | Documented consequence | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Quote before questions | Scope additions arrive as costed change requests (WRTeam) | Written scope before a price |
| Vague ownership | Leaving later means starting again (Gramosoft) | Ownership clause and your own accounts |
| No support terms | A liability, not a finished product (WRTeam) | Warranty and monthly cost in writing |
| Yes to everything | Delays or quality problems later (Gramosoft) | Ask what they would cut |
| Cheap quote, no reasons | Corners cut on testing or security (WRTeam) | Ask what the price excludes |

## What to Do Next

Run the same ten questions past every vendor on your shortlist this week, in writing, and decide only when the answers are in. 2. Send each the ten questions from the table above.
3. Run the 15-minute portfolio check and phone one client each.
4. Score each vendor on the tally sheet and drop any vendor with a red on ownership or payment.
5. Ask for the support terms and a monthly figure before you sign.

## FAQ

### What are the biggest red flags when hiring a web development company?

The biggest are a price before questions, no live work or client reference, vague ownership, a large upfront payment and no support terms. WRTeam, Gramosoft, Techprosense, Tuesday and Kamboh each name several of these. All five name screenshots with no live work.

### Are most web development agencies a scam for small businesses?

No source I read says so, and I found no data on how many agencies fail their clients. The five guides describe warning signs to check, and WRTeam says most of the industry meets a baseline of professionalism while some does not. Check each vendor against the ten flags instead of judging the whole trade.

### How many red flags are too many?

WRTeam says more than two or three flags makes the risk real, and Gramosoft says to walk away from a company showing three or more. For one flag, Gramosoft says to ask for the answer in writing. A red on ownership or payment is enough for me to stop.

### How much should I pay upfront for a website?

Pay a modest advance and tie later payments to things you can check. Gramosoft and WRTeam both warn against 50% or more upfront, and Gramosoft suggests holding the last share until a short warranty ends. A fixed quote is confirmed in writing, with GST at 18% on top of studio ranges.

### Is a very cheap quote always a red flag?

No, but it needs an explanation. WRTeam says the cheapest quote is not always wrong, and that a vendor should explain a price far below comparable ones. Ask what the quote excludes, who builds it and what happens after launch.

### How do I check a web developer's portfolio?

Open three live sites on your phone using mobile data, test a form or WhatsApp button, run Google PageSpeed Insights and ask the developer what they built. Kamboh Associates describes this as a check of about 15 minutes per developer. Then phone one client.

### Who should own the code, domain and hosting?

You should, from day one, and the contract should say so. Gramosoft lists the domain, cloud account, repository, database, admin logins, design files and documentation as the keys to hold. Ask a lawyer to read the ownership clause.

### What should post-launch support include?

It should include a bug-fix warranty, response times by severity, security updates, backups, a monthly cost and who renews domains and certificates. This list is Gramosoft's, and WRTeam and Tuesday ask for the same terms in different words. Get the figure before you sign.

### Do the same red flags apply to a web app and a simple website?

Mostly, but the weight changes. For a web app that holds logins, payments or customer data, ownership, testing, security and payment terms matter most. For a landing page, proof of live work and aftercare matter most.
