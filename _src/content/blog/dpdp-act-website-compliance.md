DPDP Act website compliance means that a business site collecting a name, phone number or email must show a notice with each consent request, take clean consent, honour withdrawal, protect the data and erase it when the purpose ends. As of 20 November 2026, most of those duties start on 13 May 2027, so a site built or changed now should be ready for that date. The honest qualification is that I read practitioner guides, a law-firm article and one government explanatory note, not a lawyer's advice, so confirm your own duties with a qualified professional.

- **Who it covers.** Any business site with a contact form or login, whatever its size.
- **When.** Core duties begin 13 May 2027, which is 174 days from today.
- **What to build.** A data ledger, a notice beside each form, a withdrawal route, a retention rule and a vendor list.
- **Where it ends.** A plugin helps, but the law judges what you actually do with the data.

## DPDP Act Website Compliance: Does the Act Apply to a Small Business Site?

The Act applies to a small business site as soon as it collects digital personal data, and the sources I read name no small-business exemption. DPDP Compliance (dpdp-compliance.in) says the Act covers organisations of every size, from a two-person startup up, with extra duties only for large "Significant Data Fiduciaries". Dpdpactindia.in adds that a contact form or login is enough, and that data used purely for personal or domestic purposes is outside the Act (Section 3).

Three roles matter. You, the site owner, are the **Data Fiduciary**: the party that decides why and how personal data is used. Your hosting, CRM, email and payment tools are usually **Data Processors**, and the visitor is the **Data Principal**.

DLA Piper adds that the Act protects individuals, not companies, and that a foreign site is covered when it offers goods or services to people in India. My reading: a clinic's booking form, a coaching centre's demo request and a shop's checkout are all covered. Exemptions are a question for a lawyer, because the sources do not set them out.

## DPDP Act Compliance Timeline: Which Dates Apply to a Website

The main website duties start on 13 May 2027, eighteen months after the Rules were notified on 13 November 2025. King Stubb & Kasiva on Legal500 describes the phased start, and DLA Piper lists three phases. The table sets them against a website, as of 20 November 2026.

| Date | What begins | What it means for your site |
|---|---|---|
| 13 Nov 2025 | Definitions and Board procedure | Nothing to build. The Board exists in law |
| 13 Nov 2026 | Consent Manager rules | Already passed. Matters only if you use a registered Consent Manager |
| 13 May 2027 | Notice, consent, security, contact, rights, breach | Your forms, tags and safeguards are tested |

The gap from today: 10 days left in November, plus 31, 31, 28, 31 and 30 for December to April, plus 13 for May, gives 174 days.

Two cautions. DLA Piper says the IT Act and its 2011 privacy rules keep governing until the core provisions start. Dpdpactindia.in follows the Gazette date of 13 November 2025, notes that some commentary counts from 14 November, and reports no official change to the dates as of 26 October 2026.

## Step One: Build a Data Ledger of Every Place Personal Data Enters

A data ledger is a table of every place personal data enters or moves on your site, and the guides I read all start with it. Dpdpactindia.in lists forms, checkout, logins, chat, analytics, ad tags, payments, hosting and backups, plus the inboxes and spreadsheets where form data lands. You answer for tools you did not build (Section 8(1)).

Legal500 adds that each row should name the purpose, the system that stores the data and the third parties that receive it. The table is an invented example for a fictional dental clinic, not a client.

| Where data enters | Data | Purpose | Who else sees it | Keep for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Booking form | Name, mobile, time | Fix an appointment | Email tool, Google Sheet | Your rule, written down |
| Offers tick-box | Mobile | Send offers | Messaging tool | Until withdrawn |
| Analytics tag | Device, pages | Count visits | Analytics vendor | Off until consent |

Dpdpactindia.in gives three rules: no field without a purpose, no purpose without a legal ground, and no tool without a row.

## Consent on Forms: Notice First, Then a Clean Tick

Consent on a form is valid only when a notice comes with or before the request and the person gives a clear affirmative action. A DPIIT government FAQ quotes the Act: consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, and limited to the data the purpose needs. Not every field needs consent, because Legal500 and Consent Server both note that the Act also recognises "legitimate uses".

Dpdpactindia.in flags an open question: whether a notice is still needed when you rely on a legitimate use. It advises asking counsel, and showing the notice anyway costs little. The table condenses its form guidance.

| Avoid | Do instead |
|---|---|
| A pre-ticked box | A blank box the visitor ticks |
| One tick for terms, privacy and marketing | One tick per purpose |
| Notice buried in the terms | A notice that makes sense alone |
| English only | English or an Eighth Schedule language |
| Withdrawal by phone call | An unsubscribe link or account setting |
| No record of who agreed | A log of time, notice version and purpose |

A worked example: a clinic form asks for name, mobile and preferred time, with a link to a short notice. A second, unticked box reads "Send me offers on WhatsApp", and declining it does not block the booking.

### Withdrawal and Children

Withdrawing consent must be as easy as giving it, so a one-click route beats a series of emails. Consent Server tests it from the visitor's side: after a withdrawal, do your CRM, email tool and SMS system all stop?

DLA Piper says a Data Principal under eighteen is represented by a parent or guardian. Dpdpactindia.in adds that no tracking or targeted ads may be aimed at children, and Legal500 reports a penalty ceiling of ₹200 crore for breaching the children's obligations. Schools and coaching centres should take advice before collecting a minor's details.

## The DPDP Privacy Notice: What to Publish and What Goes Beside the Form

The consent notice and the privacy page are different things, and the law asks for the notice with each consent request. Cynor Sense's guide at dpdpa.support says the Rules require it to stand alone, in plain language, with an itemised list of the data and each specific purpose. It must also say how to withdraw consent, use rights and complain to the Board, in English or an Eighth Schedule language.

The same guide, checked against the official text on 5 October 2026, lists what the site must publish beside it.

| Put on the site | What to write |
|---|---|
| Business contact | A name or role, with email or phone, for data questions |
| How to make a request | The form or address to use, and any identifier needed |
| The rights, in plain words | Access, correction, updating, erasure, grievance, nomination |
| Grievance period | A number of days, never more than 90 |
| Board complaint line | A person may go to the Data Protection Board after your process |

The 90 days is the outer limit for grievances, not a deadline for every rights request, says the same guide. Legal500 names the commonest failure: a policy that does not match what the business really does. If the page promises erasure, a retention rule must exist behind it.

## Data Retention: How Long Can You Keep Form Data?

You may keep personal data only while the purpose lasts, and must erase it once consent is withdrawn or the purpose is served, unless a law requires you to keep it. Dpdpactindia.in cites Section 8(7) and calls the ledger's "keep for" column your erasure rule. DPO India's checklist adds that backups must be cleared too, which is where many sites slip.

The sources give two fixed figures, and neither is a general limit for a small site. Dpdpactindia.in reads the Rules as requiring processing logs, and the data they cover, to be kept at least one year. Large platforms face a ceiling: an e-commerce entity or social media intermediary with at least two crore registered users in India must erase data after three years without contact, with 48 hours' warning (Third Schedule), per dpdpactindia.in and Seqrite.

My reading: a small site has no sourced maximum, so set a period per ledger row and be able to say why. Apply it to inboxes, spreadsheets and chat exports as well as the website database, and check that a deletion reaches backups.

## Security Safeguards and Breach Duties for a Small Site

A small site must take reasonable security safeguards, and Legal500 puts the penalty ceiling for failing at ₹250 crore. The MeitY explanatory note lists encryption, access control, monitoring for unauthorised access, backups, logs and security terms in processor contracts.

Breach duties are separate. The note says a Data Fiduciary must tell each affected person promptly and the Board without delay, then give details within 72 hours or a longer permitted time. Dpdpactindia.in adds that CERT-In's directions of 28 April 2022 require listed incidents to be reported within 6 hours of being noticed.

MeitY states that its note is not part of the Rules, and the rule numbers it uses do not match those other sources give for commencement. Read the notified text at [MeitY's page for the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025](https://meity.gov.in/documents/act-and-policies/digital-personal-dataprotection-rules-2025-gDOxUjMtQWa?pageTitle=Digital-Personal-Data-Protection-Rules-2025), the nearest thing to a DPDP Act official website for the rules, before relying on a number.

## Third-Party Tools: Analytics, Chat, CRM and Payment Gateways

Every tool that sees visitor data is a vendor to list, because the Act leaves you responsible for your processors. Dpdpactindia.in says a processor may be used only under a valid contract (Section 8(2)). The table adapts its pre-launch questions.

| Tool | Data it may see | Ask before launch |
|---|---|---|
| Form builder or CRM | Names, emails, phones | Where is it stored, and can one record be deleted? |
| Analytics and ad tags | Device data, pages | Can it stay off until consent? |
| Payment gateway | Name, contact, order | What does it keep, and what does its contract say on security? |
| Hosting and backups | Everything | Is data encrypted, and are logs kept a year? |
| Chat widgets | Chat text, email | How long is the history stored? |

A DPIIT FAQ says Section 16 lets the government restrict transfers to notified countries, and the sources name none, so ask overseas vendors where data lives. The guide to [who owns your website, domain and accounts](/blog/who-owns-my-website-code/) matters here, since you cannot instruct a vendor on an account you do not control.

## The DPDP Act Compliance Checklist for a Small Business Website

A small business can cover the core of the Act on a website in ten steps, and the order matters because each step feeds the next. None of the top pages I analysed set out what a small business should do, so this sequence is my own, built from the sources above. It is a business checklist, not legal advice.

1. **Confirm applicability.** Assume a site collecting personal data is covered, and ask a lawyer about exemptions.
2. **Build the ledger.** One row per entry point, including inboxes and spreadsheets.
3. **Cut fields.** Remove any form field with no purpose, as Legal500 advises.
4. **Fix the forms.** Blank boxes, one tick per purpose, a notice beside each form.
5. **Write the notice.** Stand-alone, plain, itemised, with a language option.
6. **Publish contact, request route and grievance period.** Footer and privacy page.
7. **Wire up withdrawal.** Test it from the page to your CRM and email tool.
8. **Set retention.** A period per row, a deletion routine and a backup check.
9. **Secure the site and list every vendor.** Access control, backups, updates.
10. **Write the breach plan.** Who detects, who decides, who tells the Board, affected people and CERT-In.

On tools, Consent Server, a seller of consent software, argues that a plugin alone may not be enough because consent must reach connected systems. That is a vendor's view. My reading: a site with one form and one email tool can manage with a ledger and a clear page, and a site with a CRM, ad tags and an app is where a consent platform starts to earn its cost.

## What Usually Goes Wrong

Most failures come from treating the law as a banner instead of a process. These are the patterns the sources describe, with cause and fix.

- **A copied policy.** Cause: a template pasted in. Fix: write each line from the ledger.
- **Waiting for May 2027.** Cause: reading the date as the starting gun. Fix: dpdpactindia.in reads sign-ups taken before 13 May 2027 as earlier consents that may need a notice afterwards, so a clean form now avoids a retrofit. Confirm with counsel.
- **Withdrawal that stops at the page.** Cause: the checkbox updates one database. Fix: test the whole chain.
- **Forgotten data stores.** Cause: audits stop at the website database. Fix: include inboxes, spreadsheets, chat exports and backups.
- **English-only notices.** Cause: translation left for later. Fix: plan a Kannada or Hindi option for your main audience. A Reddit thread on the Rules named multilingual notices and consent withdrawal as sticking points.

EY India surveyed more than 150 professionals and found close to 70% not very familiar with the Act and Rules, and nearly 81% of organisations without DPDP-aligned privacy policies drafted. The respondents were professionals across sectors, not small business owners, so read it as a sign of how early the market is.

## FAQ

### What is DPDP Act compliance?

DPDP Act compliance is meeting the duties the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the DPDP Rules, 2025 place on anyone using personal data of people in India. For a website that means notice, consent, security, rights handling, breach reporting and erasure, according to dpdp-compliance.in.

### Who must comply with the DPDP Act?

Any business, non-profit or public body that processes personal data must comply, whatever its size. Dpdp-compliance.in says size changes the extent of the duties, not whether they apply, and DLA Piper says foreign entities offering goods or services in India are covered too.

### Is the DPDP Act enforced yet?

Only in part: as of 20 November 2026 the Board provisions and Consent Manager rules have started, and the core duties begin on 13 May 2027. DLA Piper says the older IT Act privacy rules govern until then.

### Does the DPDP Act require a privacy policy?

The Act and Rules do not use the term "privacy policy", but they require a notice with each consent request, a published business contact, published request routes and a grievance period. Cynor Sense says a page covering those items rests on the law's text rather than a generic template.

### What must a consent notice contain?

It must itemise the personal data, state each specific purpose and the services it enables, and give the routes for withdrawing consent, using rights and complaining to the Board. It must also be understandable on its own, per the MeitY explanatory note.

### In which languages must the notice be available?

The notice must be readable in English or in any language listed in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution, according to Cynor Sense. For a Karnataka business that suggests Kannada beside English, which is my reading of who your visitors are.

### How long can I keep form data?

Keep it only while the purpose lasts, then erase it, unless another law requires you to keep it. The sources give no general limit for a small site, so set a period per data type and write it down.

### Does a cookie banner or plugin make my site compliant?

No, because the law judges the notice, the consent record and what happens to the data afterwards, not the tool alone. Consent Server, which sells consent software, says a plugin alone may not be enough.

### What is the penalty under the DPDP Act?

Legal500 reports a penalty ceiling of ₹250 crore for breaching the security-safeguards duty and ₹200 crore for the children's obligations, under the Act's Schedule. These are ceilings, not typical amounts, and the Board decides after an inquiry.
