Study abroad and test prep is one of the most ad-heavy categories in India. Institutes pay for every click on "GRE coaching" and "IELTS classes", so the landing page is where money is made or wasted. A page that converts 4% instead of 2% is the same as halving your ad bill.

This is the thinking behind the [Pinnacle Inc. landing page and brand presence](/work/pinnacle-inc/), a GRE and IELTS coaching company, and what we would apply to any similar page.

## One page, one goal

An ad click is not a visit to your website. It is a visitor with a specific question who is one tap from leaving. Strip everything that is not the answer:

- No full navigation menu that leads to ten other pages
- One primary action: request a call-back, book a demo class or message on WhatsApp
- One offer per page. GRE and IELTS get separate pages if you advertise them separately

## Match the headline to the search

Someone who searched "IELTS coaching in Bengaluru" should read "IELTS coaching in Bengaluru" in the first line, not a generic brand slogan. Name the exam, say who it is for and state the outcome you can honestly stand behind. Then, one line below, say what happens next: "Book a free demo class".

## Put the decision details near the top

Students and parents compare on the same few facts. Make them easy to find:

- **Batch timings and start dates**
- **Fees**, or at least what is included
- **Format** (classroom, online or hybrid)
- **Faculty** and their background
- **Demo class** availability

Hiding fees to force a call filters nobody out and annoys people who then compare with a competitor that is transparent.

## Be careful with claims

Rank, score and selection claims are heavily scrutinised in coaching advertising. Keep every claim verifiable, and keep supporting proof on file. Avoid anything that sounds like a guarantee. Consumer-protection guidelines in India have targeted misleading coaching ads, so please check the current rules before publishing results.

## Make the enquiry effortless on mobile

Most of this traffic arrives on a phone. A good enquiry setup has three paths: a short form (name, phone, exam and a preferred time), a WhatsApp button with a pre-filled message, and a tap-to-call button. Do not ask for more than you need. Each extra field loses leads.

## Build for speed

Paid traffic is the least patient traffic. Compress images, avoid heavy sliders and videos above the fold, and keep scripts lean. A landing page that takes four seconds on 4G loses people you have already paid for.

## Track what happens next

A landing page without tracking is a guess. At a minimum, track form submissions, WhatsApp clicks and calls, tagged with the campaign they came from. Then ask every enquiry "how did you find us?" and log it. That is how you find out which ad actually produces admissions, not just clicks.

## FAQ

### How long should a study-abroad landing page be?

Short enough that the enquiry button is always in reach, long enough to answer fees, batches and faculty. For most campaigns that is one to two screens of content plus a visible button, not a long brochure.

### Should the landing page be separate from my main website?

For paid campaigns, yes. A dedicated page lets you match the headline to the ad and remove distractions. Your main site can still hold the full detail.

### Do I need a WhatsApp button?

For most Indian audiences, yes. It is the lowest-friction way to start a conversation, and it often converts better than a form for parents and students.
