Yes, a restaurant website in India is still worth having if you are on Swiggy and Zomato, but a small one, because the apps rent you customers while your own site is where regulars reach you without paying commission. The honest qualification is that no source we read states a commission rate, so this article gives you a formula to run on your own contract. Mantis, a vendor of restaurant data, says Indian restaurants lean on Zomato and Swiggy for discovery and pay for it in commission, and that a direct ordering page on their own site is still uncommon.

- **Commission.** Work out what the apps cost you per 100 rupees ordered, using the rate in your own agreement, before deciding how much to build.
- **Menu.** A real text menu page, not a PDF, is the part of a restaurant site that top results skip.
- **Reservations.** A table request that confirms on WhatsApp is enough for most places; real-time booking is a bigger job.
- **Google Maps.** Your Business Profile does most of the finding, and the site has to match it.

## Do You Need a Restaurant Website in India If You Are on Swiggy and Zomato?

You need one as a supporting site: the apps handle strangers and delivery, and your site handles the guest who already knows your name. As of 16 November 2026, Swiggy's own city page describes browsing restaurants, comparing prices and getting delivery. Zomato's India page describes menus, photos, ratings and user reviews.

The table compares the two routes on criteria I chose before reading. The platform column rests on those two pages and on Mantis. The website column rests on the studio's own restaurant page.

| Criterion | Swiggy and Zomato listing | Your own website |
|---|---|---|
| Who finds you | Strangers browsing the app | People who search your name or tap your Maps link |
| Cost per order | Commission; no rate in our sources | No platform commission; gateway and build costs apply |
| Menu page | The platform's layout | Yours, with your photos and wording |
| Guest contact | Not documented in our sources | A WhatsApp or call button the guest taps |
| Effort | A listing and upkeep | A build and upkeep |

The apps are the better answer in two cases. A new place with no name needs the discovery they sell, and a kitchen with no riders needs their delivery. A site does neither on its own.

## Aggregator Commission: A Worked Example With Your Own Rate

The saving from a direct order is the bill times your commission rate, and you can check it with three numbers from your own records. Every input below is an assumption, so replace each one.

1. **Orders.** 400 aggregator orders a month.
2. **Average bill.** 500 rupees.
3. **Commission.** 20 percent, an assumed rate. Read your own agreement.

Monthly aggregator sales are 400 × 500 = 200,000 rupees, and at 20 percent that is 40,000 rupees in commission. The formula for what a website saves is orders moved × average bill × commission rate.

| Share of orders moved direct | Orders | Commission avoided |
|---|---|---|
| 5% | 20 | 20 × 500 × 0.20 = 2,000 rupees |
| 10% | 40 | 40 × 500 × 0.20 = 4,000 rupees |
| 15% | 60 | 60 × 500 × 0.20 = 6,000 rupees |
| 25% | 100 | 100 × 500 × 0.20 = 10,000 rupees |

This overstates the saving, because direct orders still carry payment gateway charges and, if you deliver yourself, rider costs. Platform discounts, delivery charges and packaging terms also differ by contract and are not in our sources. To find the break-even, divide the build cost by your monthly saving after those costs.

Whether any orders move is the real question. A listing does not shift guests by itself, so the next section covers where direct orders come from.

## Direct Orders vs Aggregator: A Decision Rule

Choose direct ordering for guests who already know you, and keep the apps for guests who do not. The studio's restaurant page says a site can add WhatsApp click-to-order and, as an optional larger build, a direct order flow with UPI payment to reduce aggregator commission.

This table is my framework, not a sourced benchmark.

| Choose this | When | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Menu, photos and a WhatsApp button | You take calls and chats already and have regulars | Orders and payments tracked by hand |
| Full direct ordering with UPI | Your commission bill is large and you can deliver or take pickups | A bigger build and a payment gateway to set up |
| Apps only | You are new and have no budget or riders | No page you control, and no direct way to reach guests |

My reading of where direct orders come from, none of it sourced:

- **Your Maps profile.** A guest searching your name should find an order or call button.
- **Your packaging.** A printed QR or number that opens a pre-filled WhatsApp chat.
- **Your tables.** A card that tells dine-in guests how to reorder next time.
- **Your Instagram bio.** One link to the menu page.

## Restaurant Menus: The Page the Top Results Skip

A restaurant menu page should be a text page a guest can search and read on a phone, not a PDF. The studio's restaurant page says the same: a real, searchable menu, not a PDF that takes ten seconds to open on 4G. None of the four top-ranking pages we read has a menu section, which makes this the opening in the results.

Here are the fields I would give each dish, as my suggestion:

| Field | What goes in it | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Name | The word guests say | Matches what they search |
| Price | The price you want guests to see | The first thing most guests check |
| Description | Main ingredients, one line | Fewer questions on chat |
| Photo | Your own, compressed | Speed on mobile data |
| Availability | In stock, lunch only, weekends | Fewer disappointed guests |

The studio says it can set up a simple CMS so you can change dishes, prices and photos without a developer. Decide on purpose whether your direct menu uses the same prices as your app menu, and say which on the page.

## Reservations and WhatsApp: How Guests Reach You

A restaurant needs a way to reach it in one tap, and for most places a table request that you confirm on WhatsApp is enough. The studio's restaurant page lists a reservation request, WhatsApp click-to-order and a call button. The India Restaurant page we read invites guests to book a table online and says it takes lunch reservations.

| Your place | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Walk-in café | Call and WhatsApp buttons | Few guests book |
| Dinner place with peaks | Table request form, confirmed on WhatsApp | You see the day's load |
| Large or event venue | A real-time booking tool | Needs live availability |

The studio says click-to-chat buttons open a pre-filled message to your number. Here is a template, as my suggestion:

- **Table:** "Hi, I would like a table for [number] on [date] at [time]. Name: [name]."
- **Order:** "Hi, I would like to order [dish and quantity] for [pickup or delivery]. Address: [address]."

Online booking is scoped as an option, and the studio quotes real-time booking after a call.

## Google Maps: Your Business Profile Does the Finding

Google Maps finds you through your Google Business Profile, and the website's job is to match it. The studio's restaurant FAQ says Maps depends mainly on the profile, and that it helps set it up and match the details on the site. Mantis says it found the restaurants and cafés in its index on Google Maps by category and area.

Keep these the same on your profile, site and app listings:

- [ ] Name, address and phone
- [ ] Opening hours and holiday notes
- [ ] The menu page link
- [ ] The WhatsApp or order link
- [ ] Directions and a map embed on the site
- [ ] FSSAI licence number displayed

The studio notes that food businesses in India are expected to display their FSSAI licence number, and it includes a place for it in the footer and menu pages. Confirm the current requirement with FSSAI, as this article is not legal advice.

## What Usually Goes Wrong With Restaurant Websites

Most problems come from setup and upkeep, not design. The causes below come from the sources' own signals, and the fixes are my reading.

| What goes wrong | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Menu opens slowly | PDF or large photos | Text page, compressed images |
| Wrong hours | Profile, site and apps edited separately | One list, updated together |
| No idea what works | No analytics (56% in Mantis's sample) | Track calls, WhatsApp taps and form sends |
| Orders get lost | WhatsApp checked by one person | Name who watches the chat |
| Ordering built without a plan | No riders or pickup rule | Decide delivery first |
| UPI promised too soon | Gateway not confirmed | Confirm UPI with your gateway |

## FAQ

### How do I make a restaurant website?

Make one page first: menu, photos, hours, map and a WhatsApp button, then add ordering if the numbers justify it. The studio's restaurant page lists these as the core. Test it on mobile data before launch.

### Do restaurants and cafés really need a website?

Yes, a small one, because the apps do not give you a page you control. The studio's restaurant page frames the site's job as moving someone from deciding where to eat to your door or WhatsApp. Skip a big build until your numbers support it.

### Is a website better than Swiggy and Zomato?

Neither is better for every case: the apps find you strangers and a website serves regulars without commission. Mantis says restaurants pay commission for discovery on the apps. Most places use both.

### Can customers order on WhatsApp instead of the apps?

Yes, through a click-to-chat button with a pre-filled message. The studio says this takes a tap and pays no commission to a platform. Confirm how you will take payment, and UPI with your gateway first.

### What should a restaurant menu page look like?

It should be a text page with a name, price, short description and availability for each dish. The studio warns against PDF menus that open slowly on 4G. Keep prices consistent with your app listings or say why they differ.

### Do I need a website to show on Google Maps?

No, because Maps depends mainly on your Google Business Profile, according to the studio's restaurant FAQ. A website supports it with matching details. Keep your profile accurate either way.

### Is there a good template for an Indian restaurant website?

Templates exist, but I cannot say which is good because I read only their titles. Test any template on phone speed, text menus, WhatsApp buttons and an FSSAI field. A build suits you better when you need ordering.
