A local business can do solid keyword research with four free keyword research tools: Google Keyword Planner for rough search volume, Search Console for what your own site already earns, Google autocomplete for the exact phrases people type, and People Also Ask for the questions they ask. None needs a paid subscription, only an afternoon and a habit of writing every finding into one sheet. The honest qualification is that Keyword Planner's numbers are estimates, Search Console needs a live site, and my evidence for the paid tools' free limits is one review and the vendors' own pages, all read on 20 November 2026.

- **The four.** Keyword Planner gives volume, Search Console gives your real queries, autocomplete gives wording, People Also Ask gives questions.
- **Free tiers help.** Several paid tools give a few free searches a day, enough for a small list.
- **Local beats big.** A city plus service phrase usually serves a local business better than a high-volume generic one.
- **One page each.** Map every phrase you keep to one page before you write or build anything.

## Which Free Keyword Research Tools Does a Local Business Need?

A local business needs the four Google-side tools in the table below, because between them they answer the four questions that matter: how often, what already works, how people word it and what they ask. The table is my own grouping, built from the tools described later in this article.

| Tool | Question it answers | What you need |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword Planner | Roughly how often is this searched? | A Google sign-in through Google Ads |
| Search Console | Which searches already bring people to my site? | A live site you can verify |
| Autocomplete | How do people actually word this? | A search box |
| People Also Ask | What do they want to know next? | A search box |

The free tiers of paid tools add a second opinion on volume and difficulty. If you have no site yet, which is common for students and new owners, skip Search Console for now and use the other three.

## Start With Seed Phrases and a City Modifier

A seed phrase is a plain description of what you sell, and a local business gets more from adding the place to it. Write down five to ten seeds before opening any tool: the service in the words a customer would use, with your area, city or "near me" attached.

Semrush's own guide gives location-based phrases such as "restaurants near me" as the way to improve local SEO with a keyword tool. In India, add the second layer yourself: the Roman-script spelling of the Hindi or Kannada word customers use for your service. This is my reading, not a sourced rule, and your own customers' messages are the best place to find those words.

## Google Keyword Planner: Free, With Strings Attached

Google Keyword Planner is free to use, but it is built for advertisers, so its competition and bid figures describe paid search, not organic ranking. Zapier's July 2026 review calls it completely free even if you never buy ads, and says you get more out of it if you do. Google's own page shows the tool's job: find keywords for your products, see how often they are searched and how that has changed, and get suggested bid estimates.

Zapier lists what each idea shows: monthly search volume, change over time, competition, ad impression share and bid ranges. It also says the tool can suggest keywords from just your website, which is a quick way to start. Zapier rates it as not as helpful for organic research.

Three cautions apply:

- **Volume is an estimate.** Wordtracker, a competitor, says Keyword Planner's numbers are scaled from a sample and that similar keywords are grouped. Semrush pitches its own free tool as an alternative with no hidden ranges. Treat each figure as a rough size, never an exact count.
- **Competition means advertisers.** Wordtracker says competition and other metrics are given only for paid search. Do not read it as how hard ranking will be.
- **Bid ranges are relative.** Semrush treats a CPC of roughly $3 or more as a sign that a keyword drives revenue. That is a dollar figure from a vendor, so for Indian local phrases I would compare bids across your own list instead.

Set the location to your city or state if the tool offers it, because India-wide volume hides local demand. Check on screen what it asks for at sign-in, since I could not confirm the current account steps from Google's page.

## Search Console: What Your Own Site Already Earns

Search Console shows the actual searches that brought people to your site, which no estimate tool can do. The sources I worked from mention it only in passing (Zapier notes that Semrush can connect to it to uncover search terms Analytics hides), so this section is my description of the steps. Check Google's own Search Console help for current screens.

1. **Verify your site.** Make sure you, not your developer, are the verified owner. My article on [who owns your website and Google accounts](/blog/who-owns-my-website-code/) explains why.
2. **Open the Performance report.** Look at the Queries view: each search phrase with clicks, impressions and average position.
3. **Sort by impressions.** Phrases where you appear often but rarely get clicked, or sit just off the first page, are the ones worth a better page.
4. **Filter by page.** Check which queries each page already attracts, then write down the ones you did not plan for.

A new site shows little at first, so do not read an empty report as proof that nobody searches. ## Autocomplete: The Gap Every Top Page Skips

Google autocomplete is a free list of phrases people really type, and none of the five top-ranking pages in the research behind this article explains how to use it. It shows wording, not volume: a suggestion tells you the phrase exists, and its position is at best a weak hint of popularity.

Work through it in this order:

1. Type your seed phrase and note every suggestion.
2. Add one letter after the seed, from a to z, and note what appears.
3. Add question words: how, what, which, can.
4. Add prepositions such as near, in and for, then your area name.
5. Repeat in a private window, since suggestions can vary by place and account.

Zapier describes AnswerThePublic as splitting ideas into related, questions, prepositions, alphabeticals and numbers. Those are the same cuts as the steps above, done by a tool, and the free plan allows 3 searches a day. If you do it by hand, doing the alphabet for your two best seeds takes 2 seeds × 26 letters = 52 checks.

## People Also Ask: Questions Become Your Sections

The People Also Ask box on a results page lists questions related to your search, and each is a ready-made heading or FAQ entry for your page. Search a phrase such as your service plus your city, open every question, and keep opening the new ones that appear.

Record each question in your sheet next to the phrase that produced it. Semrush notes that question keywords are natural long-tail phrases and that AI answer tools pull from them. SE Ranking sorts suggestions into similar, related, low-volume and question-based groups. Both are vendor claims, so my advice rests on a simpler point: a question you can answer in a sentence belongs on a page.

The box does not appear for every search. Treat it as a bonus and rely on autocomplete when it is missing.

### Can an AI chatbot do the job?

An AI chatbot can give quick keyword ideas, but not trustworthy numbers. Zapier found ChatGPT and Claude useful for one-off research, then added that they offer less precise numerical data than Keyword Planner or Semrush and have no clean way to track a keyword list. Use a chatbot to brainstorm phrases, then check each one in a tool.

## How to Choose Which Keywords to Keep

Keep a phrase when your page can satisfy it, the search intent matches the page, and you can realistically compete. Semrush's guide gives the checks; the local reading is mine.

- **Relevance first.** A phrase must match something you really offer. Semrush's example is "running shoe repair" for a store that only sells new shoes.
- **Intent matches the page.** Search the phrase yourself. If the first page is blog posts, it is informational; if it is product or service pages, it is commercial.
- **Volume.** Semrush calls 100 to 1,000 monthly searches a practical start for newer sites and says near-zero volume is rarely worth its own page. For a city plus service phrase, I would keep a lower number when the searcher is clearly a local customer.
- **Difficulty.** Semrush advises newer sites to aim under 30 on its 0 to 100 scale, and says above 80 is dominated by major brands. Scales differ between tools, so never compare a score from one tool with another's.
- **Avoid branded and broad terms.** Semrush warns that branded phrases send people to that brand and broad ones return results that do not match what you offer.

## A Worked Example: One Afternoon, Zero Rupees

Here is the method on a made-up business, Sunrise Physio, a fictional physiotherapy clinic in Jayanagar, Bengaluru. It is an illustration, not a client, and the phrases are examples, not measured data.

1. **Seeds (15 minutes).** Write five: physiotherapy, back pain physiotherapy, sports injury physio, home visit physiotherapy, and the clinic's area name.
2. **Autocomplete (30 minutes).** Run the alphabet on the two best seeds, 2 × 26 = 52 checks, then add near, in and your area.
3. **Keyword Planner (20 minutes).** Enter the seeds and the clinic's site address, set the city, and copy the ideas and rough volumes.
4. **People Also Ask (20 minutes).** Open the questions for the three best phrases and record them.
5. **Second opinion (10 minutes).** Over a five-day week, KWFinder's free tier allows 5 × 5 = 25 searches and AnswerThePublic's 3 × 5 = 15.

The time budget is my own planning guess, not a measurement. The result is a sheet like this:

| Phrase | Found by | Intent | Page |
|---|---|---|---|
| physiotherapist in Jayanagar | Seed plus area | Local service | Home |
| physiotherapy near me | Autocomplete | Local service | Home |
| back pain physiotherapy Bengaluru | Keyword Planner idea | Service | Back pain page |
| physiotherapy home visit Bengaluru | Autocomplete | Service | Home visits page |
| how many physio sessions for back pain | People Also Ask | Informational | FAQ on back pain page |

Each phrase lands on exactly one page. Our guide to [the pages a small business website needs](/blog/what-pages-should-a-business-website-have/) helps you decide which pages deserve to exist.

## What Usually Goes Wrong With Free Keyword Research

Most free keyword research fails in the choosing, not the finding. The table lists the common mistakes, with the cause as the sources describe it and the fix.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Reading Planner competition as SEO difficulty | It measures advertisers, per Wordtracker | Judge difficulty from the results |
| Chasing the biggest number | Broad terms favour big brands, per Semrush | Pick city plus service phrases |
| Keeping branded or off-service phrases | Searchers want another brand or product | Drop them |
| Two pages for one phrase | Your pages compete, per Semrush | One page per phrase group |
| Trusting a chatbot's volumes | Less precise than a dedicated tool, per Zapier | Use it for ideas only |
| Researching with no page to publish | The list is never used | Map phrases to pages first |

## FAQ

### Are there any free keyword research tools?

Yes: Google Keyword Planner, Search Console, Google autocomplete and People Also Ask cost nothing. Paid tools such as Semrush, KWFinder and AnswerThePublic also give a limited free allowance, for example 3 to 10 searches or reports a day.

### Is Google Keyword Planner really free?

Zapier's July 2026 review says it is completely free even if you never buy Google Ads. Expect to sign in through Google Ads, and Zapier notes you get more out of the tool if you run ads.

### What is the best free keyword research tool for a local business?

There is no single best, but Keyword Planner plus autocomplete is the best start. Add Search Console once your site has traffic, because it shows the real searches that already reach you.

### Can I do keyword research with Search Console alone?

Search Console alone is enough only for finding phrases you already earn impressions for. It cannot show demand you are not yet appearing for, so pair it with Keyword Planner and autocomplete.

### How do I find keywords with Google autocomplete?

Type your seed phrase into Google and note the suggestions, then add each letter of the alphabet, question words and place names. Repeat in a private window and record every phrase in one sheet.

### How do I use People Also Ask for keyword research?

Search a service plus your city, open each question in the box and keep expanding new ones. Each question is a candidate heading or FAQ entry for the page that targets that phrase.

### Are free keyword tools accurate?

They are estimates, not counts. Wordtracker says Keyword Planner's numbers are scaled from a sample and similar keywords are grouped, so use volume to compare phrases and not to forecast visitors.

### Can ChatGPT or Claude do keyword research?

They can suggest phrases quickly, but not reliable volumes. Zapier found them less precise on numbers than dedicated tools, so check every suggestion in a tool.

### When should I upgrade from free tools?

Upgrade when you keep hitting daily limits and the research is costing you more time than the subscription would. This is my reading, not a sourced rule, and most single-location businesses can stay on free tiers for a long time.
