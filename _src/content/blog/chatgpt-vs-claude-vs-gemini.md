ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini has no single winner for a small business: the sources point to Claude for careful writing and editing, Gemini if your work lives in Gmail, Docs and Sheets, and ChatGPT as the all-round default with strong voice and image tools. The honest qualification is that the published tests behind that split are dated and mostly one person's prompts, and this page ran no tests of its own. Models and plans changed within three months of 2026, so confirm any pick with the afternoon test below.

- **Jobs.** Claude is rated for long-form editing and detailed instructions, Gemini for Google apps and audio or video, ChatGPT for general help, voice and structured reports.
- **Price.** The main paid tier of each tool is about $20 a month in US list price (9to5AI, checked 20 October 2026), so price rarely decides.
- **Privacy.** All three can use consumer chats for training unless you change a setting, and business plans and the API work differently.
- **Test.** Run five of your own tasks through all three before you pay.

## ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini: The Criteria and the Limits of This Page

This comparison judges the three tools on six criteria: fit for the job, price, privacy, usage limits, fit with the apps you already use, and how much checking the output needs. It holds no first-hand tests, because none was supplied. Every result below is attributed to whoever produced it, and my own reading is labelled as mine.

The sources, read on 20 November 2026, are 9to5AI (plan prices and privacy, checked October 2026), UC Berkeley's School of Information (API prices and one data test, July 2026), Artificial Corner (one author's prompts), Type.ai (a writing comparison on 2025 models, from a company that sells model access) and the Lumoset Foundation's August 2026 chooser. None is a controlled study of small-business work.

If you want one of these models answering customers on your website or WhatsApp instead of you pasting text into a chat window, that is a different job, which the [AI chatbot service](/services/ai-chatbots/) covers.

## Which Is Better for Which Job? A Decision Table

Choose by the job, not the brand, because Berkeley's School of Information concludes that no model wins every use case. On 15 October 2026 LMArena had Gemini 4 Argon ahead, on 19 October Vals AI had Claude Sonnet 5.5 narrowly ahead, and Artificial Analysis had Claude Opus 5.5 ahead (all as reported by 9to5AI).

The table turns Lumoset's August 2026 chooser and Artificial Corner's tests into a starting point.

| Job | Start with | Why, and from whom |
|---|---|---|
| Polish long writing, follow a detailed edit | Claude | Lumoset: long-form editing; Artificial Corner: follows long prompts closely |
| Everyday questions, explanations, voice chat | ChatGPT | Lumoset: general help and voice; Artificial Corner: best voice mode |
| Work inside Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Drive | Gemini | Lumoset: best fit for Google apps |
| Audio or video analysis | Gemini | Artificial Corner: best, and ChatGPT could not analyse audio then |
| Images and infographics | Gemini or ChatGPT | Artificial Corner: Gemini best, ChatGPT close |
| A structured report to share | ChatGPT | Berkeley's test: the most documentation-ready output |

Choose ChatGPT when you want one tool for many small jobs and a natural voice mode. Choose Claude when the job is editing your own words without having them rewritten. Choose Gemini when your inbox, documents and sheets are already in Google, or when the input is a recording or a video.

The other option wins in specific places: Artificial Corner's author prefers Claude but hits its daily limit often, and found ChatGPT and Gemini better for everyday volume. My reading is that if you open Gmail all day, Gemini's fit with it matters more than any ranking.

## Emails and Content: What Published Tests Show

No source I hold ran a head-to-head test on business emails, so the evidence for emails and content is indirect. The closest is Artificial Corner's author, who tested a proofreading prompt that asked for deletions in red strikethrough and insertions in blue. Claude marked the changes correctly and ChatGPT got it wrong, and on a "light edit, keep my meaning and voice" request Claude held to the instruction while ChatGPT sometimes rewrote whole sentences.

Type.ai's writing comparison, written for 2025 models, found Claude's prose more expressive and ChatGPT's drier but useful for research and first drafts. Lumoset's chooser agrees on the split, placing long-form editing with Claude and work inside Gmail and Docs with Gemini.

My reading: for a customer email in your own voice, how well a tool obeys "change only the grammar" matters more than how good its prose is. For website copy, the guide to [writing website copy that sells when you are not a writer](/blog/website-copywriting-tips/) covers what to prepare first.

Language is a gap in the evidence. Type.ai says Claude handles about 50 languages but trained mostly in English, and that ChatGPT is fluent in more than 100. No source I hold rates Hindi or Kannada, so test a real reply in your customers' language.

## Analysis: Spreadsheets, Reports and Long Documents

For analysis, the one controlled test I hold found all three tools caught the same planted error, so the differences were in presentation and depth. Berkeley's School of Information gave each tool the same 12-row sales table with one missing UnitsSold value in row 1011, and all three caught it without being told.

ChatGPT inferred 35 units from the consistent unit price and produced a structured, shareable output. Gemini was the most technically precise and flagged a data-type risk unprompted. The part of the page I could read does not give Claude's detailed result, so I do not rank Claude here.

You can verify the inference yourself, which is the habit to copy. The other Widget B rows give 1,800 ÷ 30 = 60, 3,600 ÷ 60 = 60 and 2,400 ÷ 40 = 60 per unit, so row 1011's revenue of 2,100 ÷ 60 = 35 units. The data is 12 invented rows, not your accounts.

For long documents, 9to5AI reports on 20 October 2026 that Claude's paid plans and Google AI Pro and Ultra list a 1M-token window in the chat apps, and ChatGPT's largest is 400K on Pro. At Berkeley's rule of 1,000 tokens to about 750 words, that is roughly 750,000 words against 300,000. 9to5AI adds that a big window is not the same as good recall deep inside a long document.

### API Cost: A Worked Example for a Business Chatbot

If you build a model into your own tool, API prices decide the bill, and Berkeley found Gemini 3.0 Pro the cheapest per million tokens in July 2026. The inputs below are my assumptions, not measurements: 2,000 customer conversations a month, each with 1,500 tokens in and 300 tokens out, which is 3 million tokens in and 0.6 million out.

| Model (July 2026 prices) | Input | Output | Monthly total |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPT-5.5 | 3 × $5 = $15 | 0.6 × $30 = $18 | $33 |
| Claude Sonnet 4.6 | 3 × $3 = $9 | 0.6 × $15 = $9 | $18 |
| Gemini 3.0 Pro | 3 × $2 = $6 | 0.6 × $12 = $7.20 | $13.20 |

At this volume the gap between the dearest and the cheapest is $33 minus $13.20, or $19.80 a month, so accuracy matters more than price. Model generations had moved on by October, and I hold no October API prices, so recheck before you budget. Ask any studio that builds on these models to quote the model usage separately from its own fee.

## Data Privacy: Training, Retention and Ads

All three consumer apps can use your chats for training unless you turn a setting off, per 9to5AI's check of each provider's privacy and help pages on 20 October 2026. My reading is that this makes the free and $20 tiers the wrong place for customer details.

The sources disagree on Claude. Berkeley's July 2026 page said Claude consumer users must opt in, and 9to5AI corrected its own page on 28 October to say Anthropic's Consumer Terms allow training unless you opt out, and still use chats you give feedback on or that are flagged for safety review.

I follow the later source, which cites the provider's own terms, but open the setting yourself. The table uses 9to5AI for the apps and Berkeley (July 2026) for the API.

| What | ChatGPT | Claude | Gemini |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consumer chats used for training | Unless you opt out | Unless you opt out | Unless you opt out |
| Ads in the app | Documented | None documented | None documented |
| API data used for training | No | No | No |
| API logs kept | Up to 30 days | 7 days | Up to 55 days |
| Zero data retention | Eligible enterprise | Enterprise | Vertex AI, qualifying enterprise |

OpenAI's help centre says ads appear below answers and not in Temporary Chats, next to sensitive topics or for under-18 accounts, per 9to5AI. Berkeley recommends API or enterprise tiers for client data and a read of each provider's privacy agreement first. Lumoset says to keep passwords, account numbers, IDs and private medical, legal or financial details out of prompts.

My reading: a clinic, CA firm or coaching centre should not paste identifiable client details into any consumer app. Replace names with "Client A" first.

## Indian Rules to Check Before You Paste Customer Data

Check India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act with a qualified adviser before pasting customer data into any AI tool, because the research for this page holds no text of it. I state none of its duties here, and rules change, so confirm them yourself. This section was checked on 20 November 2026.

Two Indian primary documents are in my sources, and neither is a rulebook for a small shop. The Reserve Bank of India's FREE-AI committee report is written for RBI-regulated financial entities, names data protection among the risks AI can amplify, and recommends a board-approved AI policy and telling consumers when they deal with AI.

The DPIIT working paper on generative AI and copyright is a committee draft put out for feedback, not law. Its first part covers works used to train AI and leaves the authorship of AI-generated output to a second part, so I cannot tell you who owns text an AI wrote for you. Ask a lawyer before you rely on AI-drafted brand copy.

Borrowing from both documents and Lumoset's framework, a one-page AI policy needs five lines:

- [ ] Which tool and plan staff may use for work.
- [ ] What never goes into a prompt: passwords, PAN or Aadhaar numbers, account numbers, private health or legal details.
- [ ] The training setting is off on every work account.
- [ ] A named person reads anything before it reaches a customer.
- [ ] Customers are told when they are talking to an AI.

## Perplexity, Copilot, Grok and Coding: When a Fourth Tool Earns a Place

A fourth tool earns a place only when it fits an app or a job the big three handle less well, and Lumoset's August 2026 chooser names three such cases. It lists Perplexity for quick source discovery, Copilot for Word, Excel, Outlook and Teams in Microsoft 365, and Grok for a current social and news signal. Lumoset says to open Perplexity's sources before deciding and to keep Grok away from sensitive, official or legal decisions.

### ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini for Coding

For coding, my sources give a split and a warning rather than a winner. Berkeley says ChatGPT is strong on well-documented frameworks and Gemini is competitive and cheap for high-volume code generation, and that any model output going into production code needs human review. Artificial Corner repeats a second-hand finding, which it attributes to Peter Yang, that Claude was best at coding.

## Test the Three on Your Own Work in One Afternoon

You can run a fair three-way test in about two hours by giving all three tools the same five real tasks and scoring the results on one sheet. The times are my estimates.

1. **Pick five tasks (15 minutes).** One customer email, one piece of marketing copy, one table or report, one long document, and one task in your customers' language.
2. **Match the tiers (5 minutes).** Compare free with free or $20 with $20, because plans run different models.
3. **Run identical prompts (30 minutes).** Use anonymised data, and give each tool the same wording.
4. **Score each result (30 minutes).** Mark 1 to 5 for accuracy, following instructions, sounding like you, and minutes needed to fix it.
5. **Check the facts yourself (25 minutes).** Recompute one figure by hand, as in the 2,100 ÷ 60 check above.
6. **Record and repeat (15 minutes).** Write down the model names and the date, and re-run every quarter.

Two prompts to copy, written in the spirit of the tests above:

- "Light edit only. Keep my meaning and voice, and show every change you made."
- "Check this table for missing or odd values before you summarise it, and show your arithmetic."

## FAQ

### Is ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini better?

None is better at everything. Berkeley's School of Information finds no universal winner, and three evaluators named three different leaders in October 2026 (9to5AI).

### How much do ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini cost?

As of 20 October 2026, 9to5AI reports the main paid tier of each is about $20 a month in US list price, before tax. Your rupee price depends on tax and the exchange rate at checkout.

### When should I use ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini?

Use ChatGPT for general help, voice and structured reports, Claude for long-form editing and detailed instructions, and Gemini for Google apps and audio or video. That split comes from Lumoset's August 2026 chooser and Artificial Corner's tests.

### Is Claude better than ChatGPT for writing?

Claude is the stronger choice for editing in your own voice, according to Artificial Corner and Lumoset, and Type.ai found its prose more expressive. The tests are dated and small, so run your own on a real email.

### Which is best for analysing a spreadsheet?

In Berkeley's single test, all three caught the same missing value, so none is proven best. ChatGPT gave the most shareable report and Gemini the most technically precise one.

### Do ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini train on my chats?

Their consumer apps can, unless you turn the setting off, per 9to5AI's check on 20 October 2026. Berkeley (July 2026) reports that API data is not used for training by any of the three.

### Which is cheapest through the API?

Gemini 3.0 Pro was the cheapest of the three in July 2026, at $2 per million input tokens and $12 per million output tokens, per Berkeley. Models have changed since, so recheck current prices.

### Is the free version enough for a small business?

The free version is enough for learning and occasional drafts, as long as no private customer data goes in. Lumoset advises paying only when limits block real work or you need better privacy controls.
