## Website Requirements Document, Brief or Specification: What Is the Difference?

A website requirements document, a brief and a specification are the same idea at different lengths: a written statement of what the site must do, agreed before the build. Mindspun defines a website requirements document as a specification of the project's purpose, goals, functions, budget, deadlines and technical constraints. Its stated aim is that the site owner and the development team agree on the details.

Figr, a design-tools company, separates four formal types: a product requirements document (PRD), a business requirements document (BRD), a software requirements specification (SRS) and a functional specification (FSD). Its table gives each a different main question, such as "what business need does this address" for a BRD and "what must the software do" for an SRS.

My reading: a small-business website brief is a BRD and PRD in miniature. It needs the business reason, the visitor and the page list, and it can leave the system-level detail to the builder.

## Basic Requirements for a Website: Five Things Every Brief Must Settle

The basic requirements for a website are the goal, the visitor, the pages, the content and the money, and a brief that settles those five is already ahead of most. Feedbucket's template for agencies lists 21 items, and in my reading they group into the same five once you set the design preferences aside.

1. **Goal.** The one action a visitor should take, such as booking a demo class. Feedbucket calls this the desired action and says every design decision should point to it.
2. **Visitor.** Who they are, what device they use and what they ask before they act.
3. **Pages.** The minimum set that must exist at launch. Feedbucket says defining this early prevents scope creep.
4. **Content.** Who writes the words and supplies the photos, and by when.
5. **Money and time.** A budget range, a launch date and one person who decides.

## Website Development Requirements Document: What a Public-Sector Version Asks For

A public-sector website requirements document is a long, formal version of the same brief, and it shows which parts a small business should borrow. Assam Women's University published one in 2022 (by the date in its web address) for a site serving 8 schools, 15 departments and 16 programmes.

It asks for a role-based content management system with separate admin, department and data-entry users, backup and restore, an activity log, daily backups, migration of the existing site's content, and three years of support. It also sets accessibility and quality standards (GIGW, WCAG), English and Assamese, and ownership of the source code, text and design by the university.

My reading of what to borrow:

- **Ownership.** Write who owns the code, content and accounts at handover.
- **Backups.** Ask how the site is backed up and restored.
- **Migration.** If you are redesigning, list what must be carried over.
- **Support period.** Say how long you expect help after launch.

My reading of what to leave out: a progressive web app, GraphQL and 24x7 on-call support are sized for a university, not a clinic or a coaching centre.

## Filling In the Brief: A Sequence That Works in One Sitting

Work through the brief in the order of Parts 1 to 5, because each part depends on the one before. This is my suggested sequence, not a sourced method.

1. **Write the goal first.** Mindspun recommends SMART goals: specific, measurable, assignable, realistic and time-related. "More enquiries" fails that test; "demo-class enquiries per month, counted from three channels" passes it.
2. **List the visitor's questions.** Write the three questions a customer asks you on a call or on WhatsApp. Those become your page list.
3. **Split launch from later.** Mindspun suggests phases, for example a marketing site first, then e-commerce, then a CRM. Anything in phase two goes under "Not in version one".
4. **Gather the assets.** Feedbucket advises one shared folder for logos, photos and copy, because waiting for files can halt a project.
5. **Name the decision maker.** Feedbucket recommends one primary contact and a list of everyone else who will weigh in.
6. **Put the money down.** A range and a launch date, even if rough.

## Website Template or Requirements Template? Where Themes and AI Builders Fit

A website template is a ready-made design you fill with your content, while a website requirements template is a form that records what you need, and a brief helps you choose between buying the first or commissioning a build. My decision rule, which is a framework and not a sourced benchmark:

| Choose a theme or builder when | Choose a custom build when |
|---|---|
| You need five pages or fewer | You need booking, payments or logins |
| Nobody must integrate anything | You need WhatsApp, tracking or a CRM wired in |
| You can edit and maintain it yourself | Your team needs an editing screen built for them |

## What Usually Goes Wrong With Website Briefs

Most brief failures come from vagueness and missing people, not from missing fields. Feedbucket's author says skipping the planning step led to missed deadlines, scope creep, budget overruns and strained client relationships.

| What goes wrong | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "A modern site" as the goal | No measurable action | One action and a count, as above |
| A surprise opinion at the final review | Stakeholders never listed | Name everyone, one decides |
| Work stalls | Photos and copy not ready | Shared folder, dated hand-over |
| Features creep in mid-project | No "not now" list | Part 2 and phases |
| Budget argument late | Range never stated | Range and GST in Part 5 |
| Unclear ownership | Handover not written | Ownership row in Part 4 |
| The brief is treated as frozen | Fear of change | Mindspun says work is iterative, so date each version |

## FAQ

### What are website requirements?

Website requirements are the written list of what a site must do, who it serves, which pages and features it needs, and the limits on budget, time and technology. Mindspun describes the document as covering purpose, goals, functions, budget, deadlines and technical constraints. The template above turns that into 26 fields.

### What is required to make a website?

To make a website you need a goal, a page list, your content, a domain and hosting, and a builder or a template to put them together. The brief records each of those decisions. Pages with payments or logins add technical work.

### What should a website requirements document include?

A website requirements document should include the goal, audience, pages, features, content owner, design preferences, technical rules, budget and launch date. Feedbucket's list has 21 items and Mindspun's has 15 sections. Add an ownership line for code, content and accounts.

### What is a website template?

A website template is a pre-built design layout that you fill with your own text and images. It is different from a requirements template, which is a form you complete before building. Search results mix the two terms.

### How do I use a website requirements template?

Copy the first two columns of each table into a document, answer in the order of Parts 1 to 5, and send the result to your builder or keep it for a template. Finish with a budget range and a launch date. Update it and date each version when plans change.

### How much does it cost to buy a website template?

My research has no price data for theme marketplaces, so check the seller's own page. For a site built for you, the indicative range is {{price:landing}} for a landing page or {{price:website}} for a business website, excluding 18% GST. Those are draft ranges, never a quote.

### How long should a website brief be?

A small-business brief can be a few pages, and the template above has 26 fields in five parts. Feedbucket's agency template has 21 items. Length matters less than whether each answer is specific.

### Do I need a brief if I use a template or an AI builder?

Yes, because the brief decides which route fits and gives the builder or the tool what it needs. Writing it first stops you buying a theme that cannot do booking or payments. It costs an afternoon.

### Does a brief replace the discovery call?

No. The studio starts with a discovery call and then sends a written scope, timeline and fixed price. A brief makes the call shorter and the scope easier to write.
