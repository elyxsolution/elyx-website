How long does SEO take? For most business websites, the sources I read put the first measurable results at three to six months and clear gains in enquiries or revenue at six to twelve, while technical fixes can show up in weeks. A new domain, a crowded search or a slow developer pushes you to the long end, and competitive searches can run past a year. The honest qualification is that every figure comes from an agency, a tool maker or a poll, and Google's own advice page on hiring an SEO gives no fixed number of months.

- **The range.** Three to six months for first measurable results, six to twelve for business impact, as of November 2026 per Search Engine Land and SEO.com.
- **What moves it.** Site age, competition, how fast changes go live and whether Google can crawl the site.
- **What speeds it up.** Indexable pages, low-competition searches first, internal links and an early Business Profile. Paying Google or a link seller does not.
- **How to judge it.** Count impressions and enquiries (calls, WhatsApp taps, forms), not rankings alone.

## How Long Does SEO Take to Work? What Six Published Timelines Say

Six published timelines overlap on one idea: first measurable results at roughly three to six months, and meaningful business impact at six to twelve months or more. I built the table from each page as retrieved on 19 November 2026; the last column is my reading of who is speaking, not the publishers' own statement.

| Source | Timeline it gives | What it counts as working | Who is speaking |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/guide/how-long-does-seo-take-to-work) | 3 to 6 months for measurable results; 6 to 12 in competitive industries | Ranking and revenue impact; technical fixes can give early gains within weeks | Search marketing publisher |
| Google's Maile Ohye, as quoted by Search Engine Land | 4 months to a year | Implementing improvements first, then seeing the benefit | Google, in a video Search Engine Land says is about nine years old |
| [Orbit Media](https://www.orbitmedia.com/blog/how-long-does-seo-take/) | 2 to 3 months for low-competition keywords; 4 to 12 months for significant success; 2 to 3 years for high-competition keywords | Rankings | Web marketing agency |
| [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-seo-take/) | 3 to 6 months, from a poll of 3,680 LinkedIn and X respondents | Showing results | SEO tool maker |
| [SEO.com](https://www.seo.com/blog/how-long-does-seo-take/) (updated 29 October 2026) | 3 to 6 months, some sites 6 to 12; consistent leads at 3 to 6; revenue at 6 to 12 | Traffic, leads, sales | SEO agency |
| [Squarespace](https://www.squarespace.com/blog/how-long-does-seo-take) | 4 to 12 months | Traction in search | Site-builder blog, written by an SEO plugin founder |

Orbit attributes its 4 to 12 months to Google, which matches the Ohye quote, so treat those two as one source, not two. Ahrefs's poll counts opinions, not outcomes.

My reading: "first results" means impressions and low-competition rankings, and "results" for a business means enquiries, usually three to six months later.

## Month by Month: A Realistic First-Year Timeline

A realistic first year has three phases: foundations in months 1 to 3, first visible movement in months 3 to 6, and business results from month 6 to 12. The work column comes from the example plans published by SEO.com (months 1 to 6) and Squarespace (months 1 to 12), and the last column is my reading, built from Search Engine Land's list of early signals. Both plans are agency examples, not a schedule anyone can promise.

| Month | Work in the example plans | What you can reasonably expect to see |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set up Search Console and Analytics, check crawling and indexing, audit the site, review competitors, tidy titles and URLs | No ranking change. A written list of problems and a plan |
| 2 | Fix the most serious audit issues, claim the Business Profile and Bing Places, put keywords on the most valuable pages | Fewer crawl errors and broken links |
| 3 | Put keywords on other key pages, research link-worthy topics, keep fixing | Impressions starting to rise in Search Console |
| 4 to 5 | Evaluate results, reach out to relevant sites, improve internal linking, plan new topics | First rankings on low-competition searches |
| 6 to 7 | Six-month review, plan the next quarter, build authority with expert content and reviews | A steadier flow of enquiries for some sites |
| 8 to 9 | Press, guest posts, reviews, consistent name, address and phone across listings | Growth in visits and enquiries; crowded searches still moving |
| 10 to 12 | Compare month-over-month data, adjust keywords, extend local presence and trust signals | Revenue impact for many sites; a view on payback |

The studio's own page for [SEO optimization](/services/seo-optimization/) sequences an engagement in a similar order: an audit in week 1, technical fixes in weeks 1 to 3, service and location pages plus the Business Profile in weeks 2 to 6, then articles from month 2 with a monthly report. It says technical fixes show up in weeks and rankings for competitive local searches usually take a few months.

## Why Does SEO Take So Long?

SEO takes months because Google has to find, assess and trust your pages, and the signals that count most, such as links from other sites and a reputation, build slowly while competitors keep working. Search Engine Land names three delays: how old the site is, how long your team takes to ship the changes, and how long Google needs to reassess them. It adds that JavaScript-heavy sites can take nine times longer to crawl.

SEO.com points to more than 200 ranking factors and calls backlinks one of the biggest. Three popular explanations deserve a label.

- **The sandbox.** A supposed holding period for new sites. Search Engine Land says it is not an official Google feature and that some practitioners think it lasts months or years, while Squarespace puts it at one to three months.
- **Rank transition.** Search Engine Land quotes a Google patent that describes a rank dipping for about 20 days and settling in about 70 days after a positive change in link information. A patent is not proof of how Google ranks pages today, but it fits the experience of a page that dips before it rises.
- **Algorithm updates.** Squarespace says they roll out over several weeks, so wait until one finishes before reacting.

## How Long Does SEO Take for a New Website?

A new website usually takes longer than an established one, because Google has no history, links or search demand for the name to go on. Search Engine Land lists limited backlink data, little branded search demand and minimal engagement signals as the gaps on a new domain. Ahrefs quotes Google's John Mueller saying it could take up to a year for Google to work out where to rank new sites.

Showing up and ranking are different. SEO.com quotes former Google engineer Matt Cutts saying a site a couple of months old should be able to show up, but that is easier than ranking for a buying search such as "dentist near me".

Two things shorten the wait for a local business. The studio's SEO page says most small businesses are found through their Business Profile, and both SEO.com and Squarespace schedule it in the first two months or earlier. Google's hiring guidance adds that the best time to bring in an SEO is while planning a redesign or launching a new site, so the site is search-friendly from the start; the [website launch checklist](/blog/website-launch-checklist/) covers the checks to run before go-live.

## How Long Do SEO Changes Take After a Redesign or Site Move?

After a redesign or a URL change, expect ranking movement for a few weeks or more, and longer on a large site, according to Google's site-move documentation. For medium-sized sites Google says it can take a few weeks or more for the new URLs to replace the old ones, and a move is only complete when Googlebot has visited every URL at least once. Google also says permanent 301 redirects do not lose PageRank.

Its advice maps onto a short checklist:

- Change one thing at a time: domain, CMS and layout in separate moves.
- Keep redirects for at least a year.
- Remove any noindex rule or robots.txt block left over from development.
- Submit the new sitemap in Search Console.

If you are weighing a rebuild, [9 signs your website needs a redesign](/blog/when-to-redesign-a-website/) covers when it is worth the disruption.

## What Speeds SEO Up, and What Does Not

Four habits shorten the wait: keeping key pages indexable, aiming first at low-competition searches, fixing technical faults early and linking your own pages to each other. None of the five pages I read publishes a controlled test, so "faster" below is expert opinion except where marked.

| Lever | Evidence type | Source | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keep key pages indexable | Practitioner opinion | Ahrefs, SEO.com | A page Google cannot index cannot rank, and a stray noindex tag is a common cause |
| Target specific, low-competition searches first | Practitioner opinion, three sources agree | Ahrefs, Orbit, SEO.com | Ahrefs compares a keyword difficulty of 97 with 37 for "seo vs sem", where it now ranks first |
| Fix technical faults first | Practitioner opinion | Search Engine Land | Early gains can arrive within weeks |
| Link your own pages to the ones that matter | Practitioner opinion | Ahrefs, Orbit | Internal links are fully in your control |
| Refresh outdated pages | Practitioner opinion | Ahrefs | A refresh can lift pages that have slipped |
| Start before a redesign or launch | Official guidance | Google Search Central | The site is built search-friendly from the start |

Several things do not speed it up. Google says advertising has no effect on organic results and that it never takes money to rank a site. It also says no one can guarantee a first-place ranking, and that "priority submit" offers and link-popularity schemes are useless. Orbit adds that bought links may give a temporary spike and then a fall at the next algorithm update.

## Date Arithmetic: When to Judge Your Own Campaign

If you start on 1 December 2026, the sourced ranges put your checkpoints between late December 2026 and December 2027. This is my arithmetic on the ranges above, not a prediction.

| Checkpoint | Date | Range used | What to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 weeks | 29 December 2026 | Technical fixes show in weeks (Search Engine Land) | Key pages indexed, errors down |
| 2 to 3 months | 1 February to 1 March 2027 | Low-competition rankings (Orbit) | Impressions rising |
| 3 to 6 months | 1 March to 1 June 2027 | Measurable results (Search Engine Land, Ahrefs, SEO.com) | Clicks and the first enquiries from search |
| 6 to 12 months | 1 June to 1 December 2027 | Business impact and payback (SEO.com) | Enquiries and revenue against cost |

Now add your season. Say your busiest enquiry period starts on 1 June 2027 (a made-up date). Six months back is 1 December 2026, so a start today reaches the six-month mark just as the season opens, while waiting until January means you arrive with about five months. Treat the first season as upside and plan for the second.

## What Usually Goes Wrong

Most SEO disappointments come from judging too early, changing too much or measuring the wrong thing, and each has a fix.

| What goes wrong | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Declared a failure at month 2 | The sources expect 3 to 6 months for first results | Agree checkpoints in advance, as in the table above |
| Strategy changed every few weeks | Squarespace warns that changing before an effect lands can delay success | Change one thing, then wait for the data |
| A broad term on a new site | SEO.com's example: a brand-new site chasing "hotels" | Start with specific searches that have business value |
| Pages blocked from Google | A noindex tag or robots.txt rule left from development | Check indexing in Search Console, as Google's site-move guide advises |
| Developer queue stalls the fixes | Search Engine Land says slow implementation slows results | Agree who ships SEO fixes, and how fast |
| Success measured in visits | SEO.com says the goal you set decides the timeline | Count calls, WhatsApp taps and form fills |

## Questions to Ask Any SEO About Timelines

The questions worth asking any SEO are the ones Google lists plus a few about visible work, and the answers should be in writing. Google also says to give an auditor read-only access to Search Console at first.

- [ ] What results do you expect, in what timeframe, and how do you measure them?
- [ ] Can I see similar work and speak to past clients?
- [ ] Do you follow Google Search Essentials, and will you share every change you make?
- [ ] What visible work happens in month one? Search Engine Land says a credible team shows a roadmap early.
- [ ] Which early signs will you report: fewer errors, rising impressions, new pages live, pages improved?
- [ ] Which enquiries will you count: calls, WhatsApp taps, forms?
- [ ] Do you guarantee a ranking? If yes, find someone else.

## FAQ

### How long does SEO take to work?

SEO usually takes three to six months to show measurable results and six to twelve months to move enquiries or revenue clearly, according to Search Engine Land and SEO.com. Technical fixes can show up sooner, and competitive searches take longer.

### How long does it take for SEO changes to take effect?

Technical changes can show early gains within weeks, per Search Engine Land, but rankings usually keep moving for longer. After a redesign or URL change, Google says medium-sized sites can take a few weeks or more to be fully reflected. Wait for the data before changing again.

### Why does SEO take so long?

SEO takes long because Google must crawl, assess and trust your pages while links and reputation build slowly and competitors keep working. SEO.com cites more than 200 ranking factors. Search Engine Land adds that your own team's speed in shipping fixes matters too.

### How long does SEO take for a new website?

A new website often takes four to twelve months to rank for competitive searches, per Orbit and Squarespace, and Ahrefs quotes Google's John Mueller on up to a year for new sites. Specific, low-competition searches can move in two to three months, according to Orbit. Setting up the Business Profile early helps a local business.

### How long does SEO take on Wix?

My sources give no Wix-specific figure, so I cannot give one. Squarespace's article, written for its own users, gives the same four to twelve months, and Google's guidance applies to any platform.

### Can paid ads or a payment to Google speed SEO up?

No. Google says advertising has no effect on its organic results and that it never accepts money to include or rank sites.
### How long before SEO pays for itself?

SEO.com says the break-even point typically comes at six to twelve months, because costs such as strategy, technical fixes and content come first. That is an agency's claim, not independent data.

### Should I stop SEO if nothing happens by month three?

Not necessarily, because the sources expect measurable results at three to six months. Check the early signs instead: fewer errors, rising impressions, new pages live and existing pages improved. If there is no visible work and no plan after a month or two, that is the warning sign, per Search Engine Land.
